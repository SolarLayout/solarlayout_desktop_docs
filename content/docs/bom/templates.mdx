title: Materials list templates
description: The four built-in reference materials lists, how to open one beside the computed list, and the action that replaces the computed list with it.
---

A template is a complete materials list for a reference plant, written out in
full and shipped with the application. You load one into a window of its own,
edit it, and — if you want to — put it in place of the list computed from your
layout.

Templates exist because the two lists know different things. The computed list
knows the plant: how many tables were placed, how many control rooms, how long
the cable runs are. A template knows the procurement detail a real materials list
needs and a layout cannot infer.

## Why a template is worth starting from

The computed list carries what the layout can measure. It cannot carry the rest,
because nothing in a boundary file or a set of layout parameters implies it:

- **Connector types** — what mates with what at each junction.
- **Cable specifications** — the detail behind a length: what the conductor is,
  how it is sized, how it is insulated and protected.
- **Earthing** — the earthing material a plant needs, none of which the layout
  places.
- **Conduit** — protection and routing material for the cable runs.

A template already has lines of that kind written, described and unitised.
Starting from one and correcting its quantities is faster and less error-prone
than typing a procurement-grade list into an empty table.

## The four built-in templates

Pick one from the **BOM Template** selector. Each name states the plant it was
written for: its DC and AC capacity, its inverter topology, and its mounting
type.

| Template | Capacity | Inverter topology | Mounting type |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **30MWp_20MW_Central_FT** | 30 MWp DC, 20 MW AC | Central Inverter | Fixed Tilt |
| **30MWp_20MW_String_FT** | 30 MWp DC, 20 MW AC | String Inverter | Fixed Tilt |
| **26.65MWp_20MW_Central Inverter_SAT** | 26.65 MWp DC, 20 MW AC | Central Inverter | Single Axis Tracker |
| **26.65MWp_20MW_String Inverter_SAT** | 26.65 MWp DC, 20 MW AC | String Inverter | Single Axis Tracker |

Read the name and you know what is inside. **FT** is fixed tilt, **SAT** is a
single-axis tracker, and the two capacities are the DC and AC ratings of the
reference plant the list was written for.

Three things follow from the naming, and all three matter when you choose:

- **The topology decides which line items exist at all.** A String Inverter list
  carries string inverters and the AC side that follows them; a Central Inverter
  list carries string monitoring boxes and DC trunks instead, because the central
  inverter sits inside the control room and there is no distributed AC leg. The
  two lists are not versions of each other.
- **The mounting type decides the structural lines.** Fixed-tilt racking and a
  single-axis tracker are different structures with different material behind
  them, so the structural part of the list does not carry over between the two.
- **The capacity only decides the quantities.** Those you correct against your
  own plant anyway, so a capacity that does not match your design is the least
  important of the three mismatches.

## Loading one

<Steps>
<Step>
### Switch **📋 BOM** on

The materials list mode has to be open, because a template is loaded beside the
computed list rather than instead of it. See
[Bill of materials](/docs/bom/overview).
</Step>

<Step>
### Choose a template from **BOM Template**

Pick the one whose topology and mounting type match your design.
</Step>

<Step>
### Click **📥 Load Template**

The template opens in a **separate window** of its own, next to the computed
list.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Screenshot id="bom-template-window" />

The template window is not modal. The main window stays live behind it, so you
can move between the two, scroll each independently, and read a computed quantity
straight across into the template. Arrange them side by side and the comparison
is the whole point of the design: nothing you do in the template window touches
the computed list until you explicitly ask for it.

## Editing the template

The template window holds the same five columns as the computed list — **S.No**,
**Material Description**, **Quantity**, **Unit**, **Remark** — and edits the same
way. Click a cell and type.

What you normally change:

- **Quantities**, corrected against the computed list. This is the main work.
  The template's figures belong to its reference plant, not to yours.
- **Descriptions and remarks**, where your specification differs from the
  template's — a different cable size, a different structure grade, a make your
  organisation is standardised on.
- **Line items**, added or removed where your scope differs. A plant without
  street lighting does not need those lines; anything in your scope that the
  reference plant did not have is a line you add.

Everything the computed list can tell you is one window away while you do it: the
table count, the control room count, the inverter or string monitoring box count,
the arrester count, the pile count, and the cable and trench lengths.

## Applying it to the plant

**Overlap into Plant BOM** is the consequential step on this page. Read this
section before you click it.

After a confirmation, **Overlap into Plant BOM** **replaces** the computed list
with the template's contents. The computed rows are gone — not merged, not kept
alongside, not appended to.

<Callout type="warn">
**Overlap into Plant BOM** replaces the computed materials list, and the computed
list is what prints in the report. After overlapping, the **Bill of Material**
pages of the exported PDF carry the template's rows and quantities — including
any quantity you have not yet corrected. Correct the template first, overlap
second, export third.
</Callout>

The full chain of consequence, so none of it is a surprise:

| After overlapping | State |
| --- | --- |
| The materials list on screen | The template's rows |
| **⬇ Export BOM to Excel** / **⬇ Export BOM to PDF** | Writes the template's rows |
| The report's **Bill of Material** pages | Carry the template's rows |
| The plant, the layout, the summary table | Unchanged — only the list is replaced |

## Getting the computed list back

**⚙ Rebuild from Layout** discards whatever is in the list and computes a fresh
one from the plant. That is how you undo an overlap.

It is a full recompute rather than an undo, so it takes your edits with it as
well as the template's rows. If there is anything in the current list you want to
keep, export it to a spreadsheet before you rebuild.

## Using a template in practice

The order below is the one that wastes the least work, because it settles the
structural choices before the arithmetic.

<Steps>
<Step>
### Finish the layout and rebuild the computed list

You need real counts to correct a template against, so generate the plant, turn
cable calculation on for the final run, and rebuild the computed list once. See
[Cable routing](/docs/layout/cables).
</Step>

<Step>
### Load the template that matches your topology and mounting type

Topology and mounting type first — those decide which line items exist. Capacity
is the least important match of the three.
</Step>

<Step>
### Correct the quantities against the computed list

Work down the template with both windows open, replacing its reference-plant
figures with your plant's counts and lengths.
</Step>

<Step>
### Add what your scope has and the template does not

Then remove the lines your scope does not include.
</Step>

<Step>
### Overlap into the plant list, then export

Only once the template reads correctly for your plant. Everything downstream —
the spreadsheet, the standalone PDF, the report's materials pages — follows from
this list. See [The PDF report](/docs/exports/pdf-report).
</Step>
</Steps>

A last check worth making before you circulate the result: the template is a
starting point, not a validated schedule of material for your plant. Nothing in
the application checks a template's line items against your design, so a line
the template carries and your plant does not need will sit there quietly until
somebody reads it.
