title: Spacing and tilt
description: Tilt from the site latitude, and row pitch shade-free across the Shadow Window on 21 December, unless you override them; plus what ground coverage ratio reports.
---

The **Spacing & Tilt** card holds two numbers — the tilt angle of the module
plane and the row pitch between rows of tables — and both are derived for you by
default. The card contains nothing but two override checkboxes and the two
value fields they unlock, because in the normal case you enter nothing here at
all.

It is the last card on the **Array** tab, and appears in a fixed-tilt design
only. A tracker has no fixed tilt and no derived pitch, so choosing a tracker at
launch removes this card; see [Tracker configuration](/docs/inputs/tracker).

<Screenshot id="spacing-tilt-group" maxWidth={420} />

## The fields

| Field | Default | Range | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Override tilt angle** | off — the line under it reads *Auto (latitude-based)* | — | Off, tilt is derived from the site latitude. On, enables the tilt field |
| **Tilt angle** | 20.0° | 0.0–90.0° | The angle of the module plane from horizontal, used when the override is on |
| **Override row pitch** | off — the line under it reads *Auto (no-shading, latitude-based)* | — | Off, pitch keeps rows shade-free across the Shadow Window on 21 December. On, enables the pitch field |
| **Row pitch** | 7.0 m | 1.0–50.0 m | The repeat distance from one row of tables to the next, used when the override is on |

Two definitions before going further. **Tilt** is the angle between the module
plane and the horizontal, measured toward the equator — south in the northern
hemisphere, north in the southern. **Pitch** is the row-to-row repeat distance
measured on the ground, perpendicular to the rows, and it is not the gap between
rows: it includes the ground the tables themselves stand on.

## The automatic behaviour

With both boxes unticked, which is how the card ships, tilt is derived from
where the site is and pitch is derived from that tilt.

### Tilt from latitude

The tilt angle follows a latitude rule of thumb:

```
tilt = 0.76 × |latitude| + 3.1°, clipped to 5°–40°
```

Latitude is the site latitude in degrees, taken from the geographic position of
the boundary file; a southern site uses its size, without the sign. The rule tracks
the well-established result that the best fixed tilt for annual yield sits somewhat
below the site latitude, and flattens further as you approach the equator.

A KMZ or KML boundary carries its position already. A CAD drawing or an image
does not necessarily, which is why those two inputs open the **Site reference**
window when you load them. Locate the drawing there — by its own UTM
coordinates or by the latitude and longitude of the site's centre — and the
derivation has what it needs.

<Callout type="warn">
Choose **Layout only** in that window and the site has no location. The
automatic tilt and pitch then use the latitude you typed in the window, if you
typed one. Without it **Generate Layout** stays disabled, with the links **Set tilt
and pitch** and **Enter a latitude** under it: give a latitude, or tick both
overrides here and set the tilt angle and row pitch by hand.
</Callout>

Energy yield is a separate matter: on a layout-only site **Calculate Energy**
is disabled outright, latitude or not, so no misleading generation figure is
produced. See [CAD drawing boundaries](/docs/inputs/cad-boundary).

### Pitch from the Shadow Window

The automatic pitch is the closest row spacing that keeps rows **shade-free across
the [Shadow Window](/docs/inputs/structures-and-shadow#the-shadow-window) on 21
December**, 09:00 to 15:00 solar time by default. For one solar hour:

```
pitch = L × cos(tilt) + L × sin(tilt) × cos(azimuth) ÷ tan(elevation)
```

Every term:

| Term | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `L` | The table height measured in the tilt plane — the length of the tilted face from its lower edge to its upper edge |
| `tilt` | The tilt angle, derived or overridden |
| `L × cos(tilt)` | The ground the tilted table itself covers: its size in the plant, north to south, which placement and the drawing use |
| `L × sin(tilt)` | The vertical height of the table's upper edge above its lower edge |
| `azimuth` | The sun's bearing that hour, measured from the equator: 0 at solar noon |
| `elevation` | The angle of the sun above the horizon that hour on 21 December, for the size of the site latitude. Never taken below 5° |
| `L × sin(tilt) × cos(azimuth) ÷ tan(elevation)` | The shadow that height casts towards the next row — the clear space that must be left behind each row |

The formula is worked at three hours: the window's start, its end and solar noon.
The largest result wins, rounded **up** to the millimetre. So the pitch is the
table's own ground size plus the longest shadow its top edge throws across the
window, usually at the window's edge.

For the default table, with `L` = 4.78 m:

| Latitude | Automatic tilt | Plan depth (m) | Noon only (m) | 09–15 pitch (m) | 09–16 pitch (m) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 12° | 12.22° | 4.672 | 5.393 | 5.633 | 6.012 |
| 20° | 18.30° | 4.538 | 5.961 | 6.427 | 7.240 |
| 28° | 24.38° | 4.354 | 6.830 | 7.708 | 9.479 |

Noon only is what a window of 12 to 12 gives. The window is on the Site tab, and a
wider one widens every row gap.

### What the shade-free condition buys

- **No row shades the next inside the window on 21 December.**
- **Not "no shading ever".** Outside the window the sun is low, and rows do shade
  one another. That residual loss is what the shading figure in the energy settings
  accounts for; it is not designed away by the pitch.

This is the standard, defensible starting point for a fixed-tilt plant, and it is
deliberately conservative. Sites where land is expensive routinely accept a
tighter pitch and a small shading loss in exchange for capacity, which is what
the override is for.

## Ground coverage ratio

Ground coverage ratio — GCR — is the collector area divided by the ground it is
spread over:

```
GCR = table height ÷ row pitch
```

The table height here is the same `L` as above, the tilted face length, not the
table's ground size, so GCR is a plain number and the visible gap between rows is
the pitch less the ground size. On one sample site, `L` of 4.78 m at 8.43° covers
4.728 m of ground, at a pitch of 5.287 m and a GCR of 0.9041. It is not a field: you never enter it. It is
reported from the geometry once tilt and pitch are settled, and it feeds two
calculations — the row-to-row shading loss, which is computed from GCR
automatically unless you switch that off, and the rear-side irradiance of a
bifacial module, which falls as GCR rises because dense rows leave less lit
ground to reflect.

What a higher GCR trades:

| Higher GCR means | Consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Rows packed closer together | More capacity on the same land, so a lower cost per watt of land, cabling and fencing |
| Longer shadows falling on the row behind | More inter-row shading loss, concentrated in the early morning and late afternoon and worst in winter |
| Less lit ground between rows | Less rear-side gain on a bifacial plant |

Because GCR follows from pitch, the way to move it is to move the pitch. Read the
resulting shading loss before accepting a tighter figure — see
[Shading losses](/docs/energy/shading).

## Overriding either value

Tick **Override tilt angle** or **Override row pitch** and the matching field
becomes editable; the derivation for that value stops being used. The two are
independent — you can override one, the other, both or neither.

Override tilt when the site is anything other than an unshaded flat field facing
the equator: a fixed structural standard across a portfolio, a slope that makes a
shallower angle sensible, a soiling regime that rewards a steeper one. Override
pitch when land cost, a road grid, an existing pile layout or a specified GCR
sets the spacing rather than a shading condition.

<Callout type="warn">
An overridden pitch is used exactly as entered. Nothing checks it against the
no-shading condition, so a tight figure produces a denser plant and a larger
shading loss without any warning — the loss appears in the energy result, not at
the input.
</Callout>

## After a run, the derived values are shown and pre-filled

Generate a layout with the overrides off and the card changes usefully. Each
**Auto** line reports the value that was actually used — for example
*Auto → 12.3° (latitude-based)* and *Auto → 7.42 m (no-shading,
latitude-based)* — and those same values are pre-filled into the greyed-out
override fields.

<Screenshot id="spacing-tilt-auto-resolved" maxWidth={420} />

This is the sequence worth using deliberately:

<Steps>
<Step>
### Generate once with both overrides off

Let the derivation supply tilt and pitch for the site as it stands.
</Step>

<Step>
### Read the two values the run used

They are on the labels, in the same units you would type.
</Step>

<Step>
### Tick the override you want to move

The field already holds the derived number, so you start from the real figure
rather than from a guess or from the shipped default. Ticking an override is
itself a change to the inputs, so the pinned button reads **Regenerate
Layout** — see [Out-of-date results](/docs/workspace/out-of-date-results).
</Step>

<Step>
### Adjust and generate again

Change the value by a known amount from a known baseline, and compare the two
runs.
</Step>
</Steps>

The Summary view reports both on its **Tilt (°)** and **Pitch (m)** rows. The
[Workings view](/docs/workspace/workings) says how each was reached: the rule, the
arithmetic, and whether your figure or a rule decided it.

## Tilt and pitch are coupled

Tilt appears twice in the pitch formula — once projecting the table onto the
ground and once setting the height of its upper edge — so **a change of tilt is
a change of automatic pitch**. A steeper array stands taller, throws a longer
shadow in the window, and needs more room behind it.

That makes the four combinations meaningfully different:

| Overrides | What you get |
| --- | --- |
| Neither | Tilt from latitude, pitch derived for that tilt. Internally consistent |
| Tilt only | Your tilt, and a pitch re-derived for it. Rows stay shade-free across the window |
| Pitch only | Your spacing, with tilt from latitude. Nothing keeps the rows shade-free across the window |
| Both | Both figures exactly as entered. Nothing is derived and nothing is checked |

Overriding one and leaving the other automatic is a deliberate choice, not a
half-finished one. Fixing the tilt and letting the pitch follow keeps the shading
condition; fixing the pitch and letting the tilt follow keeps the land use. Fix
both only when an external constraint gives you both numbers.
