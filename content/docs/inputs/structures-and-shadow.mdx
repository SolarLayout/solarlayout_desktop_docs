---
title: Structures and shadow
description: Footprints and heights for the control rooms, unit substations, objects, arresters and street lights — and the shadow they clear tables out of.
---

**Structures & Shadow** describes every building and pole on the site. The
numbers you enter here do two separate jobs.

First, they set the **physical footprint** of each structure — the rectangle it
occupies on the ground. That rectangle is what is drawn on the plot, what is
reserved out of the array, and what is carried into the exported drawings. Any
table overlapping it is removed.

Second, the **heights** of the buildings and objects drive a **year-round shadow
footprint**. The application sweeps the sun through a working window of the day,
across the whole year, and takes the ground each structure's shadow covers at any
point in that sweep. Tables inside that footprint are cleared rather than placed, so no part
of the array sits permanently in a building's shadow.

## The four structures

Every footprint is entered as **Length** east–west by **Width** north–south,
with a **Height** beside it. The layout uses the values in these fields, not
any fixed size, so a room you size differently here is placed and drawn at the
size you asked for.

| Structure | Length E–W (m) | Width N–S (m) | Height (m) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **ICR** | 10.0 | 4.0 | 5.0 |
| **MCR** | 15.0 | 8.0 | 5.0 |
| **USS** | 5.0 | 4.0 | 5.0 |
| **Object** | 0.0 | 0.0 | 0.0 |

### ICR — inverter control room

The building that houses the inverter and switchgear equipment for one block of
the array. One is placed automatically for each block of DC capacity, and the
block size is set by **ICR Block** in [Site parameters](/docs/inputs/site).

Control rooms are the structures that most often cost you tables, because there
are several of them and each sits in the middle of the array it serves rather
than off to one side. Every table overlapping a room's footprint is removed, and
the plant capacity is recomputed afterwards.

### MCR — main control room

The plant's central control building, and the point every medium-voltage
circuit terminates at. Unlike the inverter control rooms it is **not** placed
automatically — you place it yourself, once, by arming **Place MCR** and
clicking a position on the plot. A position outside the boundary is refused.

Its footprint is the largest of the three buildings by default, so place it
where the array is already interrupted — beside the entrance, next to the
switchyard, or on ground the terrain or an obstruction has already excluded —
rather than in the middle of a well-packed block.

Where you put it also sets the plant's medium-voltage cable length. Every
inverter control room's circuit runs back to this building, and the circuits are
routed as one shared network rather than as parallel runs, so nearby rooms merge
into a single trench. A position central to the control rooms costs materially
less cable than a corner does. The total is reported as soon as the building is
placed, and again in the summary — so placing it, reading the figure, moving it
and reading again is a quick way to settle the position. See
[Cable routing](/docs/layout/cables).

### USS — unit substation

A local step-up substation for a plot that does not contain the main control
room. See [Unit substations in a multi-plot file](#unit-substations-in-a-multi-plot-file)
below for when one appears.

### Object

A generic structure you place by hand — a store, a pump house, a security post,
a switchyard building, anything on the site the boundary file did not carry.
Arm **Place Object**, click the plot, and repeat for as many as you need;
**Remove Objects** clears them.

All three of its fields ship at **0**, which means *not configured*. Set the
length, width and height **before** placing objects, or you are placing a
structure with no footprint and no shadow.

Once sized, an object behaves like a building in every respect that matters
here: its footprint is reserved out of the array, tables overlapping it are
removed, and its height casts the same year-round shadow a control room does.
The group carries one set of object dimensions, so every object you place takes
that same size. On a site with a large store and a small security post, size for
the structure whose shadow you care about and draw the rest as annotations.

The heights of these four structures are used for shadow geometry. They are not
a structural design, and nothing here is checked against a load case.

## Unit substations in a multi-plot file

On a site made up of several separate boundaries, the medium-voltage collection
cannot all run to one building without crossing ground you do not own. The
application handles this the moment you place the main control room:

<Steps>
<Step>
### Place the main control room in one plot

Arm **Place MCR** and click a position inside one of the plots. That plot now
holds the plant's central building.
</Step>

<Step>
### A unit substation appears in every other plot

A unit substation of the size set above is dropped into **every plot except**
the one holding the main control room — one per plot, automatically.
</Step>

<Step>
### Each plot collects to its own substation

The inverter control rooms in a plot route their medium-voltage cables to that
plot's unit substation, not across the gap to the main control room. Each plot
becomes a self-contained collection network.
</Step>
</Steps>

The remaining link — from each unit substation onward to the main control room —
is an overhead line or a buried cable on a route only you can decide, across
land the file does not describe. It is **handled outside the automatic
medium-voltage routing**, so it is neither drawn nor included in the reported
medium-voltage cable length. Add it to your own single-line diagram and your own
schedule of quantities.

[Multi-plot sites](/docs/layout/multi-plot) covers how several boundaries are
laid out and summarised together.

## Arrester height and pile

Two fields describe the lightning arrester masts themselves. They appear here
because the mast is a structure; whether arresters are placed at all, and how
far apart, is set in the **Site Parameters** group above.

| Field | Default | Range | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **LA height** | 9.0 m | 0.0–100.0 m | Nothing. A design figure you record, and no more. |
| **LA pile Ø** | 0.3 m | 0.0–5.0 m | Nothing. A design figure you record, and no more. |

<Callout type="warn">
Neither of these two fields changes any output. The mast height casts no shadow
and clears no tables; the pile diameter reaches no quantity, no pile schedule and
no export. Neither value is kept when the project is saved, so both are back at
the figures above the next time the project is opened. The arrester mast and its
foundation belong in your own structural and civil documents.
</Callout>

The tables cleared around an arrester come from the arrester's own keep-clear
footprint, which is a fixed rectangle for the mounting type rather than anything
derived from the height — see
[Lightning arresters](/docs/layout/lightning-arresters).

## The shadow window

| Field | Default | Range | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Shadow Window** | 9.0 to 16.0 | 4–12 / 12–20 solar hours | The part of the day the year-round shadow footprint is swept over. |
| **Clear tables inside shadows** | on | — | Removes tables and tracker units that fall inside that footprint. |

The window is set in **solar hours**, so 12.0 is solar noon at the site rather
than noon on a clock. The first field is the start of the window and the second
is the end, and they are constrained so the window always spans solar noon.

The default window runs from 9.0 to 16.0 — the seven hours around solar noon,
the productive part of the day. Narrowing it shrinks the footprint, because the
early-morning and late-afternoon shadows, which are the long ones, are excluded
from the sweep. Widening it grows the footprint quickly, for the same reason.

Two things make the resulting footprint larger than the shadow you would see at
any single moment:

- It is **swept across the day**, so it fans from the western side of the
  structure round to the eastern side.
- It is **swept across the year**, so it takes the winter shadow length, not the
  summer one.

In the northern hemisphere the swept area therefore lies on the **northern**
side of the structure; in the southern hemisphere, the southern side. Placing
the main control room on the equator-facing edge of a block costs fewer tables
than placing it on the pole-facing edge.

## Clear tables inside shadows

On by default. It removes every table or tracker unit falling inside the
year-round shadow footprint of the inverter control rooms, the main control room
and any objects you placed.

That list is exactly three structures. **Arrester masts are not in it** — no
arrester shadow clears a table, at any height you enter — and street-light
shadows are handled by the street-light option rather than by this one.

The reasoning is straightforward. A module that spends the productive part of
every day in a building's shadow does not earn its place: it produces a fraction
of what its neighbours produce, it drags the string it belongs to down with it
through mismatch, and it still costs a full set of modules, piles and cable. It
is better removed than placed. The plant's capacity, module count and bill of
materials all report the smaller, honest number.

Turning it off places tables everywhere they geometrically fit, shadow or not.
That is useful when you want to see the maximum capacity a site can carry, or
when you have not yet decided where the buildings go. It is not the
configuration to issue a design from.

Row-to-row shading between the tables themselves is a different matter — it is
never a reason to remove a table, and it is handled as a loss in the energy
model. See [Shading losses](/docs/energy/shading).

## Street lights

Off by default. Ticking it places perimeter lighting poles once the layout is
generated.

| Field | Default | Range | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Street light pile Ø** | 0.3 m | 0.0–5.0 m | The diameter of each pole's foundation. |
| **Street light height** | 6.0 m | 0.0–50.0 m | The pole height above ground, and the height its shadow is cast from. |
| **Street light span** | 40.0 m | 1.0–500.0 m | The pole-to-pole spacing along the perimeter. |
| **Street light setback** | 0.2 m | 0.0–50.0 m | How far inside the perimeter the poles are inset. |
| **Street lights** | off | — | Whether poles are placed at all. |

With the option on, poles are placed **along the perimeter, spaced by the
span**, just inside the fence, and **any table their shadow touches is
cleared**.

The count follows from the span: it is approximately the plant perimeter divided
by the pole-to-pole span. Raising the span thins the poles out and lowers the
count; lowering it does the reverse. The figure appears in the summary's
`St.Lt` column.

The setback is small — a fraction of a metre by default — so with a perimeter
road of any usual width the poles land **inside the road band** rather than in
the array, and the ground they stand on was already kept clear. What can still
cost you tables is the shadow, which reaches inward from the fence line toward
the first row of the array.

[Site equipment](/docs/layout/site-equipment) covers the placed poles, the main
control room and the objects on the plot.
