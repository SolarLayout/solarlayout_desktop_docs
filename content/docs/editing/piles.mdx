title: Pile layout
description: Define the pile pattern of a full table, and of a half table — derived or your own — in the Pile layout window, then count, draw and export every pile.
---

The pile layout feature produces a construction document: the position of every
foundation pile in the plant, drawn and tabulated, in a form a setting-out team
can work from. You define the pattern once, on a full table — a full tracker in
a tracker design — and the application places it on every full table in the
plant. Half tables carry a pattern of their own, which the application derives
from the full one unless you draw it yourself.

It is a construction output, not a design check. Nothing here validates the
pile pattern against a structural design — the number of piles, where they sit
along the table and how big they are all come from you, or from the structure
supplier's drawing.

## Two patterns: full and half

A plant repeats one table design across the site, so the pattern only has to be
described once. There are two:

| Pattern | Goes on | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Full pattern** | Every full table or tracker | The piles you place |
| **Half pattern** | Every half table or half tracker | **Derived** from the full pattern, or **your own**, drawn on the half unit |

With no full pattern there are no piles anywhere, on full or half units. A
plant without half units only ever needs the full pattern, and the window then
shows no trace of the second one.

A half unit is halved along its long axis: a fixed-tilt half table is half the
**width** of a full table (east–west), and a half tracker is half the **length**
of a full tracker (north–south). See
[Maximizing placement and half tables](/docs/layout/maximize-and-half-tables).

### The coordinate frame

A pattern is drawn in the unit's own local frame, independent of the site — the
window's strip states it: *origin at the bottom-left corner · X east, Y north*.

- The origin, (0, 0), is the unit's **bottom-left corner** — its south-west
  corner on the ground. A half unit is measured from its own south-west corner.
- **X runs east**, across the unit's width.
- **Y runs north**, up the unit's height.

Both axes are in metres, and the extent is the unit's own dimensions, set by the
module size, the module counts and the gaps in **MMS-Table Configuration** (or
**Tracker Configuration**). See [Table configuration](/docs/inputs/table). A
coordinate outside the range 0 to the width, or 0 to the height, puts that pile
off the unit.

### The frame is only as big as the table

The extent of the frame is the table itself, so it changes whenever the table
changes. Modules per row and the east–west gaps set how far X can run; rows per
table and the north–south gaps set how far Y can run. Orientation changes both:
a portrait table puts each module's short side east–west and its long side
north–south, and a landscape table reverses that.

The consequence is practical. A pattern entered against a portrait table does
not transfer to a landscape one, and a pattern entered before you changed the
modules per row no longer sits where you meant it to. If you change the table
configuration, open the window again and check the pattern against the new
frame before exporting.

### What a pile is

Each pile is drawn as a circle of the **Pile radius**, centred on the (X, Y) you
entered. One radius applies to both patterns; it defaults to **0.15 m** and can
be set from 0.01 to 2 m.

The radius is a drawing and setting-out figure — the pile as it appears in plan.
The pile diameters for lightning arresters and street lights are separate
fields on the **Structures & Shadow** card and are not part of this pattern. See
[Structures and shadow](/docs/inputs/structures-and-shadow).

### How a pattern reaches the ground

Each unit keeps its own position, and its pattern is applied relative to that
unit's own south-west corner. So a pile's position on the ground is:

> the unit's south-west corner, **X** metres east and **Y** metres north.

That relation lets you check the export by hand. Take any table, take its
south-west corner, step east by the pattern's X and north by its Y, and that is
the coordinate the export gives for that pile. The coordinates are in metres:
UTM for a Google Earth boundary or a CAD drawing located by its own
coordinates, and the drawing's own coordinates for a CAD or image boundary
placed by hand or laid out without a location.

## Opening the Pile layout window

Two routes open the same window. **Piles: OFF** in the Layout view's tools is
disabled until a layout exists; **Edit-Pile ▸ Define Pile Layout…** on the menu
bar is the route to come back to the pattern later. On a narrow window the
**Piles** button folds into the **Layers ▾** popover as a row of its own — it
has moved, not gone.

<Steps>
<Step>
### Generate a layout first

There is nothing to apply a pattern to before that, so **Piles: OFF** stays
greyed out until a layout is on screen.
</Step>

<Step>
### Click Piles: OFF

The first time you use it, the **Pile layout** window opens so you can define
the pattern. Its strip names the unit — *One table* (*One tracker*) on a plant
without half units, *Full table* or *Half table* on a plant with them — and
gives its size, **w × h m**; a chip counts the piles placed so far.
</Step>

<Step>
### Place the piles on the unit

The right-hand side draws the unit to scale, with a red square at the origin:
**click inside the outline to place a pile**. On the left, **+ Add** adds a pile
at the centre of the unit for you to move by editing its X and Y, **Remove**
deletes the selected piles, **Clear** removes every pile, and the list —
**#**, **X (m)**, **Y (m)**, to three decimals — is the same pattern as numbers,
which you can edit directly. Set the **Pile radius** once for the whole design.
</Step>

<Step>
### Or let Auto grid… place an even pattern

**Auto grid…** asks for **Piles per row** (along X, east; 4 by default) and
**Rows of piles** (along Y, north; 2 by default), places them *evenly spaced,
inset 6% from the ends and 10% from the sides*, and **replaces the piles placed
so far**. Its button reads **Place n piles**. It works on the unit you are
editing, so on the Half tab the grid fits the half unit, and it remembers the
counts you last used.
</Step>

<Step>
### Apply to all tables

The footer shows the plant's total, *"N piles across the plant"*, and the
primary button reads **Apply to all N tables** (**trackers** in a tracker
design). It is disabled, with the reason *"Place at least one pile"*, until the
pattern has a pile in it. Applying switches the overlay on, the tools button
reads **Piles: ON**, and the status line reports the result.
</Step>
</Steps>

The status line after applying reads, on a tracker design with *tracker* for
*table*:

| Plant | Status line |
| --- | --- |
| No half units | *"Piles applied: n per table · N piles."* |
| With half units | *"Piles applied: n per full table, m per half table · N piles."* |

## Half tables: the Full / Half switch

On a plant with half units the window gains a switch under its strip, **Full
table · 1,234** | **Half table · 56** (with *tracker* on a tracker design) —
each side naming the unit and how many the plant has. The Full side edits the
full pattern, the Half side the half pattern. A plant without half units shows
no switch.

<Screenshot id="pile-editor-half-full" />

Under the switch, a status strip says where the half pattern stands. A plain
line is for information; a bordered strip marks a state worth knowing; a tinted
strip with an exclamation mark is a warning. The texts, with *tracker* for
*table* on a tracker design:

| Side | State | Text | Button |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Full | No full pattern yet | *"Half tables follow this pattern once you place piles."* | — |
| Full | Half pattern derived | *"Half tables follow this pattern: n piles each, derived with the same end overhang and even spans."* | — |
| Full | Your own half pattern | *"Half tables use your own pattern: n piles each."* | **Review** |
| Full | Your own half pattern, full pattern changed since (warning) | *"Half tables use your own pattern, made for an earlier full table."* | **Review** |
| Half | No full pattern yet | *"Place piles on the full table first. Half tables follow it."* | — |
| Half | Derived | *"Derived from the full table: same end overhang, even spans. Editing it makes it your own."* — a tracker adds *"drive pile at the centre"* | — |
| Half | Your own | *"Your own pattern for half tables."* | **Reset to derived** |
| Half | Your own, full pattern changed since (warning) | *"Your own pattern. The full table has changed since."* | **Reset to derived** |

**Review** switches to the Half side. **Reset to derived** drops your own half
pattern and goes back to the derived one. On the Half side the full unit is
drawn dashed behind the half, labelled *full table* (*full tracker*), so you can
see where the half sits against it.

### The derived half pattern

Until you edit it, the half pattern is derived from the full one by a fixed
rule:

1. The full pattern's piles are grouped into **frames** along the unit's long
   axis — piles within 0.3 m of each other form one frame.
2. The half unit **keeps the full unit's end overhangs**: its first frame sits
   where the full unit's first frame sits, and its last frame the same distance
   from its far end.
3. Between them it gets the **fewest frames for which no span is longer than the
   full unit's longest span**, evenly spaced.
4. On a **tracker**, one frame sits at the half tracker's **centre — the drive
   pile** — and each side of it is filled by the same rule.
5. Where there is too little room, the half unit gets a single frame at its
   centre.
6. Each frame takes its posts from the full unit's frame at the nearest
   relative position. Values are rounded to the millimetre.

Two examples show the rule at work. A fixed-tilt full table 20 m long, with
frames at x = 1, 7, 13 and 19 m and posts at y = 0.5 and 3.5 m — 8 piles — gives
its 10 m half table frames at x = 1, 5 and 9 m: **6 piles**. A tracker 60 m long
with piles at y = 2, 20, 40 and 58 m gives its 30 m half tracker piles at
y = 2, **15 (the drive pile)** and 28 m: **3 piles**.

### Your own half pattern

When the half unit needs something the rule does not produce — a supplier's
half-table drawing, say — draw it yourself on the Half side. **The first edit
there makes the half pattern your own**: a click in the outline, **+ Add**, a
change in the list, **Remove**, **Clear** or **Auto grid…** all count. From then
on the half pattern stays as you drew it when the full one changes.

<Screenshot id="pile-editor-half-own" />

<Callout type="warn">
**Clear** on the Half side makes your own half pattern an empty one, so every
half unit is left with no piles. To go back to the pattern derived from the full
table, use **Reset to derived** instead.
</Callout>

With no full pattern yet, the Half side's tools are disabled: place piles on
the full unit first.

### When the full pattern changes

Your own half pattern remembers the full pattern it was drawn against. Change
the full pattern afterwards and both sides show a warning — *"Half tables use
your own pattern, made for an earlier full table."* on the Full side — so a half
pattern drawn for an earlier table design is not carried forward unnoticed.
Click **Review** to look at it, then keep it or use **Reset to derived**.

## Checking the pattern against the ground

Once applied, the piles are drawn on every unit — each full unit with the full
pattern, each half unit with the half pattern — with coordinate labels for the
piles in view.

<Screenshot id="piles-overlay" />

<Screenshot id="view-header-piles-on" />

The **Piles: ON** overlay stays visible even with **Plant layout** switched off
in the **Layers ▾** popover. That combination — piles on, layout off — is the
one to use when you are checking the pattern, because nothing is drawn except
the piles themselves and their coordinates.

<Screenshot id="piles-layout-off" />

With the tables hidden you can see the pattern as a field of points and judge
it the way the setting-out team does: whether the rows read cleanly, whether
the spacing along the table is what the structure drawing says, whether
anything lands somewhere impossible. Look along a run of half units in
particular. **Satellite** and the **Contour / terrain** row have their own
switches, so you can leave either behind the piles.

## Patterns are part of the design

The full pattern, the half pattern and the radius are design inputs:

- They are **saved in the project** and come back when it is opened. The
  overlay's ON / OFF state is not saved.
- They **survive Generate Layout** and loading a new boundary file, and are
  applied to whatever tables the new layout has.
- Editing them does **not** make the layout out of date — piles are applied to
  the layout as it stands. See
  [Out-of-date results](/docs/workspace/out-of-date-results).

## What the Summary view reports

The **Piles** row of the Summary view is the plant's total pile count: each
unit counts its own piles, so it is the full units times the full pattern plus
the half units times the half pattern. The row is filled whenever a full
pattern exists, whether or not the overlay is on, and reads `—` until then.

Two things follow:

- **Change a pattern and the total changes.** Add a pile to the full pattern
  and the row rises by one per full table — and by whatever the derived half
  pattern gains on each half table.
- **Change the table count and the total changes.** Anything that adds or
  removes tables — an obstruction, an arrester footprint, a hand edit in
  [Sketch Mode](/docs/editing/sketch-mode) — moves the pile total with it.

The full row list is in [Summary rows](/docs/reference/summary-columns).

## Exporting the piles

Every output that counts or draws piles counts each unit's own piles, half
units with the half pattern.

| Output | When it carries piles | What it carries |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Export to Excel…**, in the Pile layout window | A full pattern exists | Every pile's coordinates, on a sheet **Pile Coordinates** — from the patterns in the window, applied or not |
| **Export ▾ Export PDF (with Piles)** | The item is on the menu only while **Piles** is on, a pattern exists and a layout exists | The PDF report, with each unit's piles drawn and labelled on the first page and a **Piles** column on the summary page |
| **Export ▾ Export DXF** | **Piles** is on **and** a pattern exists | A `PILES` layer: a circle of the pile radius per pile, with its easting and northing |
| An ICR block's summary window, and **Export ICR-Block PDF** | A pattern exists | A **Piles** row per block |
| **Export ICR-Block DXF** | **Piles** is on **and** a pattern exists | A **Piles** row per block, and the `PILES` layer |
| The Google Earth file, the Word project report | Never | — |

A pattern alone is not enough for the CAD drawings: switch **Piles** on before
you export them if they are to carry the piles. See
[CAD export](/docs/exports/dxf) and [ICR blocks](/docs/layout/icr-blocks).

<Screenshot id="export-menu-piles" />

### The Excel workbook

**Export to Excel…** opens a save dialog titled **Export Pile Coordinates**,
with the name `pile_coordinates.xlsx` suggested, and reports *"Exported n pile
coordinates → …"* when it is done. Its columns, in order:

| Column | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| **Pile ID** | The pile's reference |
| **Plant** | The plant the pile is in |
| **Table No.** | The table or tracker it belongs to |
| **Unit** | `Full` or `Half` |
| **X local (m)**, **Y local (m)** | The pile's offset in the pattern |
| **Easting (m)**, **Northing (m)** | The pile's position on the ground — headed **X drawing (m)** and **Y drawing (m)** for a site placed by hand or laid out without a location |
| **Longitude**, **Latitude** | The same position in degrees; blank when the site's location is unknown |
| **Pile Ø (m)** | The pile diameter, twice the radius |

It needs active access, and it is refused while the layout is out of date.

**Export PDF (with Piles)** is the document to hand a survey or setting-out
team. Its first page is drawn at **300 dpi**, because a pile drawing is printed
large and zoomed into on site, and its other pages are the design summary, the
single-line diagram, the bill of materials and, when energy was calculated,
the energy pages. See [The project report](/docs/exports/project-report).

## Limits

- **Nothing is checked against a structural design.** Pile count, position and
  radius are inputs. The application draws and counts what you enter; it does
  not test whether the foundation works.
- **Nothing is checked against the ground.** The pattern is geometric, so it is
  applied identically on level and sloping ground. Where relief within a single
  table footprint matters, that is what **Max height var / table** in
  **Topography** is for — it governs which ground is used at all. See
  [Topography](/docs/inputs/topography).
- **A pattern cannot vary by location.** Every full unit gets the full pattern
  and every half unit the half pattern, so there is no way to treat the tables
  in one corner of the site differently.
- **A later Generate keeps piles uniform on purpose.** After Sketch Mode edits,
  Generate re-plots the aligned grid with **Maximize placement** switched off,
  precisely so that pile coordinates stay regular for construction. See
  [Sketch Mode](/docs/editing/sketch-mode#what-generate-layout-does-to-your-edits).
