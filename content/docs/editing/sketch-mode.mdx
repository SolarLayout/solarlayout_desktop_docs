---
title: Sketch Mode
description: The hand-editing surface — select, move, copy and delete, move buildings, place tables and arresters, draw roads, corridors and cable trenches, and recompute the plant totals.
---

The generated layout is a starting point. It knows what the boundary file told
it: where the fence runs, where the water is, where a transmission line
crosses. It does not know that the north-west corner is a spoil heap, that the
owner wants two tables left out beside the access gate, or that the trench to
the second control room has to follow an existing culvert. Sketch Mode is where
you put that knowledge into the design.

Everything you place by hand is part of the plant. A table you add counts
towards the module count and the capacity; a road you draw moves the rows apart
to make room. The drawing tools — lines, shapes, text, dimensions, measurements
— are covered separately in
[Annotations, measuring and layers](/docs/editing/annotations), because they
change the drawing rather than the plant.

## Turning Sketch Mode on and off

**Sketch** sits in the Layout view's tools, to the right of the view tabs, and
starts off. Sketch is a tool of the Layout view: it is available only there, and
switching to another view switches Sketch off. See
[Views](/docs/workspace/views) for the tools row.

Click **Sketch** and the left column changes. The pinned **Generate Layout**
button gives way to an orange **● Sketch Mode** banner with the table count on
its right and one button, **Exit Sketch Mode — apply edits, recalc totals**;
the input tabs are replaced by the tool palette; and an orange contextual bar
appears above the plot. The status line reads *"Sketch Mode ON — select a tool
from the left panel."*

<Screenshot id="sketch-mode" />

Click **Exit Sketch Mode — apply edits, recalc totals** and the inputs come
back. What happens next depends on what changed:

| What changed | What happens | Status line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nothing (a line colour alone does not count) | Nothing is recomputed, redrawn or re-routed | *"Sketch Mode OFF."* |
| Only trenches | No re-route. Trench totals are recomputed, and a deleted automatic trench stays deleted | *"Sketch changes applied — trench totals updated."* |
| The layout, with **Calculate cables** on | The plant totals are recomputed, the inverters or SMBs placed again and every cable routed again | *"Sketch changes applied — recalculating inverter placement and cables…"*, then a finish line starting *"Sketch changes applied"* |
| The layout, with **Calculate cables** off | The plant totals are recomputed and the inverters or SMBs placed again | *"Sketch changes applied — placing inverters/SMBs on revised layout…"*, then the same finish line |

<Callout type="warn">
The plant totals are recomputed when you **leave** Sketch Mode, not while you
are editing. Module count and capacity in the Summary view and the plant chips
can stay at their pre-edit figures until you exit, so exit before reading the
Summary, exporting, or calculating energy.
</Callout>

Under the palette a hint summarises the keyboard: *"Shift+Click = multi ·

### Moving around while you edit

In Sketch Mode a plain drag belongs to the tool, so the view has its own
gestures, and they work under any tool:

| Gesture | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Middle button drag | Pans |
| **Space** + drag | Pans |
| Mouse wheel | Zooms at the cursor |
| **F** | Fits the whole plant |

The **Home**, **Pan** and **Zoom** icons in the Layout tools are there too. While
**Pan** or **Zoom** is on, a drag on the plot moves the view and the sketch tool
ignores it, so click the icon off before you draw.

## The contextual bar

The orange strip above the plot holds the drawing aids and the numeric entry.
It is present only in Sketch Mode.

<Screenshot id="sketch-context-bar" />

| Control | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **🧲 Snap** | on | What you click snaps to nearby geometry — corners, edge midpoints, centres — so a trench meets a control room exactly rather than nearly |
| **⊾ Ortho** | off | What you draw is held horizontal or vertical, so a trench cannot pick up an accidental few degrees |
| **▦ Grid** + spacing | off, 10.0 m | Draws a reference grid at the spacing you set (0.5–1000 m) |
| **🎨 Colour** | orange-red | The stroke colour for what you draw next |
| **Layer**, **＋**, **👁** | layer *0* | The active annotation layer, a new layer, and show or hide the active layer |
| Numeric entry | — | *"len\<bearing or dx,dy → Enter"*: a typed length and direction for the point you are about to place |
| **E: — N: —** | — | The live cursor position in metres |
| **↶ Undo** · **↷ Redo** | — | Step back and forward through your edits |

**🧲 Snap** and **⊾ Ortho** are the two to have on before drawing anything you
intend to build from. A trench drawn free-hand a degree or two off square looks
right on the screen and is a drawing nobody can set out from.

The numeric entry takes over from the mouse for the second point of anything:
click a start point, then type `25.4<30` for 25.4 m at a bearing of 30°,
`10,5` for 10 m east and 5 m north, or a bare `25.4` for that distance along
the cursor direction — or a radius — and press Enter.

## Selecting

**↖ Move** is the tool you spend most of your time in. It selects, it drags,
and it is the tool that repositions anything you have already placed.

| Action | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Click an object | Selects it |
| **Shift**+click another object | Adds it to the selection |
| Drag on empty space **to the right** | A **window** rubber band: selects what lies entirely inside the box |
| Drag on empty space **to the left** | A **crossing** rubber band: selects everything the box touches |
| Drag any selected object | Moves the whole selection together |
| **Ctrl**+arrow key | Nudges the selection 1 m in that direction |
| **Ctrl+C** / **Ctrl+V** | Copies and pastes the selection |
| **Ctrl+Z** / **Ctrl+Y** | Undo and redo |
| **Esc** | Cancels the tool or clears what it was doing |

<Screenshot id="sketch-selection" />

The two rubber bands are the CAD convention and worth learning: a window
selection is for picking a clean block of tables without catching the row
beside it; a crossing selection is for grabbing every trench that passes
through an area. The 1 m nudge is how you work at table scale: pick up a
table with the mouse, drop it roughly, then step it into position without
touching the mouse again.

The **MODIFY SELECTION** group — rotate, scale, mirror, offset, array, group,
ungroup — is greyed out until there is a selection it can act on. Those tools
are described with the drawing tools on
[Annotations, measuring and layers](/docs/editing/annotations).

## Moving a building

Buildings move in Sketch Mode only. Outside it the Layout view is read-only, and
a drag on a building pans the view.

Press on an ICR, the MCR, a USS, a handover point or a placed object, under any
palette tool, drag it and release. The press belongs to the view instead while
**Space** is held or a toolbar tool is on, and it is ignored while cables are
being routed.

| Building | Where it can drop |
| --- | --- |
| **ICR** | Fully inside the usable area, or it snaps back to where it started. The tables rebuild around the new position: those under the footprint are cleared and those it vacated are available again. The ICR count does not change |
| **MCR**, **USS**, **handover point**, **object** | Anywhere |

See [Inverter control rooms](/docs/layout/icr) and
[Main control room, substations and street lights](/docs/layout/site-equipment).

## Moving, copying and deleting

| Tool | What you do | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **↖ Move** | Select, then drag | The selection moves together |
| **⎘ Copy** | Click a table | A copy is placed 5 m east and 5 m north of it |
| **🗑 Delete** | Click any object | The object is removed |

To copy several things at once, select them with **↖ Move**, then **Ctrl+C**
and **Ctrl+V**, which pastes 5 m east and 5 m north. The offset means a copy
lands clear of the original, so you can see it and drag it where it belongs.

**🗑 Delete** removes on a single click, with no confirmation. **↶ Undo** is the
way back.

## Placing plant objects

Each of these adds a real object to the plant, not a drawing of one.

| Tool | What you do | Effect on the layout |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **➕ Table** | Click a position | Places one full MMS table, at the size and orientation set in the table configuration |
| **➕ Half Table** | Click a position | Places a half unit. The button is greyed out, with the tooltip *"A half table here would hold only half a string."*, when the design has no half unit |
| **➕ LA** | Click a position | Places a lightning arrester at that spot and removes the tables under its pile and shadow |
| **🚧 Road** | Drag a straight centre-line, then enter a width | Places the road and moves the rows apart to make room, as below |
| **⚡ T-Line** | Drag a straight centre-line, then enter a width | Draws the red centre-line, records the corridor as a keep-out and removes the tables inside it |

**Tables you place are ordinary tables.** They take their dimensions from
**MMS-Table Configuration**, so a hand-placed table is the same object the
automatic pass places. A half unit holds whole strings: half the full unit's,
rounded down. See [Table configuration](/docs/inputs/table).

**The arrester grid is on the Site tab.** Set the arresters with **Place Lightning
Arresters** and its fields rather than by hand. See
[Lightning arresters](/docs/layout/lightning-arresters).

**Roads and transmission lines are drawn as a centre-line with a width.** Drag
the line, release, and a prompt asks for the width — **Road width**, default
**5.0 m**, for a road; **Transmission line corridor**, default **10.0 m**, for a
line. The width is the whole corridor: the centre-line is buffered by half of
it on each side, so the strip is the width you gave, not twice it.

**Line corridor (each side)** on the Site tab has no part in this. That field
applies to the lines read from a boundary file, and it is a setback each side
rather than a total width. To clear the same ground as a 15 m setback on a line
you draw here, enter a corridor width of 30 m.

A polygon obstruction, a rectangle from the **Tools** tab, and the main control
room and objects are placed from the Tools tab rather than from the palette. See
[Obstructions](/docs/layout/obstructions) and
[Site equipment](/docs/layout/site-equipment).

## Roads move rows

A **🚧 Road** stays where you draw it, and the plant makes room around it. What
the plant does depends on the road's angle to the rows.

<Screenshot id="road-before" caption="Before the road" />

<Screenshot id="road-moves-rows" caption="After the road: the rows on each side moved apart" />

| The road runs | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Within the limit of **along** the rows | The rows move: north and south on fixed tilt, east and west on trackers. Each side moves just enough to clear the road plus the clearance, and the rows beyond follow, so every pitch is kept, until the plot's edge or an earlier road. Only the tables beside the road move |
| Within the limit of **across** the rows | Each row opens sideways to let the road through |
| Beyond the limit | The tables under the corridor are removed |

A table pushed off the land or into a keep-out is dropped. Rows that land on wider
ground gain full tables, except on a plant capped to an AC target. Moved tables
cannot land on ICRs, the MCR, USSs, objects, arrester piles or the buildings'
shadow.

Two settings under **Advanced settings ▸ Layout ▸ Sketch roads** govern this. See
[Advanced settings](/docs/workspace/advanced-settings).

| Field | Default | Range | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Road angle that moves rows** | 10 ° | 0–45 | A road this close to along or across the rows moves them |
| **Road to MMS clearance** | 0 m | 0–10 | Clear ground kept between a road's edge and the MMS |

The status line reports what the road did:

- *"Road placed: rows moved 4.6 m south; 3 tables dropped, 0 added."*
- *"Road placed: it fits the gap, no rows moved."*
- A road at a slant: *"Road at 27° to the rows: the n tables under it were removed.
  Rows move only for roads within 10° of along or across the rows (Advanced settings
  ▸ Layout)."*

**⚡ T-Line**, and **╱ Line** or **⌒ Polyline** with a width above 0, remove the
tables under them and never move rows.

## Cable trenches

Four tools deal with trenches. They are the reason to open Sketch Mode on a
plant that is already routed: the automatic route is geometrically sound, but it
knows nothing about what is already buried on the site.

| Tool | What you do |
| --- | --- |
| **🟢 DC Trench** | Click vertices, right-click to finish: a DC trench |
| **🔴 AC Trench** | Click vertices, right-click to finish: an AC trench |
| **🟩 MV Trench** | Click vertices, right-click to finish: a medium-voltage trench |
| **✂ Del Trench** | Click any trench — automatic or hand-drawn — to delete it |

A trench you draw **replaces every automatic run of that type** in the plot holding
its middle, and the cable and trench totals then come from what you drew. In a
central design **🔴 AC Trench** draws the trunk from the SMBs to the central inverter.

**Drawn trenches last.** A drawn trench survives leaving Sketch Mode and a **Generate
Layout**; the status line reports it, such as *"Kept your drawn MV trench on Plot 1:
it replaces the automatic MV routes there."*

**A deleted automatic trench stays deleted** on leaving Sketch Mode when only trenches
changed, because nothing re-routes. A re-route would restore it, so with **Calculate
cables** on, the application asks first.

### The Re-route cables question

Any step that would re-route cables and restore trenches you deleted opens the
**Re-route cables** box: *"Re-routing restores the automatic trenches you deleted. The
trenches you drew are kept."*

| Button | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **&lt;Action&gt; and restore trenches** | Goes ahead, naming the step: *Generate*, *Leave Sketch*, *Add obstruction*, *Remove obstruction*, *Clear obstructions*, *Move ICR*, *Move MCR*, *Move USS* or *Move handover point* |
| **Cancel** | The default. Nothing happens, and your deleted trenches stay deleted. Cancelling **Leave Sketch** keeps you in Sketch Mode on the Layout view |

A move of the MCR, a USS or a handover point asks only about the medium-voltage
trenches on that plot. With **Calculate cables** off nothing asks.

After every cable run, including the re-route of a drawn obstruction, drawn trenches
are applied again and the trench totals recomputed. What that means for the reported
lengths, and why trench totals are shorter than conductor totals, is on
[Cable routing](/docs/layout/cables).

## An edit from start to finish

<Steps>
<Step>
### Switch Sketch Mode on

Click **Sketch** in the Layout view's tools. The input tabs are replaced by the
tool palette and the contextual bar appears above the plot.
</Step>

<Step>
### Zoom to the ground you are changing

Roll the mouse wheel over the area, and pan with the middle button or **Space** and
a drag. **F** or **Home** returns to the whole plant whenever you lose your place.
</Step>

<Step>
### Switch on the aids the edit needs

**🧲 Snap** for anything that has to meet existing geometry exactly.
**⊾ Ortho** for anything that has to run square. **▦ Grid** if you want a
reference to judge spacing against.
</Step>

<Step>
### Make the edit

Select with **↖ Move** and drag, nudge with **Ctrl** and the arrow keys, place
with **➕ Table**, make room with **🚧 Road**, reroute with a trench tool.
**↶ Undo** and **↷ Redo** step through anything that went wrong; **Esc** cancels a
half-finished action.
</Step>

<Step>
### Exit Sketch Mode

Click **Exit Sketch Mode — apply edits, recalc totals**. Read the module count and
capacity in the Summary view and confirm they moved the way you expected.
</Step>

<Step>
### Save the project

**Save project** in the view header writes the whole session to a `.slp` file,
sketch edits included, so the edited plant reopens as you left it. See
[Projects](/docs/projects).
</Step>
</Steps>

## What your edits mean for the numbers

Sketch Mode is not a drawing overlay. The objects you place are counted, and
the counting happens when you leave.

| Summary row | What your edits do to it |
| --- | --- |
| **Full tables** | Rises and falls with full tables placed and deleted |
| **Half tables** | Rises and falls with half units placed and deleted |
| **Modules** | Recomputed from the tables actually present |
| **DC capacity (MWp)** | Recomputed from the module count and the module wattage |

The full row list is in [Summary rows](/docs/reference/summary-columns); the
plant chips under the view follow the same figures.

Because the totals are recomputed rather than adjusted, the figure you read
after leaving Sketch Mode is the figure the whole application then works from.
The [deliverables package](/docs/exports/deliverables) is written from the current
layout, so it carries your edited plant, not the automatic one. Hand edits do not
mark results out of date; see
[Out-of-date results](/docs/workspace/out-of-date-results).

Two consequences worth planning around:

- **Calculate energy after editing, not before.** Year-one and lifetime
  generation are computed from the plant capacity. Edit the layout, exit
  Sketch Mode so the capacity settles, then calculate. See
  [How yield is calculated](/docs/energy/overview).
- **The bill of materials follows.** It is counted from the layout, so your edits
  reach it when it is next opened, and edits made in the BOM itself are kept. See
  [Bill of materials](/docs/bom/overview).

## What Generate Layout does to your edits

Pressing **Generate Layout** after editing does a precise thing:

1. If you are still in Sketch Mode, Generate leaves it first, so the edits are
   committed rather than lost.
2. If the layout was edited, Generate **re-places every table, tracker,
   inverter and control room from scratch** into the available space.
   Hand-placed and hand-deleted **tables do not survive** a Generate.
3. The obstructions, corridors, Sketch roads, main control room, USS, handover
   points, objects, annotations and drawn trenches you placed **do** survive. They
   are carried onto the fresh layout, and the obstructions and corridors are handed
   to the placement as ground to keep clear, so the plant is rebuilt *around* your
   constraints.
4. Automatic trenches you deleted come back, after the **Re-route cables** question.
5. An energy result is cleared; calculate energy again for the fresh layout.

<Callout type="warn">
The re-plot is forced back to the aligned grid: **Maximize placement** is
switched off for it, so that pile coordinates stay uniform for construction. If
you had it on, the plant you get back is the aligned one. The status line says *"Re-plotting the aligned grid around your sketch
edits…"* while this happens.
</Callout>

There is no "keep my layout and refresh the numbers" path. So the order of work
matters: draw your roads and corridors and place the control room first,
generate, and hand-edit individual tables **last** — anything you do to tables
before a Generate is discarded by it.
