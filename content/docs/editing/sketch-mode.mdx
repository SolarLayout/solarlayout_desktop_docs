---
title: Sketch Mode
description: The hand-editing surface — select, move, copy, delete, place tables, arresters, roads, corridors and cable trenches on a generated layout, and recompute the plant totals from your edits.
---

The generated layout is a starting point. It knows what the boundary file told
it: where the fence runs, where the water is, where a transmission line
crosses. It does not know that the north-west corner is a spoil heap, that the
owner wants two tables left out beside the access gate, or that the trench to
the second control room has to follow an existing culvert. Sketch Mode is where
you put that knowledge into the design.

Everything you place by hand is part of the plant. A table you add counts
towards the module count and the capacity; an arrester you add counts in the
arrester row; a road you draw clears the tables under it. The drawing tools —
lines, shapes, text, dimensions, measurements — are covered separately in
[Annotations, measuring and layers](/docs/editing/annotations), because they
change the drawing rather than the plant.

## Turning Sketch Mode on and off

**Sketch** sits in the Layout view's tools, to the right of the view tabs, and
starts off. Sketch is a tool of the Layout view: it is available only there,
and switching to another view — Summary, Energy, BOM or SLD — switches Sketch
off. See [Views](/docs/workspace/views) for the tools row.

Click **Sketch** and the left column changes. The pinned **Generate Layout**
button gives way to an orange **● Sketch Mode** banner with the table count on
its right and one button, **Exit Sketch Mode — apply edits, recalc totals**;
the input tabs are replaced by the tool palette; and an orange contextual bar
appears above the plot. The status line reads *"Sketch Mode ON — select a tool
from the left panel."*

<Screenshot id="sketch-mode" />

Click **Exit Sketch Mode — apply edits, recalc totals** and the inputs come
back. What happens next depends on whether the plot changed:

- **If nothing changed** — a line colour alone does not count — nothing is
  recomputed, redrawn or re-routed. The status line reads *"Sketch Mode OFF."*
- **If something changed**, the plant totals are recomputed, the inverters
  (or SMBs) are placed again and, with **Calculate cables** on, every cable is
  routed again. The status line reads *"Sketch Mode OFF — plant totals
  updated."*, then the *"Layout ready …"* line once the cables are done.

<Callout type="warn">
The plant totals are recomputed when you **leave** Sketch Mode, not while you
are editing. Module count, capacity and arrester count in the Summary view and
the plant chips stay at their pre-edit figures until you exit, so exit before
reading the Summary, exporting, or calculating energy.
</Callout>

On a tracker plant the plant objects read **➕ Tracker** and **➕ Half Tracker**

<Screenshot id="sketch-palette-tracker" />

Under the palette a hint summarises the keyboard: *"Shift+Click = multi ·
Ctrl+Arrow = nudge 1 m · Ctrl+C/V = copy/paste · Ctrl+Z/Y = undo/redo · Esc =
cancel. Rubber-band: drag →right = window, ←left = crossing. Click a start
point, then type len\<bearing / dx,dy ↵ in the bar above the plot."*

There are **no Pan or Zoom buttons in the palette**. The mouse wheel zooms at
the cursor, and the **Home**, **Pan** and **Zoom** icons in the Layout tools do
the rest — but while **Pan** or **Zoom** is active a drag on the plot moves the
view and the sketch tool ignores it, so click the navigation icon off before you
draw.

## The contextual bar

The orange strip above the plot holds the drawing aids and the numeric entry.
It is present only in Sketch Mode.

<Screenshot id="sketch-context-bar" />

| Control | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **🧲 Snap** | on | What you click snaps to nearby geometry — corners, edge midpoints, centres — so a trench meets a control room exactly rather than nearly |
| **⊾ Ortho** | off | What you draw is held horizontal or vertical, so a trench cannot pick up an accidental few degrees |
| **▦ Grid** + spacing | off, 10.0 m | Draws a reference grid at the spacing you set (0.5–1000 m) |
| **🎨 Colour** | orange-red | The stroke colour for what you draw next |
| **Layer**, **＋**, **👁** | layer *0* | The active annotation layer, a new layer, and show or hide the active layer |
| Numeric entry | — | *"len\<bearing or dx,dy → Enter"*: a typed length and direction for the point you are about to place |
| **E: — N: —** | — | The live cursor position in metres |
| **↶ Undo** · **↷ Redo** | — | Step back and forward through your edits |

**🧲 Snap** and **⊾ Ortho** are the two to have on before drawing anything you
intend to build from. A trench drawn free-hand a degree or two off square looks
right on the screen and exports as a drawing nobody can set out from.

The numeric entry takes over from the mouse for the second point of anything:
click a start point, then type `25.4<30` for 25.4 m at a bearing of 30°,
`10,5` for 10 m east and 5 m north, or a bare `25.4` for that distance along
the cursor direction — or a radius — and press Enter.

## Selecting

**↖ Move** is the tool you spend most of your time in. It selects, it drags,
and it is the tool that repositions anything you have already placed.

| Action | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Click an object | Selects it |
| **Shift**+click another object | Adds it to the selection |
| Drag on empty space **to the right** | A **window** rubber band: selects what lies entirely inside the box |
| Drag on empty space **to the left** | A **crossing** rubber band: selects everything the box touches |
| Drag any selected object | Moves the whole selection together |
| **Ctrl**+arrow key | Nudges the selection 1 m in that direction |
| **Ctrl+C** / **Ctrl+V** | Copies and pastes the selection |
| **Ctrl+Z** / **Ctrl+Y** | Undo and redo |
| **Esc** | Cancels the tool or clears what it was doing |

<Screenshot id="sketch-selection" />

The two rubber bands are the CAD convention and worth learning: a window
selection is for picking a clean block of tables without catching the row
beside it; a crossing selection is for grabbing every trench that passes
through an area. The 1 m nudge is how you work at table scale: pick up a
table with the mouse, drop it roughly, then step it into position without
touching the mouse again.

The **MODIFY SELECTION** group — rotate, scale, mirror, offset, array, group,
ungroup — is greyed out until there is a selection it can act on. Those tools
are described with the drawing tools on
[Annotations, measuring and layers](/docs/editing/annotations).

### Moving a control room

A control room is not moved with **↖ Move**, and does not need Sketch Mode at
all. With neither **Pan** nor **Zoom** active, click and hold a blue ICR on the
plot, drag it, and release. The layout rebuilds around the new position — tables
under the new footprint are cleared, and the tables it vacated are available
again. A drop where the room does not fit entirely inside the usable area snaps
back to where it started. A drag is ignored while cables are being routed. See
[Inverter control rooms](/docs/layout/icr).

## Moving, copying and deleting

| Tool | What you do | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **↖ Move** | Select, then drag | The selection moves together |
| **⎘ Copy** | Click a table | A copy is placed 5 m east and 5 m north of it |
| **🗑 Delete** | Click any object | The object is removed |

To copy several things at once, select them with **↖ Move**, then **Ctrl+C**
and **Ctrl+V**. The offset means a copy lands clear of the original, so you can
see it and drag it where it belongs instead of it sitting invisibly on top of
what you copied.

**🗑 Delete** removes on a single click, with no confirmation. **↶ Undo** is the
way back.

## Placing plant objects

Each of these adds a real object to the plant, not a drawing of one.

| Tool | What you do | Effect on the layout |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **➕ Table** | Click a position | Places one full MMS table, at the size and orientation set in the table configuration |
| **➕ Half Table** | Click a position | Places a half unit — half the east–west modules and capacity; half the north–south strings on a tracker |
| **➕ LA** | Click a position | Places a lightning arrester and clears the tables under its footprint |
| **🚧 Road** | Drag a straight centre-line, then enter a width | Places a road corridor of that width and removes the tables under it |
| **⚡ T-Line** | Drag a straight centre-line, then enter a width | Draws the red centre-line, records the corridor as a keep-out and removes the tables inside it |

A few things follow from that table.

**Tables you place are ordinary tables.** They take their dimensions from
**MMS-Table Configuration**, so a hand-placed table is the same object the
automatic pass places — see [Table configuration](/docs/inputs/table). A half
unit is half the east–west width and carries half the strings, and counts as
half a table wherever tables are counted.

**Arresters can be added even when the automatic pass placed none.** With
**Place Lightning Arresters** left off on the Site tab no arresters are placed
and tables fill the whole usable area; **➕ LA** is how you add the few you
actually want. The footprint that gets cleared is the arrester footprint for
your mounting type, which is much larger on a fixed-tilt plant than on a
tracker plant. See [Lightning arresters](/docs/layout/lightning-arresters).

**Roads and transmission lines are drawn as a centre-line with a width.** Drag
the line, release, and a prompt asks for the width — **Road width**, default
**5.0 m**, for a road; **Transmission line corridor**, default **10.0 m**, for a
line. The width is the whole corridor: the centre-line is buffered by half of
it on each side, so the strip cleared of tables is the width you gave, not
twice it.

**Transmission line corridor** on the Site tab has no part in this. That field
applies to the lines read from a boundary file, and it is a setback each side
rather than a total width. To clear the same ground as a 15 m setback on a line
you draw here, enter a corridor width of 30 m.

A polygon obstruction, a rectangle from the **Tools** tab, and the main control
room and objects are placed from the Tools tab rather than from the palette. See
[Obstructions](/docs/layout/obstructions) and
[Site equipment](/docs/layout/site-equipment).

## Cable trenches

Four tools deal with trenches. They are the reason to open Sketch Mode on a
plant that is already routed: the automatic route is geometrically sound, but it
knows nothing about what is already buried on the site.

| Tool | What you do |
| --- | --- |
| **🟢 DC Trench** | Click vertices, right-click to finish: a DC trench |
| **🔴 AC Trench** | Click vertices, right-click to finish: an AC trench |
| **🟩 MV Trench** | Click vertices, right-click to finish: a medium-voltage trench |
| **✂ Del Trench** | Click any trench — automatic or hand-drawn — to delete it |

A trench you draw **replaces every automatic run of that type** in the plot,
and the cable and trench totals then come from what you drew. In a central
design **🔴 AC Trench** draws the trunk from the SMBs to the central inverter.

<Callout type="warn">
Trench edits are short-lived. A drawn trench survives leaving Sketch Mode but
not a **Generate Layout**, which lays out and routes every cable afresh. A
deleted automatic run lasts only while you are in Sketch Mode: with
**Calculate cables** on, leaving Sketch Mode routes every cable again and the
run comes back. Draw trenches after the last Generate, and do not rely on
deleting automatic ones.
</Callout>

What that means for the reported cable and trench lengths — and why trench
totals are shorter than conductor totals — is on
[Cable routing](/docs/layout/cables).

## An edit from start to finish

<Steps>
<Step>
### Switch Sketch Mode on

Click **Sketch** in the Layout view's tools. The input tabs are replaced by the
tool palette and the contextual bar appears above the plot.
</Step>

<Step>
### Zoom to the ground you are changing

Roll the mouse wheel over the area, or use **Zoom** to drag a box around it —
then click **Zoom** off again so your drags reach the sketch tool. **Home**
returns to the whole plant whenever you lose your place.
</Step>

<Step>
### Switch on the aids the edit needs

**🧲 Snap** for anything that has to meet existing geometry exactly.
**⊾ Ortho** for anything that has to run square. **▦ Grid** if you want a
reference to judge spacing against.
</Step>

<Step>
### Make the edit

Select with **↖ Move** and drag, nudge with **Ctrl** and the arrow keys, place
with **➕ Table** or **➕ LA**, clear ground with **🚧 Road**, reroute with a
trench tool. **↶ Undo** and **↷ Redo** step through anything that went wrong;
**Esc** cancels a half-finished action.
</Step>

<Step>
### Exit Sketch Mode

Click **Exit Sketch Mode — apply edits, recalc totals**. If anything on the
plot changed, the plant totals are recomputed here and the cables routed again.
Read the module count, capacity and arrester count in the Summary view and
confirm they moved the way you expected.
</Step>

<Step>
### Save the project

**Save project** in the view header writes the whole session to a `.slp` file,
sketch edits included, so the edited plant reopens as you left it. See
[Projects](/docs/projects).
</Step>
</Steps>

## What your edits mean for the numbers

Sketch Mode is not a drawing overlay. The objects you place are counted, and
the counting happens when you leave.

| Summary row | What your edits do to it |
| --- | --- |
| **Full tables** | Rises and falls with full tables placed and deleted |
| **Half tables** | Rises and falls with half units placed and deleted |
| **Modules** | Recomputed from the tables actually present |
| **DC capacity (MWp)** | Recomputed from the module count and the module wattage |
| **Lightning arresters** | Recomputed from the arresters actually present |

The full row list is in [Summary rows](/docs/reference/summary-columns); the
plant chips under the view follow the same figures.

Because the totals are recomputed rather than adjusted, the figure you read
after leaving Sketch Mode is the figure the whole application then works from.
The project report, the Google Earth file and the CAD drawing are all written
from the current layout, so they carry your edited plant, not the automatic one
— which is the point of editing it. See
[The project report](/docs/exports/project-report).

Two consequences worth planning around:

- **Calculate energy after editing, not before.** Year-one and lifetime
  generation are computed from the plant capacity. Edit the layout, exit
  Sketch Mode so the capacity settles, then calculate. See
  [How yield is calculated](/docs/energy/overview).
- **Rebuild the bill of materials after editing.** The materials list is
  computed from the layout, and **⚙ Rebuild from Layout** is what brings your
  edits into it. See [Bill of materials](/docs/bom/overview).

## What Generate Layout does to your edits

Pressing **Generate Layout** after editing does a precise thing, and the
intuitive guess is wrong in both directions:

1. If you are still in Sketch Mode, Generate leaves it first, so the edits are
   committed rather than lost.
2. If the layout was edited, Generate **re-places every table, tracker,
   inverter and control room from scratch** into the newly available space.
   Hand-placed and hand-deleted **tables do not survive** a Generate.
3. The obstructions, roads, corridors, main control room, USS, objects and
   annotations you placed **do** survive — they are carried onto the new
   layout, and the obstructions and corridors are handed to the placement as
   ground to keep clear, so the plant is rebuilt *around* your constraints
   rather than ignoring them.
4. Hand-drawn cable trenches **do not** survive: every cable is routed afresh.
5. An energy result is cleared; calculate energy again for the new layout.

<Callout type="warn">
The re-plot is forced back to the aligned grid: **Maximize placement** is
switched off for it, so that pile coordinates stay uniform for construction. If
you had it on, the plant you get back is the aligned one. The status line says *"Re-plotting the aligned grid around your sketch
edits…"* while this happens.
</Callout>

There is no "keep my layout and refresh the numbers" path. So the order of work
matters: draw your roads and corridors and place the control room first,
generate, and hand-edit individual tables and draw trenches **last** —
anything you do to tables or trenches before a Generate is discarded by it.
