---
title: Sketch Mode
description: The hand-editing surface — select, move, copy, delete, place tables, arresters, obstructions and cable trenches, and recompute the plant totals from your edits.
---

The generated layout is a starting point. It knows what the boundary file told
it: where the fence runs, where the water is, where a transmission line
crosses. It does not know that the north-west corner is a spoil heap, that the
owner wants two tables left out beside the access gate, or that the trench to
the second control room has to follow an existing culvert. Sketch Mode is where
you put that knowledge into the design.

Everything you place by hand is part of the plant. A table you add counts
towards the module count and the capacity; an arrester you add counts in the
arrester column; an obstruction you draw clears the tables under it. The
drawing tools — lines, text, dimensions, measurements — are covered separately
in [Annotations, measuring and layers](/docs/editing/annotations), because they
change the drawing rather than the plant.

## Turning Sketch Mode on and off

**✏ Sketch Mode** is on the toolbar above the plot, and starts off. Switch it
on and the left-hand panel changes from the input groups to the tool palette.
Switch it off and the input panel comes back.

<Screenshot id="sketch-palette" />

<Callout type="warn">
The plant totals are recomputed when you **leave** Sketch Mode, not while you
are editing. Module count, capacity and arrester count in the
**Layout & Energy Summary** stay at their pre-edit figures until you switch
Sketch Mode off, so switch it off before reading the summary, exporting, or
calculating energy.
</Callout>

## How the palette is organised

| Group | Tools | Covered in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Selecting and moving | **↖ Move** | this page |
| Copying and deleting | **⎘ Copy**, **🗑 Delete** | this page |
| Placing equipment | **➕ Table**, **➕ Half Table**, **➕ LA** (lightning arrester), the obstruction tool, **⚡ T-Line** | this page |

| Navigating | **🖐 Pan**, **🔍 Zoom** | this page |
| Drawing aids | **🧲 Snap**, **⊾ Ortho**, **▦ Grid**, **↶ Undo**, **↷ Redo**, **🎨 Colour** | this page |
| Drawing and annotating | lines, shapes, text, dimensions, layers, groups | [Annotations](/docs/editing/annotations) |
| Measuring | **📏 Measure**, **📐 Dimension** | [Annotations](/docs/editing/annotations) |

One tool is active at a time. Pick a tool, act on the plot, and the tool stays
armed until you pick another.

## Selecting and moving

**↖ Move** is the tool you spend most of your time in. It selects and it
drags, and it is also the tool that repositions anything you have already
placed.

| Action | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Click an object | Selects it |
| Shift-click another object | Adds it to the selection |
| Drag on empty space | Draws a rubber-band box; everything inside it is selected |
| Drag any selected object | Moves the whole selection together |
| Ctrl and an arrow key | Nudges the selection 1 m in that direction |

<Screenshot id="sketch-selection" />

The rubber band is how you work at block scale — drag across a run of tables,
then move or delete the lot in one action. The 1 m nudge is how you work at
table scale: pick up a table with the mouse, drop it roughly, then step it into
position without touching the mouse again.

### Moving a control room

A control room is not moved with **↖ Move**. With no pan or zoom tool active,
click and hold a control room on the plot, drag it, and release. The layout
rebuilds around the new position — tables under the new footprint are cleared,
and the tables it vacated are available again. A drop where the room does not
fit entirely inside the usable area snaps back to where it started. See
[Control rooms](/docs/layout/icr).

## Copying and deleting

| Tool | What you do | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **⎘ Copy** | Select, then use the tool | The selection is duplicated, offset 5 m east and 5 m north of the original |
| **🗑 Delete** | Click any object | The object is removed |

**Ctrl+C** and **Ctrl+V** do the same thing as **⎘ Copy**, with the same
offset. The offset means the copy lands clear of the original, so you can see
it and drag it where it belongs instead of it sitting invisibly on top of what
you copied.

**🗑 Delete** removes on a single click, with no confirmation. **↶ Undo** is
the way back.

## Placing equipment

Each of these adds a real object to the plant, not a drawing of one.

| Tool | What you do | Effect on the layout |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **➕ Table** | Click a position | Places one full module mounting structure (MMS) table, at the size and orientation set in the table configuration |
| **➕ Half Table** | Click a position | Places a half unit — half the table's east–west width, carrying half the strings |
| **➕ LA** | Click a position | Places a lightning arrester and clears the tables under its footprint |
| **🚧** (obstruction) | Drag a rectangle | Adds a rectangular obstruction and removes the tables overlapping it |
| **⚡ T-Line** | Click each vertex, then right-click to finish | Draws a transmission line across the plot |

A few things follow from that table.

**Tables you place are ordinary tables.** They take their dimensions from
**MMS-Table Configuration**, so a hand-placed table is the same object the
automatic pass places — see [Table configuration](/docs/inputs/table). A half
unit is half the east–west width and carries half the strings, and counts as
half a table wherever tables are counted.

**Arresters can be added even when the automatic pass placed none.** With
**Place Lightning Arresters** left off in **Site Parameters** no arresters are
placed and tables fill the whole usable area; **➕ LA** is how you add the few
you actually want. The footprint that gets cleared is the arrester footprint
for your mounting type, which is much larger on a fixed-tilt plant than on a
tracker plant. See [Lightning arresters](/docs/layout/lightning-arresters).

**An obstruction drawn here behaves like an obstruction read from the boundary
file.** It is subtracted from the ground available to tables, and the tables
already overlapping it are removed. The rectangle is the Sketch Mode tool; the
obstruction controls beside the plot also offer **Draw Polygon**, **Undo Last**
and **Clear All** for a shape a rectangle cannot describe. See
[Obstructions and exclusions](/docs/layout/obstructions).

**A transmission line is a multi-click tool, and it asks for its own corridor.**
Click each vertex in turn and right-click to finish, the same pattern as the
polyline drawing tools. You are then asked for a **corridor width**, and the
centre-line is buffered by half of it — so the strip cleared of tables is the
width you gave, not twice it. Give no width and the corridor is **10.0 m**. The
centre-line is drawn in red, the corridor becomes ground to keep clear, and the
tables inside it are removed.

**Transmission line corridor** in **Site Parameters** has no part in this. That
field applies to the lines read from a boundary file, and it is a setback each
side rather than a total width. To clear the same ground as a 15 m setback on a
line you draw here, enter a corridor width of 30 m.

### Placing a main control room or an object

The main control room and free-standing objects are placed from the controls
beside the plot rather than from the tool palette. **Place MCR** arms the
placement — the button reads **Cancel Placement** while it is armed — and the
next click on the plot drops the room. **Remove MCR** takes it away again.
**Place Object** and **Remove Objects** work the same way for the objects whose
footprint and height you set in **Structures & Shadow**.

A click outside the plant boundary is refused with
*"Please place inside the boundary."* — nothing is placed, and the tool stays
armed for another try.

On a multi-plot site, placing the main control room also drops a unit
substation in every other plot. See
[Site equipment](/docs/layout/site-equipment) and
[Multi-plot sites](/docs/layout/multi-plot).

## Cable trenches

Four tools deal with trenches. They are the reason to open Sketch Mode on a
plant that is already routed: the automatic route is geometrically sound, but it
knows nothing about what is already buried on the site.

| Tool | What you do |
| --- | --- |
| **🟢 DC Trench** | Click points to draw a DC trench polyline |
| **🔴 AC Trench** | Click points to draw an AC trench polyline |
| **🟩 MV Trench** | Click points to draw a medium-voltage trench polyline |
| **✂ Del Trench** | Click a cable trench — automatic or hand-drawn — to delete it |

<Callout type="info">
Trench edits survive the next run. Once you have deleted an automatic trench or
drawn one of your own, **Generate Layout** keeps the current cables instead of
re-routing them, so your route is not overwritten the next time you change an
input and generate again.
</Callout>

What that means for the reported cable and trench lengths — and why trench
totals are shorter than conductor totals — is on
[Cable routing](/docs/layout/cables).

## Navigating

| Tool | What you do |
| --- | --- |
| **🖐 Pan** | Drag to move the view |
| **🔍 Zoom** | Drag a box to zoom into it |
| **Home (⌂)** | Fits the view to the whole plant |

**Home** is worth remembering. It does not step back through your zoom
history — it fits the view to the plant's own extent, so it is one click back
to the whole site from wherever you have got to.

Note that a control-room drag needs **no** pan or zoom tool active. If a
click-hold on a control room pans the view instead of picking the room up, turn
the pan tool off first.

## The drawing aids

These do not place anything. They change how the tools behave and how the
result looks.

| Control | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **🧲 Snap** | Constrains what you click to snap onto nearby geometry, so a trench meets a control room exactly rather than nearly |
| **⊾ Ortho** | Constrains what you draw to horizontal and vertical, so a trench or a line cannot pick up an accidental few degrees |
| **▦ Grid** | Draws a reference grid on the plot |
| **↶ Undo** | Steps back through your edits |
| **↷ Redo** | Steps forward again |
| **🎨 Colour** | Sets the stroke colour used by the drawing tools |

**🧲 Snap** and **⊾ Ortho** are the two to switch on before drawing anything
you intend to build from. A trench drawn free-hand a degree or two off square
looks right on the screen and exports as a drawing nobody can set out from.

The stroke colour applies to drawn elements. The default stroke is an
orange-red, 1.5 pt wide; **🎨 Colour** changes it for the elements you draw
next, not for what is already on the plot. Plant objects — tables, control
rooms, cables, arresters — keep their own colours from the legend regardless.

## An edit from start to finish

<Steps>
<Step>
### Switch Sketch Mode on

Click **✏ Sketch Mode** on the toolbar. The input panel is replaced by the tool
palette.
</Step>

<Step>
### Zoom to the ground you are changing

Use **🔍 Zoom** to drag a box around the area. **Home (⌂)** returns to the whole
plant whenever you lose your place.
</Step>

<Step>
### Switch on the aids the edit needs

**🧲 Snap** for anything that has to meet existing geometry exactly.
**⊾ Ortho** for anything that has to run square. **▦ Grid** if you want a
reference to judge spacing against.
</Step>

<Step>
### Make the edit

Select with **↖ Move** and drag, nudge with Ctrl and the arrow keys, place with
**➕ Table** or **➕ LA**, clear ground with the obstruction tool, reroute with a
trench tool. **↶ Undo** and **↷ Redo** step through anything that went wrong.
</Step>

<Step>
### Switch Sketch Mode off

The plant totals are recomputed here. Read the module count, capacity and
arrester count in the summary and confirm they moved the way you expected.
</Step>

<Step>
### Save the project

**💾 Save(Project)** writes the whole session to a `.slp` file, sketch edits
included, so the edited plant reopens as you left it. See
[Projects](/docs/projects).
</Step>
</Steps>

## What your edits mean for the numbers

Sketch Mode is not a drawing overlay. The objects you place are counted, and
the counting happens when you leave.

| Summary column | What your edits do to it |
| --- | --- |
| `Full Tbl` | Rises and falls with full tables placed and deleted |
| `Half Tbl` | Rises and falls with half units placed and deleted |
| `Modules` | Recomputed from the tables actually present |
| `DC(MWp)` | Recomputed from the module count and the module wattage |
| `LA` | Recomputed from the arresters actually present |

The full column list is in
[Summary columns](/docs/reference/summary-columns).

Because the summary is recomputed rather than adjusted, the figure you read
after leaving Sketch Mode is the figure the whole application then works from.
The PDF report, the Google Earth file and the CAD drawing are all written from
the current layout, so they carry your edited plant, not the automatic one —
which is the point of editing it. See [Exports](/docs/exports/pdf-report).

Two consequences worth planning around:

- **Calculate energy after editing, not before.** Year-one and lifetime
  generation are computed from the plant capacity. Edit the layout, leave
  Sketch Mode so the capacity settles, then calculate. See
  [How yield is calculated](/docs/energy/overview).
- **Rebuild the bill of materials after editing.** The materials list is
  computed from the layout, and **⚙ Rebuild from Layout** is what brings your
  edits into it. See [Bill of materials](/docs/bom/overview).

## What Generate Layout does to your edits

Pressing **Generate Layout** again after editing does **not** preserve the
tables you placed and deleted by hand. It re-places every table, tracker,
inverter and control room from scratch.

What survives is the ground you ruled out. The obstructions, corridors and main
control room you drew are collected and handed to the placement as areas to keep
clear, so the plant is rebuilt *around* your constraints rather than ignoring
them. Hand-drawn cable trenches also survive, by a separate mechanism described
in [Cable routing and trenches](/docs/layout/cables).

<Callout type="warn">
The re-plot is forced back to the aligned grid: **Maximize placement** is
switched off for it, so that pile coordinates stay uniform for construction. If
you had it on, the plant you get back is the aligned one, at a lower table
count. The status line says *"Re-plotting the aligned grid around your sketch
edits…"* while this happens.
</Callout>

So the order of work matters. Draw your obstructions and place the control room
first, generate, and hand-edit individual tables **last** — anything you do to
tables before a Generate is discarded by it.
