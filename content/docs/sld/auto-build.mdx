---
title: Building a diagram from the plant
description: The automatic single-line diagram — built from the layout, the electrical grouping and the inverter file, in a Summary and a Full form, and kept in step with the layout.
---

The automatic **single-line diagram** — SLD — is drawn from the plant you have
already laid out. It reads the layout's equipment counts, the **electrical
grouping** — the combiner boxes, the transformers, their ratings and voltages, as
the [ACCB & Transformer card](/docs/inputs/accb-and-transformers) produces them —
and the inverter file, then places the chain from the modules through to the grid
and annotates each block with how many of that item there are and what it is
rated at.

It never reads the bill of materials, so editing the materials list or changing a
BOM profile cannot change the diagram. It is a starting point, not a finished
drawing: the chain is the generation-to-grid path, and the protection, switching
and instrumentation an issued SLD carries is drawing work you do on top of it.

## What it needs

<Steps>
<Step>
### Generate the layout

The equipment counts come from the placed plant, and the grouping is made when
the plant is placed. With no layout the view reads *"No layout yet"*. See
[Your first layout](/docs/first-layout).

**No energy calculation is needed.** Running the yield calculation first adds
nothing to the diagram.
</Step>

<Step>
### Load the inverter file

The ratings and the low-voltage side come from the inverter file on the inverter
card. Without one the diagram is still drawn, by count: the transformers print
*— kVA* and *33 kV / — V*, the inverter's rating and the plant's MW AC figure are
left off, and the **DIAGRAM** card offers **Load OND…**. See
[Inverter settings](/docs/inputs/inverter).
</Step>

<Step>
### Open the SLD view

Click the **SLD** tab (**Ctrl+7**). With a layout and no diagram, the automatic
diagram is built on entry, with no question to answer.
</Step>
</Steps>

## How it stays current

An automatic diagram you have not touched rebuilds at once whenever the layout, the
**ACCB & Transformer** card or the inverter file changes it, as one undo step. One
you have edited is kept and marked out of date, with **Rebuild** and **Keep this
diagram** over the drawing, so nothing you drew is lost without your say. The full
rule is on
[Building the single-line diagram](/docs/sld/overview#when-the-layout-changes).

## The chain it draws

Which chain you get follows the inverter choice made at launch, because the two
topologies convert the DC in different places. See
[Choosing a design mode](/docs/design-modes).

<Tabs items={["String inverter", "Central inverter"]}>
<Tab value="String inverter">

```
PV → String Inverter (×N) → ACCB (×n) → Transformer (×n) → MV Panel → Grid
```

| Stage | What it is | What the build writes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **PV** | The module field feeding the inverters | The module count and wattage, such as *88,984 × 610Wp*, and *PV Array* with the string count |
| **String Inverter (×N)** | The string inverters placed across the plant | The rating in kVA from the inverter file, and *String Inverter ×N* |
| **ACCB (×n)** | The AC combiner boxes the inverters land on. `n` is the grouping's count: in each ICR block, the block's inverters ÷ **Inverters per ACCB, max**, rounded up | *ACCB ×n* |
| **Transformer** | The inverter duty transformers taking the collected output up to medium voltage | The rating, such as *16,000 kVA* or a range *12,500–16,000 kVA*, the voltages such as *33 kV / 800 V*, and *Transformer ×n (3-Wdg)* |
| **MV Panel** | The medium-voltage switchgear the transformers feed | *33 kV Switchgear* — the card's **MV voltage** |
| **Grid** | The outgoing connection | *GRID 33 kV* |

The wires between the blocks carry the multiplicities: the modules and strings on
one inverter (*560 modules, 20 strings*), the most inverters on one ACCB
(*15 Str.Inv*), the most ACCBs on one transformer (*2 ACCB*), and the transformer
count (*8 Tx*).

</Tab>
<Tab value="Central inverter">

```
PV → SMB (×N) → Central Inverter (×n) → Transformer (×n) → MV Panel → Grid
```

| Stage | What it is | What the build writes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **PV** | The module field feeding the string monitoring boxes | The module count and wattage, and the string count |
| **SMB (×N)** | The string monitoring boxes placed across the plant, each passing its strings' DC output on without converting it | *SMB ×N* |
| **Central Inverter (×n)** | The central inverters inside the control rooms. `n` is the grouping's count, made per ICR block | *Central Inverter ×n*, with the SMBs per central inverter on its wire |
| **Transformer** | The transformers the central inverters land on | The rating and voltages, the winding count, and the central inverters per transformer on its wire |
| **MV Panel** | The medium-voltage switchgear the transformers feed | The card's **MV voltage** |
| **Grid** | The outgoing connection | The same voltage |

There is no AC leg before the inverter here, because the conversion happens inside
the control room rather than in the field.

</Tab>
</Tabs>

A Y harness and inline string fuses, where the design has them, appear as marks on
the wire. The sheet is headed *SINGLE-LINE DIAGRAM*, with a line giving the plant's
MWp DC, its MW AC (with an inverter file), the topology and the number of ICRs.

Both chains are drawn along a horizontal bus, which is why the PV module, the
inverter and the transformers are placed already rotated to line up with it. See
[Symbol reference](/docs/sld/symbols#which-symbols-arrive-pre-rotated).

## Summary or Full

The build has two forms, switched by **Summary | Full** on the **DIAGRAM** card. The
status bar confirms the change, such as *"SLD shown as Full."*

| Form | What is drawn | Paper |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Summary** | *"One chain for the whole plant."* Each block carries a **×N** multiplicity for how many of it the plant holds | By its rows, as below |
| **Full** | **One row per feeder** — per ACCB in a string-inverter design, per central inverter in a central-inverter design — under column heads such as *PV array*, *String inverters*, *ACCB* and *Transformer*, labelled **ACCB-1**, **ACCB-2** … or **C.Inv-1** …, and grouped onto their transformers, **TX-1 · ICR-1** and so on | A3 up to 10 rows, A1 up to 28, A0 beyond |

Choose the form before you start editing. Switching rebuilds the diagram, and on an
edited diagram the card warns *"Switching rebuilds and discards your edits
(undoable)."* Summary is the readable one on a plant with dozens of machines; Full
shows every feeder and every transformer separately.

### The transformer symbol

The transformer symbol is chosen from the feeders on the transformer, one
low-voltage winding each:

| Feeders on the transformer | Symbol |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | 2-winding |
| 2 | 3-winding |
| 3 | 4-winding |
| 4 or more | Multi-winding |

Summary uses the most feeders on any one transformer; Full uses each transformer's
own. One multi-winding symbol draws every transformer above four windings, so read
the winding count from its label, such as *Transformer ×8 (3-Wdg)*: the label
follows the grouping whichever symbol draws it.

## The grouping it draws

The counts, ratings and voltages on the chain are those of the plant's electrical
grouping, set on the **ACCB & Transformer** card on the Electrical tab —
**Transformer** in a central-inverter design:

| Rule on the card | Default |
| --- | --- |
| **Inverters per ACCB, max:** *(string designs)* | 15 |
| **Transformer forms (windings):** | 5, 3, 2 |
| **Transformer size limit:** | 17.2 MVA, but never above the largest listed rating — 16,000 kVA with the default list |
| **Standard transformer ratings (kVA):** | 2,500, 3,150, 5,000, 6,300, 8,000, 10,000, 12,500, 16,000 |
| **MV voltage:** | 33.0 kV |

The grouping is made per ICR block. Each block takes the largest form first, with
every transformer full, and a block whose load is over the limit drops to a smaller
form. Each transformer takes the smallest listed rating at or above its load, and
one that fits no listed rating prints *— kVA*, with a notice on the **DIAGRAM**
card. The full rules are on the card's own page.

**The diagram, the bill of materials, the Summary view and the cable schedule all
read the same grouping**, so the box and transformer counts agree between them.

<Callout>
If your scheme groups equipment differently — a different number of inverters per
box, or transformer sizes you have already fixed with a supplier — set it on the
card: the inverters per ACCB, the transformer forms, and your own list of ratings.
An untouched diagram follows at once.
</Callout>

## What the annotations mean

Each block on the chain is annotated with two things:

- **A multiplicity**, written as `×N` — how many of that item the plant carries.
  One inverter symbol carrying a multiplicity stands for every inverter in the
  plant, drawn once. That is normal single-line practice, and it is what keeps the
  sheet readable on a plant with dozens of machines.
- **A rating** — for the transformers, from the grouping; for the inverter, the
  nominal AC power in the inverter file.

Both are put on the sheet by the build. Where one is wrong — a rating that does not
match the machine you are buying, or a count you have since revised — change the
card, so that the diagram and everything else follow. To change only the drawing,
edit the label by double-clicking it in **Edit diagram**, or place your own with
**Text**. That makes the diagram an edited one.

## What to do next

<Steps>
<Step>
### Check every rating

Read each one against the equipment you are actually procuring.
</Step>

<Step>
### Add what the build does not cover

The chain carries the generation-to-grid path. Isolators, breakers, instrument
transformers, meters, relays, arresters and phase indication are yours to place and
wire in. See [Symbol reference](/docs/sld/symbols).
</Step>

<Step>
### Read the title block

Click **Sheet**. The title block fills itself from **File ▸ Company…** and the
project details, and prints **—** where they are empty. See
[Building the single-line diagram](/docs/sld/overview#the-title-block).
</Step>

<Step>
### Export it

Use **PDF…** or **DWG…** on the **EXPORT** card. See
[Exporting the diagram](/docs/sld/export).
</Step>
</Steps>
