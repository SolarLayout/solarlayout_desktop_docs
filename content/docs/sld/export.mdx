---
title: Exporting the diagram
description: The SLD view's two exports — a PDF of the sheet and a CAD drawing whose symbols are real entities — and how they relate to the deliverables package.
---

A **single-line diagram** — SLD — leaves the SLD view by two routes, both on the
**EXPORT** card in the left column. Both write the diagram as you built, edited or
traced it, frame and title block included. What differs is what the receiving end can
do with it.

| Button | What you get |
| --- | --- |
| **PDF…** | The sheet on its own, as a one-page PDF |
| **DWG…** | The sheet as a CAD drawing, with the symbols written as real entities |

Both need active access. See [Building the single-line diagram](/docs/sld/overview).

## When the buttons wait

The **EXPORT** card's note says why the buttons are off.

| State | What the card says | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nothing on the sheet | *"There is no SLD to export yet."* | Generate a layout, or draw by hand |
| The layout is out of date | The reason the results are out of date | Generate again. See [Out-of-date results](/docs/workspace/out-of-date-results) |
| An edited diagram is out of date | *"Exports wait until you rebuild or keep this diagram (see the note above the drawing)."* | Click **Rebuild** or **Keep this diagram** over the drawing |
| A kept diagram | *"Exports this diagram as it is (a MWp); the layout is b MWp."* | Nothing: the buttons work, and the note tells you the two differ |

## The deliverables package has its own diagram

The [deliverables package](/docs/exports/deliverables) carries a single-line diagram in
its [drawing set](/docs/exports/drawing-set), but that is a separate, automatic diagram
drawn from the design. Your edits and tracing do not reach it, and the package does not
wait for this view. Anything you added by hand leaves only through **PDF…** and
**DWG…**.

## A PDF of the sheet

**PDF…** opens a save window, *Export SLD to PDF*, with the file name `SLD.pdf`. The
status bar then reads *"SLD exported to"* and the path.

<Screenshot id="sld-page" />

This is the review copy. It carries the border, the zone grid, the legend band and the
title block, so a reviewer can mark it up against a zone reference and hand it back. A
traced background prints at full strength.

The paper follows the diagram: A3, A1 or A0 by the number of rows.

## A CAD drawing

**DWG…** opens a save window, *Export SLD to CAD*, with the file name `SLD.dwg`,
offering *AutoCAD Drawing (\*.dwg)* and *DXF Drawing (\*.dxf)*. Choose `.dxf`: it is
the CAD interchange format every CAD program reads. A `.dwg` needs the free ODA File Converter, which the application does
not include, and without it the file is saved as DXF and the message says so.

### The symbols come across as geometry

This is the point of the CAD route. Each symbol's shape is written into the drawing
file as **real CAD entities**, rotated as it sits on screen: a drawing a drafter can
select, move, copy and edit, not an image of your screen dropped into a frame.

The same holds for a symbol you traced yourself, because it is stored as strokes rather
than as a picture. See
[Importing diagrams and custom symbols](/docs/sld/import-and-custom-symbols).

<Callout type="warn">
The one exception is a traced background. A scanned page is a picture on the sheet and
is not written to the CAD file at all. If the CAD file is the deliverable, redraw that
content with palette symbols, or send the PDF as well.
</Callout>

### The sheet frame comes across too

The frame and the title block are in the CAD file. A drafting team receiving the framed
sheet can work on the drawing itself rather than dropping it into their own frame. The
title block carries what the project gave it, and **—** where a field has nothing.

## Exporting is not saving

Both routes produce a document for somebody else. Neither is how you keep the diagram.
The diagram is part of the project: **Save project** keeps its elements, its form, its
paper and any traced background, and reopening the project brings it back editable. The
undo history does not come with it. See [Projects](/docs/projects).

## Which route to use when

| You are sending it to | Use | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A bid or tender pack | The [deliverables package](/docs/exports/overview) | One package carries the drawing set, the design report, the schedules and the BOM together, with its automatic diagram |
| A reviewer or an internal approval | **PDF…** | Your diagram on its own, with the zone grid to refer to, openable by anyone |
| A drafting office or a detailed-design team | **DWG…** | Real entities they can edit, inside the sheet frame |

If you are unsure, send the PDF for agreement and the CAD file for work. The PDF settles
what the diagram says; the drawing file is what somebody builds on.

## Before you export

<Steps>
<Step>
### Check the ratings and the equipment

The automatic build's annotations come from the grouping on the **ACCB & Transformer**
card and the inverter file, not from your purchase order. See
[Building a diagram from the plant](/docs/sld/auto-build).
</Step>

<Step>
### Read the title block

Click **Sheet** at the bottom right of the drawing. The title block is filled from
**File ▸ Company…** and the project details. **DRAWING NO.** and **REV.** print **—** on
this sheet. See [Building the single-line diagram](/docs/sld/overview#the-title-block).
</Step>

<Step>
### Check that nothing sits outside the frame

**Sheet** shows the page as it prints, whatever the zoom.
</Step>

<Step>
### Export, then open what you exported

Check the extension of the CAD file, and that the page reads as you expect.
</Step>
</Steps>
