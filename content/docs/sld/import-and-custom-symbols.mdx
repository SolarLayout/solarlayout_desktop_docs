title: Importing diagrams and custom symbols
description: Tracing an existing diagram onto the sheet, as editable elements or as a scanned background, and turning your own image into a symbol under My symbols.
---

The SLD view takes two kinds of import. An existing **single-line diagram** — SLD —
comes onto the sheet with **Trace over a PDF / DXF…**. An image of a symbol becomes a
symbol of your own with **Import image…**. Both are in the editing panel: click
**Edit diagram** in the [SLD view](/docs/sld/overview).

| What you import | How | What you get |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A DXF drawing or a PDF with vector lines | **More ▸ Trace over a PDF / DXF…** | Editable lines, wires and labels on the sheet |
| A scanned PDF, with no vector lines | **More ▸ Trace over a PDF / DXF…** | A background picture to draw over |
| An image of a symbol | **My symbols ▸ Import image…** | A symbol traced into strokes, on a tile of your own |

## Tracing an existing diagram

**Trace over a PDF / DXF…** reads the file and places it in the sheet's drawing area,
with its proportions kept. What it becomes depends on what the file contains. A
`.dwg` also works if you have installed the free ODA File Converter, which the
application does not include; otherwise save the drawing as `.dxf`.

### A DXF or a vector PDF becomes editable

Lines come in as polylines and wires, and text as labels, each an element you can
select, move and delete. The status bar reads *"Imported n editable elements. Ctrl+Z
undoes the import."* They are the drawing's own lines rather than typed equipment
symbols: a file records lines, arcs and text, and not which of them were a circuit
breaker.

The sheet is a hand drawing from then on. The **DIAGRAM** card reads **Drawn by hand**
and it doesn't follow the layout.

A CAD file with nothing to draw reports **Nothing to Import**: *"No drawable geometry
(lines, polylines, circles, text) was found in this CAD file."*

### A scanned PDF becomes a background

A PDF with no vector lines has nothing to read as elements, so its **first page**
comes in as a picture behind your work, with its longest side capped at **2600
pixels**. The status bar reads *"This PDF has no vector lines (it may be scanned), so
it is a background to trace over. Draw on top, then export."*

<Callout type="warn">
A background is a picture. You cannot move a symbol inside it or change a rating on
it; only what you place on top is editable. It shows faint on screen so that your work
stands out, prints at full strength in **PDF…**, and is not written to a CAD file.
</Callout>

Read that as a working method:

- **To annotate a scanned diagram**, add your notes and labels over the top. Nothing
  underneath moves.
- **To extend it**, draw the additional bays or feeders beside it with real symbols.
- **To redraw it properly**, trace over it with palette symbols, then click **Remove
  the traced background** under **More ▸**. You end with a diagram made entirely of
  real symbols.

### When something is already on the sheet

Tracing over a diagram or a background asks first, inline in the panel: *"Replace the
diagram with “file”?"*

| Button | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Replace diagram** | Discards your elements and any traced background |
| **Add on top** | Keeps them and adds the file over them |
| **Cancel** | Changes nothing |

With only a background on the sheet, the first button reads **Replace background**.
**Ctrl+Z** undoes a blank sheet, a clear, a trace, or removing the traced background.

## Drawing over an existing diagram

<Steps>
<Step>
### Open the editing panel

Click the **SLD** tab, then **Edit diagram**. To start from nothing, choose **More ▸
Start a blank sheet**.
</Step>

<Step>
### Trace the file

Click **More ▸ Trace over a PDF / DXF…** and pick the diagram. Answer the question if
the sheet already holds something.
</Step>

<Step>
### Draw what you need on top

Place symbols, connect them with **Wire**, and label with **Text**.
</Step>

<Step>
### Remove the background when you are done with it

If the file came in as a background, click **More ▸ Remove the traced background**.
Your work stays.
</Step>

<Step>
### Export

See [Exporting the diagram](/docs/sld/export). The project keeps the traced background
with the rest of the diagram.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Importing your own symbol

**Import image…** under **My symbols** adds a symbol the palette does not carry — a
make-specific device, a customer's block symbol, a glyph your drawing office uses. It
reads PNG, JPG, BMP and GIF images.

The image is **traced into strokes** rather than pasted in as a picture. Two
consequences follow:

- **It draws as crisp line art.** Zoom in and you get lines, not enlarged pixels.
- **It exports to CAD as real vectors**, the same treatment the built-in symbols get.

Your symbols are stored in the `SolarLayout_Symbols` folder in your home folder and
stay available across projects. They appear under **My symbols**.

### The Symbol Editor

Tracing is automatic, and automatic tracing is never perfect. So the **Symbol Editor —
edit, then Accept** window opens first, showing the traced strokes.

<Screenshot id="sld-symbol-editor" />

| Tool | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **✏ Pen** | Adds a stroke — for an outline the trace missed or broke |
| **🧽 Erase** | Removes a stroke — for a speck, a stray edge, or a piece of the background that came through |
| **🗑 Clear** | Removes every stroke, so you can start the drawing again |

**Accept** moves on to naming the symbol. **Reject** discards the trace and adds
nothing.

### Naming it

The **Symbol name** window takes the **Name** — *"Shown on its tile under My symbols ·
an existing name is replaced"* — and **Save symbol** adds it. The status bar reads
*"Saved the symbol “name”. It is under My symbols."*

### Removing one

Right-click its tile and choose *"Remove “name” from My symbols…"*. The panel asks
inline, *"Its file is deleted. Undo can't bring it back."*, with **Remove symbol** and
**Cancel**.

Copies you have already placed keep their strokes, so removing a symbol does not change
a drawing that uses it, and a project opens with them on another computer too.

## Tracing a symbol, step by step

<Steps>
<Step>
### Prepare the image

Crop it to the symbol alone, on a plain background, with the line work as dark and as
sharp as you can get it.
</Step>

<Step>
### Import it

Open **My symbols**, click **Import image…** and pick the file. The Symbol Editor opens
on the traced strokes.
</Step>

<Step>
### Clean up the trace

Erase anything that is not part of the symbol with **🧽 Erase**, and add the strokes
the trace missed with **✏ Pen**.
</Step>

<Step>
### Accept and name it

Click **Accept**, type a **Name** and click **Save symbol**.
</Step>

<Step>
### Place it like any other symbol

Click its tile, then click the drawing. Turn it with **Rotate 90°**, wire it in, and
label it with **Text**.
</Step>
</Steps>

## What traces well

The trace follows outlines, so give it outlines to follow: a clean line drawing with
strong contrast. Expect to repair anything fainter in the editor.

An image with no outline to follow reports *"Could not extract an outline from this
image."* and asks for a clearer line drawing or a higher-contrast image. Accepting an
empty editor reports *"The symbol is empty — nothing to save."*

Two habits help more than anything else:

- **Crop to the symbol.** Anything else in the frame — a title block, a neighbouring
  device, a caption — traces too, and then has to be erased.
- **Raise the contrast before importing** rather than repairing the result afterwards.
