title: How yield is calculated
description: The energy model month by month, the performance ratio, specific yield, degradation and both capacity factors, the Yield tab that runs it, and the Energy view.
---

The energy calculation works month by month. Each month's in-plane irradiation
goes through that month's performance ratio and becomes energy; the twelve
months add up to one year-one figure. That figure is then degraded across the
plant lifetime and reported at three exceedance probabilities. Every input is a
field you can see on the **Yield** tab, a value fetched and shown on screen, or
a setting you can open, and the **Workings** view shows how the result was
reached. That is what makes the output defensible.

One run gives one year-one energy. The Energy view, the Summary, the chart
window, the time-series file and the design report all carry the same figure.

This page is the whole chain in order, the controls that run it, and every
place the result appears. The sub-pages hold the detail behind each link in
the chain.

## What the calculation needs before it can run

Two things, and it will not produce a yield without either.

**A generated layout.** Plant capacity in kWp is the placed module count
multiplied by the module wattage, so it does not exist until placement has run.
See [Your first layout](/docs/first-layout). Everything else in the chain is a
per-kWp figure that only becomes energy when it is multiplied by that capacity.

**A site position.** Latitude and longitude decide the solar geometry, the
irradiance the weather source returns, the automatic tilt and the automatic row
pitch. A Google Earth boundary file carries the position inside it. A CAD
drawing or a raster image gets one in the **Site reference** window when it is
loaded, from the drawing's own UTM coordinates or from the latitude and
longitude you type.

<Callout type="warn">
A CAD or image boundary loaded as **Layout only** still produces a layout: the
geometry is complete and the summary fills in. The site's location is unknown,
though, so **energy calculation is off**. **Calculate Energy** is disabled,
and its tooltip, the Yield tab's status line and the Energy view read *"Energy
yield is off: the site's location is unknown (layout only)."* To get a yield,
pick the file again and locate it. See [CAD boundary](/docs/inputs/cad-boundary)
and [Image boundary](/docs/inputs/image-boundary).
</Callout>

The result also has to belong to the layout on screen. Energy is calculated for
the last generated layout, so once an input changes and the layout is
[out of date](/docs/workspace/out-of-date-results), **Calculate Energy** is
refused with *"Regenerate the layout first: inputs changed since the last
run."* That includes every loss on this tab: changing a loss makes the whole
layout out of date. Regenerate, then calculate again.

## The chain, in order

### 1. In-plane irradiance

The calculation starts from **GTI**, global tilted irradiance: the irradiation
arriving on the plane of the modules, in kWh/m². It is not the same quantity as
**GHI**, global horizontal irradiance, which is what most weather records
contain. GTI is the one that drives the yield.

The run takes twelve monthly GTI values. PVGIS, its NASA POWER fallback and an
hourly file all give twelve. See [Weather data](/docs/energy/weather).

### 2. Performance ratio

**PR**, the performance ratio, is the fraction of the energy a plant would
produce if every module ran at its nameplate efficiency that it actually
delivers to the grid. It is a dimensionless number below 1, and it carries
every loss between the light hitting the glass and the energy leaving the
plant: inverter conversion, cable resistance, soiling, module temperature,
mismatch, shading, availability, transformer and the rest.

You do not enter PR. You enter the components, and PR follows from them. The
components are combined **multiplicatively**, as a product of the factors and of
one minus each loss, not as one minus their sum. Each month gets its own figure,
`PR_m`, because module temperature changes through the year. The relation,
component by component, is on [The loss breakdown](/docs/energy/losses).

### 3. Each month's energy

```
E_m (MWh) = capacity_kWp × GTI_m (kWh/m²) × PR_m × gain / 1000

gain = LID × (1 + bifacial gain) × weather data correction
```

`capacity_kWp` is the plant's installed DC capacity: the **DC capacity (MWp)**
row of the Summary view and the **DC capacity** chip, in kWp.

| Term in the gain | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `LID` | `1 − 1st year degradation ÷ 100`. **1st year degradation** ships at **1.0 %** and accepts 0–10 %. It stands for the loss a module takes in its first year of exposure and never recovers, principally light-induced degradation. It is a one-off step, not a rate |
| `bifacial gain` | The rear-side gain on a bifacial plant, zero otherwise. See [Bifacial modules](/docs/energy/bifacial) |
| `weather data correction` | **1.05** by default for PVGIS and NASA POWER weather, **1.0** for an hourly file. See [Weather data](/docs/energy/weather#weather-data-correction) |

### 4. The year

```
Year 1 energy = Σ E_m
Year's GTI    = Σ GTI_m
Year's PR     = Σ(GTI_m × PR_m) / Σ GTI_m
```

Year-one energy is the sum of the months, so it already carries first-year
degradation, the bifacial gain and the correction. The year's performance ratio
is the months' ratios weighted by their sunlight.

A run that has only a yearly irradiance figure, with no months, uses one
performance ratio for the year, with the entered temperature loss, in place of
the sum: `Year 1 energy = capacity_kWp × GTI × PR × gain / 1000`.

### 5. Specific yield

```
Specific yield = Year 1 energy / DC capacity
```

**Specific yield** is the year's energy per unit of installed DC capacity, in
kWh/kWp: the IEC final yield. It is the figure to compare two designs on the
same site with, because it is independent of how big the plant is. It includes
first-year degradation, the bifacial gain and the correction, so it is not the
same number as GTI multiplied by PR.

### 6. Every year after the first

```
Year n (n ≥ 2) = Year 1 energy × (1 − annual degradation %)^(n−1)
```

**Annual degradation** ships at **0.4 %/yr** and accepts 0–5 %/yr. It
compounds: the exponent is `n − 1`, so year 2 has taken one year of
degradation and the final year of a 30-year plant has taken 29.

**Plant lifetime** ships at **30** years and accepts 1–50. It sets how far the
series runs.

### 7. Capacity factor, on DC and on AC

```
CUF on DC = Year 1 energy / (capacity_kWp × 8760) × 100 %
CUF on AC = Year 1 energy / (plant AC_kW × 8760) × 100 %
```

**CUF**, the capacity utilisation factor, is year-one energy as a percentage of
what the plant would generate running flat out at its rated capacity for every
hour of the year. Both are worked out on the same year-one energy, against
different capacities, and both are shown wherever a CUF appears.

**Plant AC** is the AC capacity you typed in
[Simulation with AC Capacity](/docs/simulation/ac-capacity) while its cap is
on, and otherwise the installed inverter AC. Without an inverter file there is
no AC rating, so **CUF on AC** reads `—` with the note *needs an OND file*. On
a plant of several plots the total is all the energy over all the AC, and it
reads `—` if any plot lacks an AC. Tenders and PPAs state the CUF on the
contracted AC, so check which one a request means.

## A worked run

The same plant of 50 MWp, GTI 2050 kWh/m²/yr, every other input at its default
and no module file, from the two kinds of weather source:

| Weather | Year 1 (MWh) | PR (%) | Specific yield (kWh/kWp) | CUF on DC (%) | Correction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Twelve months from PVGIS | 87,111 | 81.76 | 1742.2 | 19.89 | 1.05 |
| Twelve months from an hourly file | 82,963 | 81.76 | 1659.3 | 18.94 | 1.0 |

The performance ratio is the same in both; the 5 % between the energies is the
correction.

## Degradation, worked from the shipped defaults

The table below is the output multiplier relative to the plant before any
degradation, at **1.0 %** first-year degradation and **0.4 %/yr** annual
degradation. The reported year-one energy is the second row.

| Year | Relation | Multiplier |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before degradation | — | 1.000 |
| 1 | 1 − 0.010 | 0.990 |
| 2 | 0.990 × 0.996 | 0.986 |
| 10 | 0.990 × 0.996⁹ | 0.955 |
| 30 | 0.990 × 0.996²⁹ | 0.881 |

On the shipped defaults, the final year of a 30-year plant generates about 88.1 % of what it
would have generated before any degradation at all.

See [Lifetime, degradation and P-values](/docs/energy/lifetime-and-p-values)
for how the same series is turned into the exceedance probabilities.

## Monthly figures

The month-by-month table follows **IEC 61724-1**, the standard for PV system
performance monitoring. Three quantities per month:

```
Y_r  = H_i / G_STC          reference yield, in hours
Y_f  = E_AC / P_0           final yield, in kWh/kWp
PR_m = Y_f / Y_r            monthly performance ratio
```

- `H_i` is the month's in-plane irradiation and `G_STC` the standard test
  condition irradiance, so `Y_r` is the number of equivalent full-sun hours in
  the month.
- `E_AC` is the month's AC energy and `P_0` the installed DC capacity, so `Y_f`
  is the month's specific yield.

Monthly PR moves with module temperature, and module temperature moves with the
month's ambient temperature and its own operating irradiance. Monthly ambient
temperature comes from an hourly temperature series when the run has one, which
a PVGIS hourly fetch or your file's temperature column can provide, and
otherwise from a seasonal model scaled by latitude. A hot low-latitude site
therefore shows its lowest monthly PR in the hottest months. The relations are on
[Temperature loss](/docs/energy/temperature).

## Where else the result appears

**The Summary view.** The **Plant** sheet carries the headline rows in its
**Capacity** group and the rest in an **Energy** group that appears once energy
is calculated. Before a run the Capacity group has none of these rows.

| Row | Group | What it holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **P50 energy, year 1 (MWh)** | Capacity | Year-one energy at the first exceedance probability |
| **CUF on DC (%)** | Capacity | The capacity factor on DC |
| **CUF on AC (%)** | Capacity | The capacity factor on AC, or the reason *needs an OND file* |
| **P75 energy, year 1 (MWh)** | Energy | Year-one energy at the second exceedance probability |
| **P90 energy, year 1 (MWh)** | Energy | Year-one energy at the third |
| **P50 energy, 25 years (MWh)** | Energy | The **lifetime** total: every year from 1 to **Plant lifetime**, summed, at the first probability |

The three probability rows are **named from your own settings**. They read
P50 / P75 / P90 because **Exceedance prob. 1**, **2** and **3** ship at 50.0,
75.0 and 90.0 %. Change any of them and the row name changes with it. Two
results are therefore only comparable row for row if both were run with the
same three probabilities: check the names, not the position.

The two lower probabilities are derived from the first through the combined
uncertainty:

```
P75 = P50 × (1 − 0.674 × σ)
P90 = P50 × (1 − 1.282 × σ)
```

`σ` is **Combined uncertainty (1σ)**, which ships at **5.0 %** and accepts
0.1–30.0 %. The multipliers are the standard normal deviates at those
exceedance levels.

<Callout type="warn">
**The last row's name is fixed text, and the figure is not a 25-year one.** The
row reads **P50 energy, 25 years (MWh)** whatever the lifetime is set to, while
the value beside it is the sum of every year from 1 to **Plant lifetime**,
scaled to the first exceedance probability. On the shipped default lifetime of
**30** years, the row named "25 years" therefore reports a **30-year** total.
Quote the figure as the lifetime total and state the lifetime you used alongside
it.
</Callout>

**The Workings view.** The **Energy** group of
[Workings](/docs/workspace/workings) sets out **Year-1 energy**, **CUF on DC**
and **CUF on AC** with their arithmetic and inputs, including the weather data
correction. **How?** beside a Summary row opens it at that figure.

**The plant chips.** The row under every view carries a **Year-1 P50 energy**
chip, `—` before a run, which opens the Energy view when clicked. The status
bar never carries it.

**The Yield tab's status line.** After a run it reports the performance ratio,
the year-one energy, both capacity factors and the lifetime total in one line.
The lifetime figure there is the same as the Summary row, under the same fixed
"25yr" label.

**The design report.** Section 9, **Energy**, of
[the design report](/docs/exports/design-report) in the deliverables package
holds the basis, the monthly table and the losses. The full Summary row set is on
[the Summary view reference](/docs/reference/summary-columns).

## What this model is and is not

State these plainly to anyone reviewing the output, because they are the
questions a careful reviewer asks.

**It is a yield estimate from a monthly loss model, not an hourly plant
simulation.** Each month's result is that month's irradiation multiplied by a
performance ratio and a capacity. Hourly data is used for the transposition from
horizontal to in-plane irradiance, for the shading loss, for monthly temperatures
when it carries them, and to spread the months over the chart and the export. The
energy figure is not an hour-by-hour simulation of module, inverter and
transformer behaviour, and it does not replace one.

**Row-to-row shading is modelled; terrain and horizon shading are not.**
The near-shading loss is computed from your own row geometry, and tables
falling inside the year-round shadow of a structure you place are cleared from
the layout. Distant horizon profiles and shading from the site's own relief are
not part of the model. See [Row-to-row shading](/docs/energy/shading).

**The result is only as good as the weather data behind it.** Every number on
this page is downstream of the irradiance. A monthly climatology average and a
purchased site-specific typical meteorological year will not give the same
answer. Check which source produced your irradiance
before quoting the yield: the source line on the Yield tab names it.
