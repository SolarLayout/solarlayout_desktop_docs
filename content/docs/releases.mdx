This page records what changed in each release — new inputs, changed defaults,
corrections, and anything that alters a result. Read it before you compare a new
run against an older one, or re-open a project you last generated months ago.

## Which version you have

Read the version from your own machine, never from a page like this one.

The application shows its version in the status bar at the bottom of the main
window — that is the quickest place to check, and it is the number to include
when you report a problem. The Microsoft Store's **Library** listing also
shows it, and is the place to check whether an update is waiting.

## How you get a new version

Updates arrive through the Store. Windows checks for them on its own schedule and
installs them in the background, so there is nothing to download and no installer
to run. Two consequences worth knowing:

- You can be moved to a new version without having asked for one.
- An update may wait until the application is closed. Close it if an update looks
  stuck.

You can also check by hand at any time, which is worth doing if you have been told
a correction is out and it has not reached you yet — the Store's own schedule is
not immediate. A copy that did not come from the Store receives no Store
updates; the download page,
[solarlayout.app/downloads/solarlayout](https://solarlayout.app/downloads/solarlayout),
always carries the current version. Your licence is not part of the package either, so an update never
asks you to activate again. Both are covered in
[Updates and reinstalling](/docs/install/updates).

## Entries

Each entry names a version and its release date, and the newest carries a
**Current** tag. A change to a default, a calculation or an export format is
called out inside the entry it shipped in, because those are the changes that
alter results between runs.

<Release version="2.1.0" date="2026-09-28" tag="current">

### New studies

- **Earthing Design** — **Tools ▸ Earthing Design…** or the **⚡ Earthing
  Design** button sizes the plant's earthing for the current layout: earth pits,
  strip and cable sizes, the plant grid resistance against your target, a
  calculation report, a BoQ, and PDF, DXF and CSV exports.
- **Robot count from DXF** — sizes the cleaning-robot fleet from any plant
  drawing, with no layout needed.
- **Simulation with AC Capacity** takes an **ICR block AC**; regenerating writes
  the matching block size into **ICR Block**.

### ICR blocks and electrical grouping

- **Click an ICR block** (with the **ICR blocks** layer on) to open its
  summary: capacity, tables, ACCBs, transformer, cable lengths, arresters and
  piles. **Export ICR-Block PDF** draws the plant and one sheet per block;
  **Export ICR-Block DXF** sheets carry the same summary tables.
- **The ACCB & Transformer card** on the Electrical tab sets how inverters are
  grouped into ACCBs and transformers, the standard transformer ratings, and the
  power transformer. The Summary gains **ACCBs**, **Transformers (IDT)** and
  **Power transformer (MVA)**; the automatic single-line diagram follows the
  card, the layout and the inverter file.

### Changes that alter results

- **ICR count** is decided per separate piece of land, and each piece is split
  into blocks of equal capacity; half tables count as half.
- **Cables card defaults:** **Inter-module lead** 0.00 m/module and **String
  return run along table** unticked.
- **Every inverter and SMB gets a cable.** Each goes to the nearest ICR along
  the plant that still has room within the building's rating; DC string runs
  keep to the usable area, and AC feeders and DC trunks are kept inside the
  plant wherever a route exists — any that are not show as design warnings. DC
  string cables are routed in full except on a very large plot, where the DC
  trench is marked as an estimate.
- **The cable schedule** sizes AC feeders as one 3C × 300 mm² aluminium run on
  the inverter's maximum AC current, steps a feeder up only when its current
  needs it, and adds a **Flag** column (a stepped-up feeder, or a drop over
  2.5 %), the plant's LV drop budget and a collection schedule.
- **Half tables and half trackers get their own pile pattern**, derived from the
  full one or your own, and every output counts each unit's own piles.
- **A CAD drawing is never placed at an assumed location.** The Site reference
  window starts empty: use the drawing's own coordinates, place it by latitude
  and longitude, or choose layout only.

### Working with results

- **Results say when they are out of date.** Change an input after a run and
  the layout, Summary, Energy view and block windows say so, and exports wait
  for **Regenerate Layout**.
- **Design warnings** — overloaded ICRs, long feeders, trenches outside the
  plant — show in the Summary, the PDF summary page and the BOM.
- **A project keeps every input**, and a reopened project's exports and
  drawing tools work without a new Generate.
- While cables are routed, the obstruction, MCR and object tools wait, and a
  change you make meanwhile is applied when the run finishes.

</Release>

<Release version="1.2.0" date="2026-09-21">

### A rebuilt interface

The window has been reorganised around the way a design is worked through.
Nothing about placement, routing or the energy model changed in this release;
what changed is where things are and what they are called. Older screenshots
and the in-app guide describe the previous arrangement.

- **The New design window** replaces the four start-up cards. Two segmented
  controls — **MOUNTING** and **INVERTER** — with a live summary card and one
  **Continue** button. The ribbon's **design chip** and **File ▸ Move to
  String / Central Window…** open a second window for a different design.
- **The ribbon** across the top carries the brand, the design chip, the access
  chip, and **Docs** and **Support** buttons.
- **Five stage tabs** — **Site · Array · Electrical · Yield · Tools** — replace
  the single long input panel, with **Generate Layout** (**F5**) pinned above
  them and a hint line saying why it is disabled. Every card is collapsible.
  The **Tools** tab, which holds obstructions, the main control room and the
  three studies, is locked until a layout exists.
- **Five views** of the plant on the right — **Layout · Summary · Energy · BOM
  · SLD** (**Ctrl+1** to **Ctrl+5**) — with the current view's tools beside the
  tabs. The **Summary** view shows the per-plant figures as a sheet turned on
  its side, grouped under SITE, ARRAY, ELECTRICAL, CABLES and ENERGY, with
  **Copy** and **Open in window**; the **Energy** view shows the yield result
  with tiles, the monthly table and charts.
- **The Layers popover** gathers the drawing's ON/OFF rows — plant layout,
  legend, arresters, ICR blocks, the three cable layers, contours and plant
  totals. The legend is folded behind a **Legend ▾** chip on the plot.
- **Plant chips** under every view carry the totals — DC capacity, site area,
  tables, modules, ICR blocks, arresters and year-one P50 energy — and open the
  Summary or Energy view when clicked.
- **Three navigation tools** — **Home**, **Pan** and **Zoom** — and the mouse
  wheel zooms at the cursor. The view you set persists across redraws until
  Home, a new layout or a new boundary file.
- **The Cables card** on the Electrical tab holds **Calculate cables** and
  three DC allowances: inter-module lead, the string return run and DC cable
  slack.
- **String sizing** opens from a **Size…** button beside **Modules per row** or
  **Modules per string (N–S)** — the Auto / Manual prompt after loading a
  module file is gone.
- **The Export menu** beside **Save project**: **Export KMZ**, **Export DXF**,
  **Export ICR-Block DXF** (one sheet per control-room block), **Export Cable
  Schedule (Excel)**, **Export Detailed Project Report**, and **Export Image
  (PNG)** for the plot. **Export PDF (with Piles)** appears while the pile
  overlay is on.
- **The report is now a Word document** — cover page, methodology, design
  basis, results, energy yield and the drawing annexures — in place of the
  PDF layout-and-summary export.
- **Rebuilt dialogs.** Site reference for CAD and image boundaries, Check
  boundaries, String sizing, Simulation with AC Capacity, Robotic module
  cleaning, Shadow View and the Pile layout editor follow one pattern: inputs
  on one side, the answer on the other as it updates, and one primary button
  that names its effect.

</Release>

<Release version="1.0.0" date="2026-08-18">

### The first documented release

This is the first release covered by this documentation. Rather than a list of
changes — there is no earlier documented version to compare against — it records
what the application does as it ships.

### Design

- **Four design modes**, chosen at every launch: fixed tilt or single-axis
  tracker, each with string inverters or central inverters and string monitoring
  boxes.
- **Three boundary inputs** — a Google Earth file, a CAD drawing, or a traced
  image. The Google Earth file is the only one that carries water bodies,
  obstructions and line corridors along with the boundary.
- **Automatic tilt and row pitch** from the site latitude, with the no-shading
  pitch at winter solstice, and an override for each.
- **Placement options** for maximising table count against boundary edges, and
  for filling leftover ground with half units.
- **Terrain-aware placement** that keeps tables off ground too steep or too
  uneven to build on, from satellite elevation data or your own contour file.
- **Keep-clear shadows** around control rooms, placed objects and perimeter
  street lights.

### Equipment and cables

- Inverter control rooms sized to a capacity block you set, placed by table
  grouping and movable by hand.
- Lightning arresters on an overlapping coverage grid.
- A main control room and, on multi-plot sites, a unit substation per plot.
- Routed DC, AC and medium-voltage cables with conductor and trench lengths
  reported separately.

### Analysis

- **Energy yield** from an automatic weather service or your own hourly file,
  with a loss-by-loss performance ratio, a computed thermal model, computed
  row-to-row shading, bifacial gain, degradation over the plant lifetime, and
  exceedance probabilities you choose.
- **Sizing from a target AC capacity** and DC/AC ratio.
- **Cleaning robot fleet sizing**, with per-gap bridging decisions.

### Drawing and output

- **Sketch Mode** for hand editing, with drawing, measuring and dimensioning
  tools, layers, groups and a typed command line.
- A **pile pattern** editor and construction pile report.
- A **single-line diagram** builder with a full switchgear symbol set, automatic
  build from the plant, and import of an existing diagram.
- An editable **bill of materials**, computed from the layout, with four
  built-in templates.
- Exports to a multi-page A3 PDF report, Google Earth, a layered CAD drawing, a
  spreadsheet, and an irradiance and energy time series.

</Release>
