title: Earthing design
description: A preliminary IEC earthing calculation for the generated layout — earth pits, GI strip and Cu cable sizes, the earthing plan, a report and a BoQ.
---

**Earthing Design** is a preliminary earthing calculation for the layout you
have generated. It works out how many earth pits the plant needs and where they
go, which GI strip or Cu cable size each earthing connection needs, whether the
plant grid meets its resistance target and the touch-voltage limit, and what
all of it adds up to as a bill of quantities. It draws the earthing plan over
the plant and exports the plan, the calculation and the quantities.

The design basis is IEC. IS 3043 and IEEE 80 figures are worked out alongside
as cross-checks and printed next to the IEC ones, so a reviewer working to
either can see both.

## Opening it

Two routes open the same window:

- **Tools ▸ Earthing Design…** on the menu bar. Before a layout exists it
  answers with **Generate first** — *"Generate a layout first, then open
  Earthing Design."*
- **⚡ Earthing Design**, the last of the five buttons in the **Studies** card
  on the **Tools** tab. It is greyed out until a layout exists.

The window works on every plot of the layout that holds tables; water plots and
plots with no tables are left out.

## The window

**Earthing Design** opens beside the main window rather than in front of it, so
you can keep it open while you work; it can be minimised and maximised. A
verdict banner runs across the top. The inputs sit on the left in a scrolling
column; the results sit on the right in three tabs, **Layout**, **Calculation**
and **BoQ**; the exports are in the footer.

<Screenshot id="earthing-layout" />

**There is no Run button.** The window calculates as it opens, and again a
moment after you stop changing an input — about a third of a second. Every
field applies to every design: fixed tilt or tracker, string or central
inverter. The system frequency is taken as 50 Hz.

## Inputs

Seven groups, in the order they appear.

### Soil & site

| Field | Default | Range | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Soil resistivity** (Ω·m) | 100.0 | 1–100,000 | The measured soil resistivity, from a Wenner four-pin test. Multiplied by the season factor, it gives the design resistivity used everywhere |
| **Season factor** | 1.50 | 1–5 | The dry-season multiplier, for a test done in the wet season |
| **Gravel resistivity** (Ω·m) | 3000 | 0–100,000 | The gravel or crushed-rock layer at the ICR and MCR yards — the surface layer in the touch and step limits |
| **Gravel thickness** (m) | 0.10 | 0–1 | Used in the IEEE 80 surface-layer factor |
| **Soil corrosion** | Medium | Low / Medium / High | Sets **Corrosion allowance** to 15, 25 or 40 % |
| **Corrosion allowance** (%) | 25 | 0–200 | Extra cross-section added to every GI strip for corrosion over the design life |
| **Design life** (years) | 25 | 1–60 | Printed in the report |

With the defaults the design resistivity is 150 Ω·m.

### Fault data

| Field | Default | Range | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **LV fault current** (kA) | 5.0 | 0.1–200 | The inverter AC-side earth fault. Sizes the array strip and the Cu cable |
| **LV fault time** (s) | 1.00 | 0.05–5 | Its duration, for the same sizing |
| **MV voltage** (kV) | 33.0 | 1–66 | Names the main-grid duty and appears in the report |
| **MV fault current** (kA) | 25.0 | 0.1–200 | Sizes the main-grid strip, and is the fault current for touch and step |
| **MV fault time** (s) | 1.00 | 0.05–5 | Its duration, for the main-grid strip sizing |
| **MV neutral** | Solidly earthed | Solidly earthed / Resistance earthed (NGR) | A report label. With an NGR, enter the NGR-limited current as **MV fault current** |
| **HV voltage (kV)** | 132 | 66 / 132 / 220 | Sets **HV fault current** to 31.5, 40 or 50 kA |
| **HV fault current** (kA) | 40.0 | 0.1–200 | The HV duty, for the report only |
| **HV fault time** (s) | 1.00 | 0.05–5 | The same |
| **Show HV in report** | off | — | The design stops at the MCR; when ticked, the HV switchyard strip size is shown in the report only |
| **Touch / step duration** (s) | 1.00 | 0.05–5 | The fault duration used for the touch and step voltages |
| **Split factor** | 1.00 | 0.05–1 | The share of the MV earth-fault current that flows into the grid |
| **X/R** | 10.0 | up to 60 | The system X/R ratio, for the decrement factor |

### Targets

| Field | Default | Range | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Whole plant** (Ω) | 1.00 | 0.05–50 | The plant grid target. Drives the verdict and the supplementary pits |
| **Each earth pit** (Ω) | 5.0 | 0.1–100 | The target for each equipment earthing point |
| **LA earth pit** (Ω) | 10.0 | 0.1–100 | The target for each lightning arrester's ring of pits |
| **Pit rule** | One pit per point, bonded to grid | or **Add pits until each point meets target** | How many pits each equipment point gets — see [Earth pits](#earth-pits) |

### Earth pit (maintenance-free)

| Field | Default | Range | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Electrode length** (m) | 3.0 | 1–60 | The length of each pit's electrode |
| **Rod diameter** (mm) | 17.2 | 5–100 | The Cu-bonded rod diameter |
| **Bore diameter** (mm) | 150 | 20–500 | The bore, filled with backfill compound |
| **Backfill resistivity** (Ω·m) | 0.20 | 0.01–50 | The backfill compound's resistivity |
| **Pit spacing** (m) | 6.0 | 1–50 | The spacing between pits; keep it at least twice the electrode length |
| **Strip depth** (m) | 0.60 | 0.3–3 | The GI strip burial depth |

### Conductors

| Field | Default | Range | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Initial temperature** (°C) | 30 | 0–60 | The conductor temperature before a fault |
| **Final temperature** (°C) | 300 | 150–500 | The highest temperature of buried galvanised steel during a fault |
| **GI strip sizes** (mm) | 25x3, 25x6, 32x6, 40x6, 50x6, 50x10, 50x12, 65x10, 75x12 | text | The strip sizes you can buy, as width x thickness |
| **Cu cable sizes** (mm²) | 4, 6, 10, 16, 25, 35, 50, 70, 95, 120 | text | The Cu earthing cable sizes you can buy |
| **Wastage** (%) | 5 | 0–50 | Added to every strip and cable total |

The size lists take commas or semicolons, `×` for `x`, in any case. An entry
that cannot be read turns the banner to **Check the size lists** — *"GI strip
sizes are width x thickness (e.g. 25x6, 50x10); Cu cable sizes are mm² (e.g.
16, 35)."* — greys out the three exports with *"Fix the inputs to export"*, and
leaves the results at the last calculation that worked.

| **Gates** | 1 | 0–20 | One pit per gate |

### Layout rules

| Field | Default | Range | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Strip in AC / MV trenches** | on | — | Lays strip along the MV and AC cable routes and any trenches you drew. When off, or with no MV route, an L-shaped MV backbone joins each ICR ring to the USS or MCR ring |
| **MMS link length** (m) | 2.0 | 0–20 | Strip per MMS table, for the riser and clamp — counted, not drawn |
| **Equipment link length** (m) | 5.0 | 0–50 | Strip per equipment body link |
| **Fence / pole link length** (m) | 3.0 | 0–50 | Strip per fence, CCTV and street-light pit |

| **Max cross-link length** (m) | 30 | 1–200 | The longest row-to-row cross link |

## What gets earthed

The network is built plot by plot:

- a GI **row runner** along every table row — east–west near the south edge on
  fixed tilt, north–south along the centre line on trackers — broken at gaps
  wider than **Row runner gap split**, with **cross links** between rows within
  **Max cross-link length**, and an **MMS link** allowance for every table;
- a **Cu cable** from each inverter or SMB to the nearest row runner;
- **strip along the MV and AC trenches**, when that option is on;
- a **ring** 1.5 m round each **ICR**, with pit groups at its corners and
  transformer-neutral pits south of it — the transformer count comes from the
  [electrical grouping](/docs/inputs/accb-and-transformers), or the fallback;
- **USS** and **MCR** rings with their pits, and the auxiliary-transformer
  pits; **weather-station** pits beside the MCR;
- a **ring of pits round each lightning arrester**;
- **fence**, **gate**, **CCTV** and **street-light** pits — local pits, not
  bonded to the grid and not drawn with a strip, each counted with its link
  allowance;
- L-shaped strips tying any isolated part of the network to the rest;
- on **trackers only**, a bonding jumper at each pile of the
  [pile pattern](/docs/editing/piles), or estimated from the bearing spacing
  when there is none.

Each strip and cable total is the drawn length plus the allowances, plus
**Wastage**. A site with several plots gets one network and one grid check per
plot; the banner reports the worst.

## Method and standards

| Step | Governing method | Cross-checks shown alongside |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conductor sizing | IEC 60364-5-54 Annex A, `S = I·√t / k`; minimum sizes from IEC 62561-2 / IEC 60364-5-54 Table 54.1 | IS 3043, IEEE 80 |
| One earth pit | Dwight formula with the backfill column (BS 7430 / EN 50522 practice) | IS 3043 |
| Plant grid resistance | EN 50522 meshed electrode, `R = ρ / (2D)` (IEC 61936-1) | IS 3043, IEEE 80 |
| Touch voltage | IEC 61936-1 / EN 50522, with IEC 60479-1 body data | IEEE 80 mesh and step voltages |
| Lightning arrester earthing | IEC 62305-3, type A arrangement | — |
| Module and MMS bonding | IEC 62548, IEC 60364-7-712 | — |

### Conductor sizes

Each connection is sized for its fault duty from `S = I·√t / k`, with k for
galvanised steel taken from the initial and final temperatures — 68.7 on the
defaults. The required area is the larger of that and 90 mm², plus the
corrosion allowance, and the strip chosen is the smallest in your list that
meets it **and is at least 3 mm thick**. There are four duties: **Main grid**
(the ICR and MCR rings, transformer bodies and neutrals, the HT panel, the MV
backbone), **Array field — LV** (row runners, cross links, MMS links, AC-trench
strip, central inverters), **Lightning arrester**, and **Fence, gates &
poles** — the last two at the minimum size. **Show HV in report** adds an HV
switchyard duty to the report only. On the defaults that gives
**50x10** for the main grid and **25x6** for the rest.

The Cu cable is sized from the LV fault with k = 143 and a 6 mm² minimum, and
carries no corrosion allowance — **35 mm²** on the defaults. If no listed size
is big enough for a duty, the report says so and asks for a larger size.

### Earth pits

One pit on the defaults comes to 32.45 Ω by the IEC method (34.87 Ω by IS
3043). The **Pit rule** decides what happens next:

- **One pit per point, bonded to grid** (the default): each equipment point
  gets one pit, bonded to the main grid, so the whole-plant value applies at
  every point. When one pit alone misses **Each earth pit**, the report notes
  it and the banner adds *"One pit alone is R Ω; bonded to the grid it takes the
  plant value."*
- **Add pits until each point meets target**: pits are added in a line at each
  point until it meets **Each earth pit** on its own — ten pits on the
  defaults.

Each arrester gets a ring of at least **Min. pits per LA**, grown until it meets
**LA earth pit** — five pits on the defaults. The report also gives the
electrode length one pit would need to meet its target alone, and warns when
the electrode is shorter than 2.5 m, the rod thinner than 15 mm, or the pit
spacing less than twice the electrode length.

### Plant grid and touch voltage

The IEC grid resistance depends **only on the plot's area and the design
resistivity**: adding strip or pits does not lower it. The IEEE 80 figure does
respond to the network, and the resistance carried forward is the larger of the
two. When the IS 3043 or IEEE 80 figure misses **Whole plant**, supplementary
pits are added along the array until both meet it. When the plot's area alone
puts the target out of reach, no pits are added and the report says so,
suggesting a grid extended outside the array (counterpoise) or soil treatment.

For touch and step, the grid current is the MV fault current × **Split
factor** × a decrement factor from **X/R** and the duration; the earth
potential rise is that current × the grid resistance. The IEC check passes when
the earth potential rise, or failing that the mesh touch voltage, is within the
permissible touch voltage — allowing for footwear and the gravel layer.

## Results

### The verdict banner

The banner reads *"n earth pits · plant grid R Ω (target ≤ T Ω)"*, with the
strip lengths by size and the Cu cable length under it. It is green when every
plot meets the **Whole plant** target and passes the IEC touch check, and amber
otherwise, adding *"The grid misses its IEC target or touch limit — see
Calculation."* Per-point and arrester targets do not change it; they are marked
in the report.

<Screenshot id="earthing-warn" />

### Layout

The earthing plan over the plant — boundary, tables, ICRs, the MCR and the
inverters in the background — with the strips coloured by duty (main grid red,
array and AC trench blue, arresters purple, fence and poles grey, Cu cable
green dashed), a marker for each kind of pit, and a legend with the count of
each. The plot toolbar zooms and pans it.

### Calculation

The calculation as a report, each check marked **✓ OK** or **! Not met**, in
numbered sections: **1. Design basis**, **2. Key inputs**, **3. Conductor
sizing (GI strip / Cu cable)**, **4. Earth pit design**, **5. Plant earth grid
resistance (Ω)** (one row per plot), **6. Touch & step voltage (MV earth
fault)**, **7. Where each earthing connection goes**, **8. Earth pit count**,
**9. Bill of quantities**, and **10. Notes** when there are any.

<Screenshot id="earthing-calculation" />

### BoQ

**S.No · Description · Qty · Unit · Remark**: the maintenance-free earth pit,
with the pit count by category in the remark; one row per GI strip size,
including wastage; the Cu earthing cable; the MMS earthing connections; the
serrated washers; the tracker bonding jumpers, on a tracker plant; and a test
joint for each arrester's down conductor.

## How it relates to the rest of the application

- **Its quantities stay in this window.** Earthing materials are not in the
  [bill of materials](/docs/bom/overview), the Summary, the **Export ▾** menu,
  the layout DXF, the KMZ or the Word report. Take them from the BoQ tab and
  its CSV.
- **Its inputs are not saved with the project.** They are remembered while the
  application window stays open — reopening Earthing Design brings back your
  last values — and **File ▸ New Project** starts them again at the defaults.
  Note the values you used, or keep the PDF, which prints the key inputs.
- **It works on the layout it was opened on.** An open window does not follow a
  later **Generate Layout** or an edit to the layout, and is not marked out of
  date. After changing the layout, open Earthing Design again.
