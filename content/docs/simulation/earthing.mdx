---

**Earthing Design** is the project's earthing design. It works out how many
earth pits the plant needs and where they go, which GI strip or Cu cable size
each earthing connection needs, whether the plant grid meets its resistance
target and the touch-voltage limit, and what all of it adds up to as a bill of
quantities. It draws the earthing plan over the plant and exports the plan, the
calculation and the quantities.

The design basis is IEC. IS 3043 and IEEE 80 figures are worked out alongside
as cross-checks and printed next to the IEC ones, so a reviewer working to
either can see both. The one place a cross-check enters the result is the plant
grid resistance, carried forward as the larger of the IEC and IEEE 80 values.

The design belongs to the project. Its inputs are saved with the project, and
the bill of materials, the plan, the deliverables package and the Layers popover
all read the design without the window being open.

## Opening it

Two routes open the same window, and both are greyed out until a layout exists:

- **Tools ▸ Earthing Design…** on the menu bar.
- **⚡ Earthing Design**, the last of the five buttons in the **Studies** card
  on the **Tools** tab.

<Screenshot id="tools-menu-locked" maxWidth={320} />

There is one window: opening it again brings the open one forward. It works on
every plot of the layout that holds tables; water plots and plots with no tables
are left out.

## The window

**Earthing Design** opens beside the main window rather than in front of it, so
you can keep it open while you work; it can be minimised and maximised. A
verdict banner runs across the top. The inputs sit on the left in a scrolling
column; the results sit on the right in three tabs, **Layout**, **Calculation**
and **BoQ**; the exports are in the footer.

<Screenshot id="earthing-layout" />

**There is no Run button.** The window calculates as it opens, and again about
a third of a second after you change an input. The system frequency is taken as
50 Hz.

**The window follows the layout.** When the layout is replaced, by a Generate,
opening a project, a cable refresh or a regroup, the window closes; open it
again for the new layout. Your inputs are kept. While the inputs of the run are
out of date the banner reads ***Out of date.*** *These figures are from the last
run, and n inputs have changed since. Regenerate the layout to update them.*, and
the exports are off with *"Inputs changed since the last run. Regenerate first."*

Editing an earthing input re-feeds the bill of materials and marks the project
changed. It does not make the layout
[out of date](/docs/workspace/out-of-date-results).

## Inputs

Eight groups, in the order they appear.

### Array earthing

| Field | Default | Options | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Rule** | **Jumpers and a ring** | **Jumpers and a ring** / **A strip along each row** | How the tables are bonded. See [What gets earthed](#what-gets-earthed) |

### Soil & site

| Field | Default | Range | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Soil resistivity** (Ω·m) | 50.0 | 0–100,000; 0 shows **Unknown** | The measured soil resistivity, from a Wenner four-pin test; 50 Ω·m is assumed until it is measured. Multiplied by the season factor, it gives the design resistivity |
| **Season factor** | 1.50 | 1–5 | The dry-season multiplier, for a test done in the wet season |
| **Gravel resistivity** (Ω·m) | 3000 | 0–100,000 | The gravel or crushed-rock layer at the ICR and MCR yards: the surface layer in the touch and step limits |
| **Gravel thickness** (m) | 0.10 | 0–1 | Used in the IEEE 80 surface-layer factor |
| **Soil corrosion** | Medium | Low / Medium / High | Sets **Corrosion allowance** to 15, 25 or 40 % |
| **Corrosion allowance** (%) | 25 | 0–200 | Extra cross-section added to every GI strip for corrosion over the design life |
| **Design life** (years) | 25 | 1–60 | Printed in the report |

With the defaults the design resistivity is 75 Ω·m.

**Unknown soil.** Set **Soil resistivity** to 0 and it reads **Unknown**: one pit
at each earthing point, nothing sized from the soil, and the grid not checked.
The banner reads *"n earth pits · soil resistivity unknown"* and asks for a
measured value.

## What gets earthed

The network is built plot by plot. **Rule** decides how the tables are bonded:

<Tabs items={["Jumpers and a ring", "A strip along each row"]}>
<Tab value="Jumpers and a ring">

The default.

- a **GI jumper** across each gap between neighbouring tables of a row, as long
  as the gap plus **Jumper: the gap +**. The tables' structure carries the bond
  between jumpers, and is not counted;
- a **break bridge** across a gap wider than **A break: a gap over**, if it stays
  inside the fence and is no longer than **Longest break bridged**. A longer one,
  or one that would leave the plot, splits the row;
- **straight strips** joining row ends row to row, at both ends;
- a **main ring** on the MV duty, **Ring inside the fence** inside each fence,
  with a pit every **Ring pit every**.

</Tab>
<Tab value="A strip along each row">

- a GI **row runner** along every table row, broken at gaps wider than **Row
  runner gap split**;
- **cross links** between rows, within **Max cross-link length**;
- an **MMS link** allowance for every table.

<Screenshot id="earthing-strip-along-rows" />

</Tab>
</Tabs>

Under either rule:

- a **Cu cable** from each inverter or SMB to the nearest array conductor;
- **strip along the MV, AC and hand-drawn trench routes**, when that option is on;
- a **ring** 1.5 m round each **ICR**, **USS** and **MCR**, with its pits spread
  on the ring;
- **IDT body and neutral pits** south of each ICR. The transformer count comes
  from the [electrical grouping](/docs/inputs/accb-and-transformers), or the
  fallback;
- **power transformer body pits** north of the MCR and **auxiliary neutral pits**
  south of it; **weather-station** pits on one plot;
- a **ring of pits round each lightning arrester**;
- a **fence strip** along the whole fence, joining its pits; **gate** pits on the
  first fence; **CCTV** and **street-light** pits;
- a string inverter's own pits, on string plants only;
- on **trackers**, a bonding jumper at each pile of the
  [pile pattern](/docs/editing/piles), or estimated from the bearing spacing
  when there is none.

Each strip and cable total is the drawn length plus the allowances, plus
**Wastage**. A site with several plots gets one network and one grid check per
plot; the banner reports the worst.

Pits are counted by category: ICR body, USS body, IDT body, IDT neutral, MCR
body, power transformer body, string inverter, main ring, aux transformer
neutral, lightning arrester, fence and gate, CCTV pole, street light pole,
weather station and supplementary (grid).

## Method and standards

| Step | Governing method | Cross-checks shown alongside |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conductor sizing | IEC 60364-5-54 Annex A, `S = I·√t / k`; minimum sizes from IEC 62561-2 / IEC 60364-5-54 Table 54.1 | IS 3043, IEEE 80 |
| One earth pit | Dwight formula with the backfill column (BS 7430 / EN 50522 practice) | IS 3043 |
| Plant grid resistance | EN 50522 meshed electrode, `R = ρ / (2D)` (IEC 61936-1) | IS 3043, IEEE 80 |
| Touch voltage | IEC 61936-1 / EN 50522, with IEC 60479-1 body data | IEEE 80 mesh and step voltages |
| Lightning arrester earthing | IEC 62305-3, type A arrangement | — |
| Module and MMS bonding | IEC 62548, IEC 60364-7-712 | — |

### Conductor sizes

Each connection is sized for its fault duty from `S = I·√t / k`, with k for
galvanised steel taken from the initial and final temperatures: 68.7 on the
defaults. The required area is the larger of that and 90 mm², plus the
corrosion allowance, and the strip chosen is the smallest in your list that
meets it **and is at least 3 mm thick**. There are four duties: **Main grid**
(the ICR and MCR rings, transformer bodies and neutrals, the HT panel, the MV
backbone), **Array field — LV** (row runners, cross links, MMS links, AC-trench
strip, central inverters), **Lightning arrester**, and **Fence, gates &
poles**, the last two at the minimum size. **Show HV in report** adds an HV
switchyard duty to the report only. On the defaults that gives **50x10** for the
main grid and **25x6** for the rest.

The Cu cable is sized from the LV fault with k = 143 and a 6 mm² minimum, and
carries no corrosion allowance: **35 mm²** on the defaults. If no listed size
is big enough for a duty, the report says so and asks for a larger size.

### Earth pits

One pit on the defaults comes to 16.24 Ω by the IEC method (17.44 Ω by IS 3043).
The **Pit rule** decides what happens next:

- **One pit per point, bonded to grid** (the default): each equipment point
  gets one pit, bonded to the main grid, so the whole-plant value applies at
  every point. When one pit alone misses **Each earth pit**, the report notes
  it and the banner adds *"One pit alone is R Ω; bonded to the grid it takes the
  plant value."*
- **Add pits until each point meets target**: pits are added in a line at each
  point until it meets **Each earth pit** on its own, up to 50 pits. Section 4 of
  the report states the count, as in *"1 → 16.24 Ω (! Not met vs 5 Ω); IS 3043
  needs 5"* on the default rule.

Each arrester gets a ring of at least **Pits per LA**, grown until it meets **LA
earth pit**: two pits on a ring come to 9.11 Ω on the defaults, within the 10 Ω
target. The report also gives the electrode length one pit would need to meet its
target alone, 13.3 m on the defaults, and warns when the electrode is shorter than
2.5 m, the rod thinner than 15 mm, or the pit spacing less than twice the electrode
length.

### Plant grid and touch voltage

The IEC grid resistance depends **only on the plot's area and the design
resistivity**: adding strip or pits does not lower it. The IEEE 80 figure does
respond to the network, and the resistance carried forward is the larger of the
two. When the IS 3043 or IEEE 80 figure misses **Whole plant**, supplementary
pits are added along the array until both meet it. When the plot's area alone
puts the target out of reach, the report says so, suggesting a grid extended
outside the array (counterpoise) or soil treatment.

For touch and step, the grid current is the MV fault current × **Split
factor** × a decrement factor from **X/R** and the duration; the earth
potential rise is that current × the grid resistance. The IEC check passes when
the earth potential rise, or failing that the mesh touch voltage, is within the
permissible touch voltage, allowing for footwear and the gravel layer.

## Results

### The verdict banner

With a soil resistivity set, the banner reads *"n earth pits · plant grid R Ω
(target ≤ T Ω)"*, with the strip lengths by size and the Cu cable length under it.
At the assumed value it adds *"Soil resistivity assumed at 50 Ω·m, not measured:
enter the measured value to confirm the grid."*

It turns amber, with an exclamation mark, when something needs your attention:

| Detail line | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| *"No listed size fits the &lt;duty&gt; — extend the size list."* | Add a larger size to **GI strip sizes** or **Cu cable sizes** |
| *"Each equipment point needs more than 50 pits to reach T Ω — treat the soil or lower the target."* | Treat the soil, or lower **Each earth pit** |
| *"The grid misses its IEC target or touch limit — see Calculation."* | Open **Calculation**. The touch limit can fail while the resistance target is met |

If a calculation fails on the inputs, the banner reads **Could not calculate with
these inputs** and names the fields to check. The plan and tables show the last
good result and the exports are off, with the reason *"Fix the inputs to export"*.

### Layout

The earthing plan over the plant, with the boundary, tables, ICRs, the MCR and the
inverters in the background. The strips are coloured by duty (main grid red, array
and AC trench blue, arresters purple, fence and poles grey, Cu cable green dashed),
with a marker for each kind of pit and a legend with the count of each. The plot
toolbar zooms and pans it.

### Calculation

The calculation as a report, each check marked **✓ OK** or **! Not met**, in
numbered sections: **1. Design basis**, **2. Key inputs**, **3. Conductor
sizing (GI strip / Cu cable)**, **4. Earth pit design**, **5. Plant earth grid
resistance (Ω)** (one row per plot), **6. Touch & step voltage (MV earth
fault)**, **7. Where each earthing connection goes**, **8. Earth pit count**,
**9. Bill of quantities**, and **10. Notes** when there are any.

<Screenshot id="earthing-calculation" />

### BoQ

**S.No · Description · Qty · Unit · Remark**: the maintenance-free earth pit,
with the pit count by category in the remark; one row per GI strip size,
including wastage; the Cu earthing cable; the jumpers and break bridges, or the
MMS earthing connections under **A strip along each row**; the serrated washers;
the tracker bonding jumpers, on a tracker plant; and the arrester test joints.

## Where the design goes

The design needs no opening of this window to reach the rest of the application.

| Place | What it carries |
| --- | --- |
| The project file | The inputs that differ from the defaults. **File ▸ New Project** starts them again at the defaults |
| **Layers ▾ ▸ Earthing** | The strips, jumpers and pits on the plan, off by default and in the **Cables** group. It follows your edits and hides with **Plant layout** |
| [Bill of materials](/docs/bom/overview) | Strip by class, jumpers, pits by kind and bolt sets, as measured lines. The trench strip drops out with **Calculate cables** off |
| **Export ▾ Earthing layout (DXF)…** | The earthing layout on its own, as `Earthing_Layout.dxf`. See [Exports](/docs/exports/overview) |
| [Drawing set](/docs/exports/drawing-set) | The D6 earthing sheets, and their layers in the DXF twin |
| [Design report](/docs/exports/design-report) | Section 8, **Earthing**: inputs, conductor sizing, the earth grid check and quantities |

<Screenshot id="earthing-on-plan" />

The Summary has no earthing row.

**The BOM and this window's BoQ can differ.** The BOM takes the inverters' Cu
earthing cable by its own rule, 15 m for each, and adds its own rule lines, while
the BoQ measures the design.

<Callout type="warn">
The design is worked from the soil resistivity and fault levels you enter. Until
a soil test confirms it, the grid check rests on an assumed 50 Ω·m. Confirm it
against a measured soil model and the actual fault study before it is issued for
construction.
</Callout>
