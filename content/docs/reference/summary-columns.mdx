The **Summary** view is the per-plant sheet turned on its side: a **Metric**
column on the left, **one column per plant** — one per plant boundary in your
file — and a **Total** column when there are two or more plants. The rows are
grouped under **SITE · ARRAY · ELECTRICAL · CABLES · ENERGY**. It is the second
of the five view tabs across the top of the right side (**Ctrl+2**), and before
a layout exists it reads *"Generate a layout to see the per-plant summary
here."* See [Views](/docs/workspace/views).

<Screenshot id="summary-with-energy" />

## How the view is laid out

- **Plant column headers** carry the plant's name followed by **›**. Clicking
  one shows that plant on the Layout view.
- **Total** appears only with two or more plants and sums every plant. See
  [Multi-plot sites](/docs/layout/multi-plot).
- Two tools sit right of the view tabs. **Copy** puts the whole sheet on the
  clipboard as tab-separated text (*"Copied. Paste into Excel or a
  document."*). **Open in window** opens **SolarLayout — Summary**, a separate
  window that follows the layout as it changes, with two tabs: **Summary** and
  **Monthly energy (Year 1)**, the second enabled once energy exists.
- The **Plant totals** row of the **Layers ▾** popover governs the row of
  plant chips under every view, not this sheet. Clicking any chip opens the
  Summary view; the energy chip opens the Energy view.

## The banners above the sheet

Two banners can sit above the figures. Neither appears when it does not apply.

**The out-of-date banner.** When any input has changed since the layout was
generated, the sheet still shows the last run's figures, greyed, under a
banner that names each change — for example **Out of date.** *These figures
are from the last run, and 2 inputs have changed since: Module wattage 610.0 Wp
→ 700.0 Wp, Soiling losses 2.0 % → 3.0 %. Regenerate the layout to update
them.* **Copy** is disabled meanwhile, with the tooltip *"Inputs changed since
the last run. Regenerate first."*, and the **Out of date** chip heads the plant
chips. Change the values back, or click **Regenerate Layout**, and the banner
goes. The **SolarLayout — Summary** window carries the same banner. See
[Out-of-date results](/docs/workspace/out-of-date-results).

**The design warnings banner.** When the last run's design falls outside a
design rule — a control room over its rating or not placed, a trench outside
the plant or under a table, a feeder over one run or over its voltage-drop
limit, the plant's LV drop over budget, a DC trunk too long — a warning-tone
banner titled *"1 design warning"* or *"2 design warnings"* reads *"The layout
is complete, but this falls outside the design rules. The PDF summary and the
BOM carry it too."*, then lists one line per warning, led by the plot name on a
multi-plot site. A **Design warnings** chip carrying the count sits at the head
of the plant chips, after the **Out of date** chip when both show. A warning
never blocks anything, and the banner clears when the design is clean again.
The trench, feeder and drop checks need **Calculate cables** on. What each
check means and how to clear it is on
[Design warnings](/docs/reference/design-warnings).

<Screenshot id="summary-design-warnings" />

The design warnings stay on the sheet, greyed, while the layout is out of
date. They are not in the **SolarLayout — Summary** window or in what **Copy**
puts on the clipboard.

## Reading the sheet

**A trailing asterisk means the value was calculated, not entered.** **Tilt**
and **Pitch** carry a `*` when the layout derived them from the site latitude
rather than taking an override from you. It appears on fixed tilt only. No
asterisk on those two rows means you supplied the number yourself — the fastest
way to check that an override you ticked took effect. See
[Spacing and tilt](/docs/inputs/spacing-and-tilt).

**A dash means not computed.** A `—` is not a zero and not an error. The
commonest case is the whole CABLES group when **Calculate cables** was left off:
the inverter and box counts are still computed, and every cable row reads `—`.
The ENERGY rows read `—` until energy has been calculated.

**Whole numbers of four or more digits get thousands separators** on screen.

## SITE

| Row | What it means | Reads — or 0 when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Plant area (acres)** | The plant's **gross** boundary area, taken before the perimeter road setback | Never — filled by the layout run |
| **Boundary length (m)** | The perimeter of the plant boundary | Never |
| **ICR blocks** | Inverter control rooms placed. The number needed is counted per separate piece of land: each piece's capacity ÷ **ICR Block**, rounded up. A room the plot needs but could not fit is reported as the design warning **ICR not placed** | Never |
| **Lightning arresters** | Arresters placed | 0 unless **Place Lightning Arresters** was on, or you added arresters in Sketch Mode |
| **Street lights** | Street light poles placed along the perimeter | 0 unless **Place street lights** was on |
| **Cleaning robots** | The fleet the cleaning study sized | Empty until **Robotic Module Cleaning** has been run |
| **Piles** | Each unit's own piles, summed: the full pattern on every full table or tracker, the half pattern on every half unit. Counted whether or not the pile overlay is on | `—` until a pile pattern exists |

**Plant area is the site's area, not the array's.** It is the area enclosed by
the whole boundary polygon, before the perimeter road setback and before any
obstruction or terrain exclusion — so it does not fall as exclusions grow. Add
an obstruction, tighten the slope limits or widen the perimeter road and the
capacity drops while this row stays exactly where it was.

**ICR blocks can exceed the plant capacity ÷ ICR Block.** Tables separated
from the rest by a gap of more than 300 m form a separate piece of land, and each piece gets its own
rooms however small it is. The count also follows the capacity left after the
rooms and the building-shadow keep-clear are cleared, so road width,
obstructions and the shadow window can change it. See
[Inverter control rooms](/docs/layout/icr); the per-block figures are in the
ICR block window, see [ICR blocks](/docs/layout/icr-blocks).

**Piles counts every unit's own pattern.** A half table carries the half
pattern — derived from the full one, or your own — so a plant with half-table
infill counts fewer piles there than on its full tables. See
[Piles](/docs/editing/piles).

## ARRAY

| Row | What it means | Reads — or 0 when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Full tables** / **Full trackers** | Full MMS tables, or full tracker units, placed | Never |
| **Half tables** / **Half trackers** | Half units placed | 0 unless **Add half tables** / **Add half trackers** was on, or you placed them in Sketch Mode |
| **Modules** | Modules placed across the plant, full units plus half units | Never |
| **Tilt (°)** / **Max tracking angle (°)** | The fixed tilt the layout used, or the tracker's rotation limit — a tracker has no single tilt. May carry a `*` | Never |
| **Pitch (m)** | The row spacing the layout used. May carry a `*` | Never |

The first word of each row follows the mounting type; the position in the
sheet does not move.

## ELECTRICAL

The inverter rows are a different set in each design, not the same rows
relabelled. The rows after them are common to both, except **ACCBs**, which a
central design does not have.

| --- | --- |
| **DC capacity (MWp)** | Plant DC capacity: the placed modules multiplied by the wattage |
| **String inverters** | String inverters placed |
| **Inverter rating (kWp)** | The DC capacity one string inverter carries |

<Tab value="Central inverter">

| Row | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **DC capacity (MWp)** | Plant DC capacity: the placed modules multiplied by the wattage |
| **SMBs** | String monitoring boxes placed |
| **SMB rating (kWp)** | The DC capacity one box carries |
| **Central inverters** | Central inverters, counted per ICR block: the block's boxes ÷ **Max SMB per Central Inverter**, rounded up, at least one and at most 4 in one control room building |
| **Central inverter rating (kWp)** | The box capacity multiplied by **Max SMB per Central Inverter** |

</Tab>

Then, in both designs:

| Row | What it means | Reads — when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **AC capacity (MWac)** | The AC capacity the plant is sized against: the target of a **Simulation with AC Capacity** when one was run, otherwise the inverter capacity: the inverter file's AC power rating × the inverter count | No inverter file has been loaded |
| **Inverter capacity (MW)** | The installed inverter capacity | No inverter file has been loaded |
| **DC / AC ratio** | DC capacity divided by AC capacity. The design overload limit is 1.5 for string inverters and 1.4 for central inverters | No inverter file has been loaded |
| **ACCBs** *(string design only)* | AC combiner boxes, from the **ACCB & Transformer** card: each ICR block's inverters ÷ **Inverters per ACCB, max**, rounded up | Before inverters are placed |
| **Transformers (IDT)** | The inverter duty transformers and their ratings, e.g. *"4 × 16,000 kVA"* or *"2 × 12,500 + 2 × 16,000 kVA"*. Without an inverter file only the count, *"4 · rating needs OND"*; a transformer whose load fits no listed rating reads *"4 · 1 with no listed rating"* | Before inverters are placed |
| **Power transformer (MVA)** | The whole site's power transformer at the main control room, e.g. *"63"* or *"2 × 80"*. Shown in the column of the plot that holds the MCR and in **Total**; *needs OND* when there is an MCR but no inverter file | No MCR is placed; in every other plot's column; before inverters are placed |

**Total** sums the ACCBs and the transformers. The three grouping rows follow
the **ACCB & Transformer** card at once — an edit there regroups the plant and
updates them without a Generate. See
[ACCB and transformers](/docs/inputs/accb-and-transformers).

Comparing a string design against a central design therefore means comparing
different rows. Read **DC capacity**, **ICR blocks**, **Transformers (IDT)**
and the CABLES group instead — those are common to both.

## CABLES

Six rows in metres, answering two different questions. The first three are
**conductor** to procure; the last three are **trench** to dig. Mixing them up
over-orders cable or under-digs trench.

| Row | What it means | Reads — when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **String DC cable (m)** | Conductor from the tables to the string inverter or the SMB, counted twice for the positive and negative conductors | **Calculate cables** was off |
| **AC cable to ICR (m)** *(string)* / **DC cable to central inverter (m)** *(central)* | AC from each inverter to the control room it is assigned to, counted once as a three-phase run — or the DC trunk from each box to the control room, counted twice. There is no AC leg in a central design, because the central inverter sits inside the building | **Calculate cables** was off |
| **MV cable (m)** | Medium-voltage conductor from the control rooms to the main control room | No MCR placed, or the layout not generated again with cables on since placing it |
| **DC trench (m)** | Trench length for the string DC cables | **Calculate cables** was off |
| **AC trench (m)** | Trench length for the second leg | **Calculate cables** was off |
| **MV trench (m)** | Trench length for the medium-voltage run | As **MV cable (m)** |

Each inverter is assigned to the nearest control room along the plant that
still has room within its rating, not merely the nearest building. How each
route is chosen is on [Cable routing](/docs/layout/cables).

A trench total is the de-duplicated path length per cable type, so a stretch
several cables share is counted once. The trench figures are therefore the
smaller ones, and the gap widens as the plant grows, because more cables share
each corridor near the control rooms; medium-voltage routes in particular merge
nearby control rooms into one trunk.

Because one second-leg figure is counted once and the other twice, the two are
not comparable as a cable quantity even on the same plant.

On a plot of more than 30 000 tables, the DC trench is measured on a drawn
sample of the DC cabling and scaled to every table. The bill of materials, the
PDF summary page and the Word report mark that figure *"(est.)"*.

<Callout type="warn">
Drawing an obstruction on the Tools tab — and **Undo Last**, **Clear All** or
dragging a control room — re-places the inverters and, with cables on,
re-measures **String DC cable (m)** and the second-leg row. The **trench** rows
keep their previous figures until the next full cable run: a **Generate**, or
leaving Sketch Mode after a change. Generate again before quoting trench
lengths.
</Callout>

## ENERGY

All five rows are empty until energy has been calculated, and every Generate
that replaces the layout clears them again. See
[How yield is calculated](/docs/energy/overview).

| Row | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **P50 energy, year 1 (MWh)** | Year-one energy at the first exceedance probability |
| **P75 energy, year 1 (MWh)** | Year-one energy at the second exceedance probability |
| **P90 energy, year 1 (MWh)** | Year-one energy at the third exceedance probability |
| **CUF (%)** | Capacity utilisation factor: year-one energy divided by capacity in kWp × 8760 hours, as a percentage |
| **P50 energy, 25 years (MWh)** | The lifetime total — see below |

**The three P rows are named from the exceedance probabilities you set**, not
fixed. On the shipped defaults of 50 %, 75 % and 90 % they read as above; change
any of the three fields under **Uncertainty & exceedance** and the row name
changes with it. Reading a sheet someone else produced, check the row names
before quoting a number.

**"P50 energy, 25 years" does not report 25 years.** The figure is the
cumulative energy over years 1 to **Plant lifetime**, after degradation, at the
**first** exceedance probability — and the row name does not follow the
lifetime setting. Plant lifetime ships at 30 years, so on shipped settings a row
named "25 years" carries a 30-year total, and raising or lowering the lifetime
moves the figure while the name stands still.

<Callout type="warn">
Do not quote the lifetime row by its name. Read **Plant lifetime** and the
first exceedance probability off the Yield tab and state both alongside the
figure, or the number will be attributed to a period it was never computed
over. How the figures are derived is on
[Lifetime and P-values](/docs/energy/lifetime-and-p-values).
</Callout>

## Where the same figures appear elsewhere

The sheet is not the only place these numbers surface, and the other places
carry a subset rather than a different calculation.

| Destination | What it carries |
| --- | --- |
| The plant chips under every view | DC capacity, site area, plants (multi-plot only), tables or trackers, modules, ICR blocks, lightning arresters (when placed) and year-one P50 energy, led by the **Out of date** and **Design warnings** chips when they apply. Water-body results are left out of the totals |
| The ICR block window | One block's figures — area, tables, modules, inverters, ACCBs, transformer and its load, capacities, DC/AC ratio, cable lengths, arresters and piles. Energy is not split per block |
| The Yield tab's status line | After a run: PR, year-one energy, CUF and the lifetime total |
| The Word report | The **Results & Analysis — Output** section and the **Design Summary** annexure, with its design warnings block; the energy sections only when energy was calculated. See [Project report](/docs/exports/project-report) |
| The cable schedule (Excel) | One sheet per ICR block: the DC strings, the AC feeders or DC trunks, and the collection runs from each combiner box or central inverter to its transformer and on to the main control room. See [The cable schedule](/docs/layout/cables#the-cable-schedule) |
| The TMY data CSV | The GHI, in-plane irradiance and energy time series the ENERGY rows are built from, at the interval you choose. See [Time series](/docs/energy/timeseries) |
| The Google Earth file | A summary placemark per plant carrying capacity in MWp, area in acres, pitch and table count, plus an **Overall Summary** folder aggregating every boundary. See [KMZ export](/docs/exports/kmz) |
| The bill of materials | Quantities computed from the same placed counts and the same grouping, including the cleaning fleet once you have asked for one, and a `Check:` remark on the rows a design warning qualifies. See [Bill of materials](/docs/bom/overview) |
