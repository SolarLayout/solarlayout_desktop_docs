title: CAD drawing export
description: The layered drawing export — every layer and what it carries, which coordinates it is written in, which layers are conditional, and the ICR-block drawing set beside it.
---

The CAD drawing export writes the plant as a layered drawing: the boundary,
the exclusions, the tables, the equipment, the cables, the trenches and your
annotations, each on a layer of its own. It is the deliverable for a drafting
or engineering team — the people who will take the layout apart, dimension it,
add their own details and issue construction drawings from it.

It is not a picture of the layout. The report's drawing page is that. This
file is geometry a drafter can work in, at true size, with everything
separable.

Beside it in the same menu sit an **ICR-block drawing set**, which breaks the
plant into one sheet per inverter control room and is covered at the end of
this page, and a **cable schedule** workbook. The earthing plan is a separate
drawing again, written from the **Earthing Design** window.

## Who it is for, and what they get

| Recipient | What the layers give them |
| --- | --- |
| A drafting team | Separable geometry to dimension, annotate and issue |
| A civil engineer | The boundary, the excluded ground and the contours, without the electrical clutter |
| An electrical engineer | Cable routes and trench routes, by type, without the structures |
| A survey or setting-out team | Real coordinates that can be checked against a survey |

Anyone who only needs to look at the layout is better served by the
[Google Earth export](/docs/exports/kmz) or the report — neither needs CAD
software.

## Coordinates are real, in metres

Every coordinate in the drawing is in **metres**, and nothing is scaled,
fitted or normalised on the way out. Which coordinates depends on how the site
was located when the boundary was loaded:

| Boundary | The drawing is written in |
| --- | --- |
| A Google Earth file (KMZ or KML) | The site's local **UTM** zone |
| A CAD drawing located by **Drawing's coordinates** | **UTM**, the same coordinates as the drawing you loaded |
| A CAD drawing or an image placed by **latitude and longitude** | **The drawing's own coordinates** — the layout is moved back so the export overlays the source drawing exactly |
| A CAD drawing or an image continued as **Layout only** | **The drawing's own coordinates** |

The plot's units note — *"UTM 42N · metres"* or *"Drawing coordinates ·
metres"* — tells you which applies before you export. How the location is
chosen is on [A CAD drawing as the boundary](/docs/inputs/cad-boundary).

Three consequences, all of them the point:

- **The drawing is at true size.** A distance measured in CAD is the distance
  on the ground, in metres. No drawing scale factor has to be applied or
  remembered.
- **It overlays its source.** A UTM export overlays survey data in the same
  zone with no fitting step; an export in the drawing's own coordinates drops
  straight back onto the CAD drawing it came from.
- **It can be set out from.** A coordinate read off a UTM drawing is a
  coordinate a setting-out crew can use.

<Callout type="info">
Check the coordinates before combining the drawing with anything else. Survey
data supplied in a different projection, in feet, or in a local site grid will
not overlay, and the mismatch is easy to miss because both drawings look
plausible on their own.
</Callout>

## The layers

Grouped below by what they belong to. The layer names are exactly as they
appear in the drawing's layer list.

<Screenshot id="dxf-rendered" caption="The exported file, drawn from its layers in a CAD viewer" />

### Site

| Layer | Carries | Colour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `BOUNDARY` | The plant boundaries | Yellow |
| `PERIMETER_ROAD` | The road setback band, as two outline polylines with no fill | Grey |
| `OBSTACLES` | Exclusion zones from the boundary file | Red |
| `WATER` | Water bodies | Light blue |
| `OBSTRUCTIONS` | Obstructions you drew yourself | Green |
| `TERRAIN` | Reduced-level contour lines | Grey |

The kept-clear ground arrives as separate layers rather than as one.
`OBSTACLES` and `WATER` are treated differently on site — a building or a
substation is an obstacle to be worked around, a pond is a water body with its
own consequences — and `OBSTRUCTIONS` holds what you drew by hand rather than
what the boundary file declared, so a recipient can tell the two apart. See
[Obstructions](/docs/layout/obstructions).

`PERIMETER_ROAD` is the band between the plant boundary and the usable area,
and it comes across as **two outline polylines with no fill** — the outer edge
and the inner edge. A drafter therefore receives the road reserve as
boundaries to work with rather than as a solid to delete, and the same band is
the one drawn on the plot and in the report. See
[How placement works](/docs/layout/how-placement-works).

### Array

| Layer | Carries | Colour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `TABLES` | Every placed module table or tracker unit | Blue |

One layer for the whole array. Switch it off and the site, the equipment and
the cabling are all still there — which is how a drafter works on anything
other than the modules.

**Tables are written as block references, not as individual outlines.** Every
table of a given size is an insert of one shared block definition, so editing
that block in your CAD program updates every table in the plant at once. The
block's own geometry sits on layer `0`, so each insert takes the properties of
the layer it is placed on, which is standard CAD practice. Tell the drafting
team: a change to the table detail is one edit, not tens of thousands.

### Equipment

| Layer | Carries | Colour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ICR` | The inverter control rooms | Cyan |
| `MCR` | The main control room | Violet |
| `INVERTERS`, or `SMB` in a central-inverter design | The string inverters, or the string monitoring boxes | Lime |
| `OBJECTS` | The objects you placed | Brown |
| `STREET_LIGHT` | The street-light poles | Orange |
| `LA` | The lightning arresters | Maroon — written only when arresters were placed |

The equipment layer name follows the inverter choice. In a central-inverter
design the layer is named `SMB` and carries the string monitoring boxes,
because the central inverters themselves sit inside the control room rather
than out among the tables. See [Inverter control rooms](/docs/layout/icr).

### Keep-clear shadows

| Layer | Carries | Colour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ICR_MCR_SHADOW` | The year-round keep-clear shadow of the control rooms | Grey |
| `OBJECT_SHADOW` | The keep-clear shadow of the placed objects | Grey |
| `STREET_LIGHT_SHADOW` | The keep-clear shadow of the street lights | Grey |

Three layers a recipient will otherwise mistake for stray hatching. Each one
is the ground a structure shadows across the shadow window — and with **Clear
tables in shadows** on, which is its shipped state, that is the ground the
tables were cleared from. The shadow layers are therefore the justification
for the gap in the array beside every building, object and pole. Keeping them
separate means a drafter can show that justification or switch it off, and a
civil engineer can see which cleared ground is shadow rather than obstruction.
See [Structures and shadow](/docs/inputs/structures-and-shadow).

### Cables

| Layer | Carries | Colour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `DC_CABLES` | DC string cables | Orange |
| `AC_CABLES` | AC cables from the inverters | Magenta |
| `MV_CABLES` | Medium-voltage cables, control room to main control room | Green |

Three layers rather than one, because the three are different cables with
different sizes, different burial requirements and usually different
contractors. All three are written only when cable calculation was on for the
run — see the conditional layers below.

### Trenches

| Layer | Carries | Colour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `DC_TRENCH` | DC trench routes, automatic and hand-drawn | Green |
| `AC_TRENCH` | AC trench routes, automatic and hand-drawn | Red |
| `MV_TRENCH` | Medium-voltage trench routes, automatic and hand-drawn | Dark green |

A trench and a cable are not the same length, and that is why they are on
separate layers. Several cables share one trench, so trench routes are the
de-duplicated routes on the ground while the cable layers carry the conductor
runs. The trench layers are what a civil contractor prices and excavates. See
[Cable routing](/docs/layout/cables).

### Drafting

| Layer | Carries | Colour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `SKETCH` | The dimensions drawn in Sketch Mode | White |
| `ANNOTATIONS` | The application's own labels: the plant name, **ICR-n**, the inverter or SMB numbers, **LA-n**, **MCR**, **USS** and the object labels | White |
| `0`, and any layer you added in Sketch Mode | Every other Sketch Mode annotation, on the layer it was drawn on | — |

## Layers that may be absent

Most of the layers above are written on every export, whether or not they
have anything in them. Three groups are conditional, and a layer that is not
in the file is usually a setting you chose rather than a fault in the export.

| Conditional layer | Written when | Colour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `DC_CABLES`, `AC_CABLES`, `MV_CABLES` | Cable calculation was **on** for the run | Orange, magenta, green |
| `LA` | Lightning arresters were placed | Maroon |
| `PILES` | A pile pattern exists **and** the pile overlay is on — the view header reads **Piles: ON** — when you export. A pattern alone is not enough | Orange |

Cable calculation and arresters are **off** by default, no pile pattern exists
until you define one, and the overlay is off until you switch it on. So a
first export, produced from a first run at the defaults, has no cable layers,
no arrester layer and no pile layer — and that is correct behaviour rather than
a missing export. The `PILES` layer draws a circle of the pile radius at every
pile with its easting and northing as text; each table carries its own pattern,
so half tables carry the half pattern. See [Piles](/docs/editing/piles).

If the recipient needs cable and trench routing, tick **Calculate cables** on
the Electrical tab and generate again before exporting. Cable calculation is
the slow step on a large plant, which is why it is off to begin with — the
recommended order is to review the layout first, then enable it for the final
run.

## Your annotations come across

Everything you drew in Sketch Mode — the lines, polylines, rectangles,
circles, arcs, ellipses, splines, fills and text — is written to a CAD layer
named after the Sketch Mode layer it was drawn on, in the colour you drew it
in. Annotations on the starting layer, *0*, land on CAD layer `0`; a layer you
added with **＋** in the contextual bar arrives as a CAD layer of the same name.
Dimensions go to the `SKETCH` layer.

That is worth using, and worth telling the recipient: markup drawn on layers of
its own is separable from the plant. They can switch those layers off and have
the plant with none of your commentary on it, or leave them on and read what
you were pointing out. Neither requires them to delete anything.

<Callout type="warn">
Layer `0` is the default layer of every CAD drawing, so markup left on the
starting layer mixes with anything else a recipient puts there. Add a Sketch
Mode layer for each set of markup you want kept apart — before you draw it —
and the layer structure carries the distinction across.
</Callout>

See [Annotations](/docs/editing/annotations) for the drawing tools themselves.

## Before you export

<Steps>
<Step>
### Turn cable calculation on, if routing is needed

Generate again with **Calculate cables** ticked. Without it there are no cable
layers, the trench layers hold nothing but what you drew by hand, and a
trenching estimate cannot be made from the drawing.
</Step>

<Step>
### Finish the layout edits after the last Generate

Hand-placed tables, deleted tables, drawn obstructions and moved control rooms
all export, so make them before you write the file rather than after.
Hand-drawn trenches export too, but a Generate routes every cable afresh and
drops them, so draw them after the last Generate. See
[Sketch Mode](/docs/editing/sketch-mode).
</Step>

<Step>
### Clear the annotations you do not want issued

Anything left in Sketch Mode goes into the file, on the layers it was drawn
on. It is separable, but it is still in the drawing. **🗑 Clear Annotations** removes
it.
</Step>

<Step>
### Switch the pile overlay on, if piles are needed

With a pile pattern defined, the view header's **Piles** button must read
**Piles: ON** for the `PILES` layer to be written.
</Step>

<Step>
### Export the drawing

Choose **Export ▾ ▸ Export DXF** — the **Export ▾** menu sits beside **Save
project** at the right of the view tabs. Choose the folder and the file name.
The status line confirms with *"DXF exported: …"*. The export needs active
access, see [Activating access](/docs/licence/activate), and a layout that is
up to date: if an input has changed since the last run, the menu reads
***Inputs changed since the last run.*** *Regenerate first.* and nothing
exports until you regenerate.
</Step>

<Step>
### Open it and check the layer list

Open the file and read its layer list against the tables above. This one
check catches the common surprises immediately — no cable layers because the
run had cables off, no arrester layer because arresters were off, no pile
layer because the overlay was off. All three are fixed before the export,
not by exporting again.
</Step>
</Steps>

## The ICR-block drawing set

**Export ▾ ▸ Export ICR-Block DXF** writes the plant as a drawing set rather
than one sheet. An ICR block is one inverter control room and the tables wired
to it through their inverters or string monitoring boxes — the blocks the
**ICR blocks** row of the Layers popover colours on the plot. The item is
available once inverters are placed. See [ICR blocks](/docs/layout/icr-blocks).

The model space is the whole **Export DXF** drawing described above, with each
block's tables and outline added on layers of their own. The paper space holds
A3 landscape sheets:

| Sheet | What it carries |
| --- | --- |
| **01 - Plant** | A viewport on the whole plant; the title *"ICR BLOCKS -"* followed by the plant names; a table of every block — area, tables or trackers, modules, DC capacity, inverters or SMBs, AC capacity and DC/AC ratio, with a total row when there are several blocks; and notes pointing to the block sheets and to the cable schedule |
| **02 - ICR-1**, **03 - ICR-2**… | One per block: a viewport on the block, a title of the block name, *"SUMMARY -"* and the plant name, the design (*"Fixed tilt - String inverter"* and so on), an **ITEM / VALUE** table of the block's figures — area, perimeter, tables, modules, DC capacity, inverters, ACCBs or central inverters, transformer and its load, capacities, DC/AC ratio, cable lengths, arresters, piles — and the block notes |

| Layer | Carries |
| --- | --- |
| `ICR1_TABLES`, `ICR2_TABLES`… | Each block's tables, in the block's colour |
| `ICR1_BND`, `ICR2_BND`… | Each block's outline and label |
| `SUMMARY_TABLE` | The tables on the sheets, as paper-space text |

- Each block sheet freezes the other blocks' layers, so a sheet shows its own
  block.
- The tables use CAD-safe characters: dashes become `-`, `×` becomes `x`, and
  `·` becomes a comma, so a transformer prints as `1 x 16,000 kVA, 4-winding`.
- On a site where more than one plot has control rooms, block names carry a
  plot prefix such as `P2-`.
- The **Piles** row and a `PILES` layer appear under the same condition as in
  the plain export: a pattern and the overlay on.
- The arrester layer follows the **Lightning arresters** row of the Layers
  popover.

Use it when the plant is going to be built or reviewed one block at a time,
with sheet 1 keeping the whole plant for orientation. The status line confirms
with *"ICR-block DXF exported (n block sheets + plant): …"*, or *"DXF exported
(no ICR blocks — whole plant only): …"* when the plant has no blocks. The same
set as a PDF is **Export ICR-Block PDF**.

## The cable schedule

**Export ▾ ▸ Export Cable Schedule (Excel)** writes a workbook with one sheet
per ICR block: the DC strings, the AC feeders or DC trunks, and the collection
runs to the transformers and the main control room, with recommended conductor
sizes and the basis they were computed on. It is not part of the drawing. What
each table carries and how the sizes are chosen is on
[The cable schedule](/docs/layout/cables#the-cable-schedule).

## The earthing drawing

The earthing plan — earth pits, GI strip and copper earthing cable — is not in
this export. **Export DXF…** in the **Earthing Design** window writes it as a
separate drawing, in the layout's own coordinates, with a reference layer for
the plant, a layer per strip size and duty, a layer per earthing-cable size and
a layer per pit category. See
[Earthing design](/docs/simulation/earthing).

## Limits

- **It is geometry, not a drawing set.** The plain export has no border, no
  title block, no drawing number and no revision block. Those are the
  recipient's, in their own template.
- **Nothing is dimensioned unless you dimensioned it.** The dimensions in the
  file are the ones you drew in Sketch Mode.
- **It carries no quantities.** The plain export has none, and the ICR-block
  set carries only its per-block tables. Take quantities from the
  [bill of materials](/docs/bom/overview) rather than counting entities in
  CAD.
- **The single-line diagram is exported separately.** It is not part of this
  file. See [Exporting the diagram](/docs/sld/export).
