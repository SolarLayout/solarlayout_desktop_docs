---
title: Main control room, substations and street lights
description: Placing the main control room and other structures from the Tools tab on a generated layout, and the perimeter street lights you enable before generating.
---

Some site equipment is not placed with the layout. The **MCR** — main control
room — and any generic objects are placed by clicking a position on the plot,
from the **Main Control Room & Objects** card of the **Tools** tab. Unit
substations appear on their own when a multi-plot site gets its main control
room. Street lights are a setting on the **Site** tab that you turn on before
you generate.

The inverter control rooms — ICRs — are different: they are counted from the
plant capacity and sited automatically. See
[Inverter control rooms](/docs/layout/icr).

| Equipment | How it arrives | When | Footprint set in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **MCR** — main control room | You click **Place MCR**, then click the plot | After a layout exists | **Structures & Shadow**, the **MCR** row |
| **Object** — anything else on the site | You click **Place Object**, then click the plot | As soon as the object has a size — a layout is not needed | **Structures & Shadow**, the **Object** row |
| **USS** — unit substation | Placed for you, one per plot, when the main control room is placed | Multi-plot sites only | **Structures & Shadow**, the **USS** row |
| **Street lights** | Placed with the layout | Enabled **before** generating | **Structures & Shadow**, the street light rows |

## The placement card

The **Tools** tab is locked until a layout exists — its tooltip reads
*"Generate a layout first"* and its buttons are greyed out. Once a layout is on
screen, the **Main Control Room & Objects** card offers **Place MCR**,
**Remove MCR**, **Place Object** and **Remove Objects**, with a hint line under
them that tells you what the next click on the plot will do.

## Placing the main control room

<Steps>
<Step>
### Click Place MCR

The button changes to **Cancel Placement** while it is armed, and the hint
reads *"Click on the plot to place the MCR."*, so you can always tell whether
the next click on the plot will drop a building. Click **Cancel Placement** to
disarm it without placing anything.
</Step>

<Step>
### Click the position on the plot

The main control room is placed at the point you click. One click places it and
the button returns to **Place MCR**.
</Step>

<Step>
### Keep it inside the boundary

A click outside the plant boundary is refused with the message
*"Please place inside the boundary."* Nothing is placed, and the button stays
armed so you can click again in the right place.
</Step>

<Step>
### Generate again to route the medium-voltage cables

As soon as the room is dropped the hint reads *"MCR placed. Enable 'Cable
Calc' and regenerate layout to route MV cables."* Placing the room gives the
medium-voltage network its destination; it does not route anything. Tick
**Calculate cables** on the **Cables** card of the Electrical tab if it is not
already on, and click **Generate Layout**.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Screenshot id="mcr-placed-regenerate-hint" />

After that run the status line adds *"| MV cable: n m"*, the **MV cables
(ICR→MCR)** row of the Layers popover has something to draw, and the **MV cable
(m)** row of the Summary view is filled in. **Remove MCR** is enabled while a
room exists.

<Screenshot id="mcr-placed-hint" />

**Remove MCR** takes the room away again. Place it once more wherever you want
it, then generate again to re-route the medium-voltage cables to the new
position.

### The footprint

The main control room's size comes from your own fields on the
**Structures & Shadow (keep-clear)** card of the Site tab, not from a fixed
value:

| Field | Default | Unit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **MCR** length (east–west) | 15.0 | m |
| **MCR** width (north–south) | 8.0 | m |
| **MCR** height | 5.0 | m |

Length is always the east–west dimension and width the north–south one, so a
building entered as 15 by 8 is wide across the rows, not along them. The height
drives the shadow footprint rather than the plan area. Set these before you
place the building; see
[Structures and shadow](/docs/inputs/structures-and-shadow) for the whole card,
including the **Clear tables in shadows** option, which is **on** by default and
removes tables that fall inside the building's year-round shadow.

## Why the main control room comes first

Medium-voltage routing runs **from every inverter control room to the main
control room**. Until the main control room exists there is no destination, so
**no medium-voltage cable is routed at all** — the **MV cable (m)** and **MV
trench (m)** rows of the Summary view read a dash even on a run with cable
calculation switched on.

<Callout type="warn">
Medium-voltage cables are routed on the **next Generate**, not when the room is
placed. Until you generate again with **Calculate cables** on, the MV row of the
Layers popover has nothing to show and the Summary's **MV cable (m)** reads
`—`, however long ago you dropped the room.
</Callout>

The order that works is therefore:

1. Generate the layout.
2. Place the main control room.
3. With **Calculate cables** ticked, generate again.
4. Read the medium-voltage figures.

Place it where the plant's export actually leaves the site — usually near the
point of interconnection — because the trunk network is built to reach it and
its position sets the whole medium-voltage cable length. Moving it changes that
length. See [Cable routing and trenches](/docs/layout/cables) for how the trunk
is formed and what the reported lengths mean.

## Placing generic objects

An object is anything on the site that keeps modules off the ground around it
and that your boundary file does not carry: a tree, a pole, a tank, an existing
shed, a structure the survey missed.

- **Place Object** arms placement in the same way as **Place MCR** — the button
  reads **Cancel Placement** while armed, and a click outside the boundary is
  refused with the same message.
- **Place Object** is the one button on the card that does not wait for a
  layout: it is enabled as soon as the **Object** row has a length, a width and
  a height, so you can mark a tree or a tank before the first run and let the
  layout be built around it.
- **Remove Objects** clears the objects you have placed.

Their size and height come from the **Object** row of the
**Structures & Shadow** card:

| Field | Default | Unit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Object** length (east–west) | 0.0 | m |
| **Object** width (north–south) | 0.0 | m |
| **Object** height | 0.0 | m |

<Callout type="warn">
All three object fields ship at **0.0**, which means *not configured*. Fill in a
length, a width and a height before you place an object, or you are placing
something with no extent and no shadow.
</Callout>

The point of an object is the **shadow**, not the footprint. With
**Clear tables in shadows** on, the year-round shadow of each object across
the shadow window clears the tables it falls on — which is exactly what you want
for a tree that no boundary file will ever describe. The height is what makes
that shadow long, so it is the field to get right.

## Unit substations on a multi-plot site

A **USS** — unit substation — is placed for you, and only on a site whose
boundary file contains more than one plant boundary.

On a multi-plot site the hint under **Place MCR** changes to *"n plots — click
INSIDE each plot to drop a USS (Unit Substation). The LAST unassigned plot you
click gets the MCR. Each plot's ICRs route to its own USS/MCR."* Click inside
each plot in turn: every plot you click receives a unit substation, and the
last plot you click receives the main control room instead. Each plot's
inverter control rooms then route their medium-voltage cables to the building
in their own plot instead of running across country to the main control room.

| Field | Default | Unit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **USS** length (east–west) | 5.0 | m |
| **USS** width (north–south) | 4.0 | m |
| **USS** height | 5.0 | m |

The link from a unit substation onward to the main control room is an overhead
line or a buried cable, and it is **not** part of the automatic medium-voltage
routing — plan and cost it outside the application. See
[Multi-plot sites](/docs/layout/multi-plot) for the full behaviour, including
how each plot gets its own column in the Summary view.

## Street lights

Street lights are perimeter lighting: a line of poles set close inside the
fence, right around the plant.

They are **off** by default and, unlike the main control room, they are **not**
placed by clicking. Tick **Place street lights** on the **Structures & Shadow**
card of the Site tab **before** you click **Generate Layout**; a layout already
on screen does not gain them.

### What placement does

- Poles are spaced along the plant perimeter at the pole-to-pole span.
- Each pole sits inside the fence, inset by the street light setback.
- Any module table the pole's shadow touches is cleared, so lighting costs a
  little capacity around the edge of the plant.

The number of poles is approximately the **perimeter divided by the span**, so a
long, irregular boundary carries more lights than a compact one of the same
area.

### The street light settings

All of them are under the *— Street Light —* divider of the
**Structures & Shadow** card:

| Field | Default | Range | Unit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pile Ø | 0.30 | 0.0–5.0 | m |
| Height | 6.0 | 0.0–50.0 | m |
| Span | 40.0 | 1.0–500.0 | m |
| Setback | 0.20 | 0.0–50.0 | m |
| **Place street lights** | off | — | — |

What each one changes:

- **Span** sets the pole count. Widen it for fewer poles and less cleared
  ground; narrow it for more of both.
- **Setback** decides how far inside the fence the poles stand. It is small by
  default, which keeps the poles in the perimeter road band rather than out
  among the tables.
- **Height** sets how far the pole's shadow reaches, and therefore how many
  tables it clears.
- **Pile Ø** describes the foundation.

None of these settings is kept in a project file; a reopened project shows the
defaults.

### Reading the result

The Summary view reports the pole count in the **Street lights** row, one
column per plant with a Total column on a multi-plot site. The module and
capacity rows already account for the tables the lights cleared.

## If a placement does not do what you expect

<Accordions>
<Accordion title="The Tools tab is greyed out">
No layout exists yet. The tab's tooltip reads *"Generate a layout first"*.
Load a boundary file and click **Generate Layout**; only **Place Object** works
before that, and only once the object has a size.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Clicking the plot places nothing">
The tool is not armed. While placement is armed the button reads
**Cancel Placement**; if it reads **Place MCR** or **Place Object**, the next
click on the plot is an ordinary click. Arm it first.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Please place inside the boundary.">
The point you clicked is outside the plant boundary. Nothing is placed and the
tool stays armed — click again inside the boundary.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="An object was placed but nothing changed">
The **Object** length, width and height all ship at **0.0**, which means *not
configured*. An object with no extent and no height has no footprint to clear and
casts no shadow. Enter its dimensions on the **Structures & Shadow** card, then
place it.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Street lights did not appear">
They are not placed by clicking. Tick **Place street lights** on the Site tab
and click **Generate Layout** again — an existing layout does not gain them.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="The medium-voltage rows still read a dash">
Three things are needed together: the main control room must be placed,
**Calculate cables** must be ticked, and the layout must have been generated
**after** the room was placed. Cable calculation is off by default, and placing
the room routes nothing on its own.
</Accordion>
</Accordions>

## Where the structures end up in the outputs

- **On the plot.** The main control room, the unit substations, objects and
  street light poles are drawn with the rest of the layout; the main control
  room is violet.
- **In the CAD drawing.** The main control room is written to its own `MCR`
  layer, objects to `OBJECTS` and street light poles to `STREET_LIGHT`, each
  with its keep-clear shadow on a layer of its own. See
  [CAD export](/docs/exports/dxf). Street lights are not written to the Google
  Earth file.
- **In the bill of materials.** The automated list is computed from the layout,
  so use **⚙ Rebuild from Layout** after placing or removing equipment. See
  [Bill of materials](/docs/bom/overview).
