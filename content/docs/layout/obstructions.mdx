title: Obstructions
description: Every kind of ground kept clear of modules — water, buildings, line corridors, terrain, shadows — and how to draw an exclusion by hand from the Tools tab or in Sketch Mode.
---

An obstruction is **area subtracted from the usable area**. Whatever the reason
for it — a pond, a building, a transmission line corridor, steep ground, a
building's shadow, or a rectangle you drew yourself — the effect on the layout is
identical: the ground stops being available, and **any module table overlapping
it is removed**.

That single rule is worth stating plainly because it makes the plot readable.
Wherever you see a gap in the table grid, something was subtracted from the
usable area there. This page collects every source of those gaps in one place.

## How the usable area is built

The order matters, because each step works on the result of the last.

1. The plant boundary is **shrunk inward by the perimeter road width**. What is
   left is the usable area.
2. Polygon obstructions and water bodies are **subtracted** from it.
3. Line features — transmission lines, canals, roads — are buffered into
   corridors by the line corridor on each side, and those corridors are
   subtracted too.
4. Terrain exclusions are subtracted, when topography is enabled.
5. Tables and tracker units are then placed only where they fit **entirely**
   inside what remains.

Because a table must fit entirely inside the usable area, an obstruction clears
slightly more ground than its own outline: a table that merely clips the edge of
a pond is dropped in full. See
[How placement works](/docs/layout/how-placement-works).

## Obstructions that come from the boundary file

A Google Earth file carries its exclusions with it, so a well-prepared file
needs no hand drawing at all. Three kinds are read automatically:

| Kind | Drawn in the file as | What the application does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Water body** | A closed polygon inside the plant boundary, named with a water word | Subtracted from the usable area with its **Area setback**, drawn blue, and exported as water |
| **Generic obstruction** | A closed polygon inside the plant boundary, any other name | Subtracted from the usable area with its **Area setback**, drawn red, and exported as an exclusion |
| **Line corridor** | A line — not a polygon — for a transmission line, canal or road | Buffered into a corridor and subtracted: the line corridor on **each** side |

Draw every feature **inside** a plant boundary. A polygon drawn clear of every
boundary is read as another plot, and tables are placed on it. See
[Boundary file requirements](/docs/inputs/kmz-requirements) for how each feature
must be named and drawn.

### Water is set by name

The shape decides whether a feature is an area or a line. Its **name** sets
whether it starts as water: a whole word for a pond, lake, reservoir, wetland,
canal, drain, nala or river, in any case. You can change it with the **Water**
tick in the **Clean boundary** review.

The consequence to know:

<Callout type="warn">
A water body whose name carries no water word is treated as a **generic
obstruction**. It still clears the tables over it, so the layout is not wrong —
but it is drawn in the obstruction colour instead of blue, and it is exported on
the obstruction layer instead of the water layer. Name your ponds.
</Callout>

That is the whole cost of a misnamed pond: presentation and the export, not
placement. It is still worth fixing, because the drawing goes to a contractor
who will read a red polygon as a building and a blue one as water.

## Line corridors and their setback

A line feature has no width, so it cannot exclude anything on its own. Every
line is buffered on **both** sides by its corridor, which means the strip kept
clear of modules is **twice** the value you enter. For a road, enter half its
width. Each line in the file has its own row on the Site tab, so a road and a
transmission line can differ.

| Field | Default | Range | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Line corridor (each side)** | 15.0 m, 30 m total | 0.0–500.0 | The distance kept clear each side of a line feature |
| **Area setback** | 0.0 m | 0.0–100.0 | The distance kept clear around an area in the boundary file |

The setback that suits a distribution line will not suit an export line. The
application's own guidance is to raise it for higher voltages — for example 30 m
per side on a 400 kV line. Set it on the **Site Parameters** card of the Site
tab; see [Site parameters](/docs/inputs/site).

The corridor is subtracted from the usable area exactly like a polygon, so the
tables stop short of it on both sides and you get the clean service strip a line
crossing actually needs.

## Steep and unsuitable ground

Terrain exclusion is the same subtraction driven by elevation instead of by a
drawn shape: ground steeper than your north–south and east–west slope limits,
ground whose relief within a single table footprint exceeds the allowed
variation, and — when you ask for it — depressions where water would pool.

It is **off** by default, and turning it on needs either a contour file or an
internet connection for the satellite elevation model. The excluded area is
reported in acres. See [Topography](/docs/inputs/topography).

## Shadows of structures

The year-round shadow of a building or an object is also treated as ground to
keep clear. With **Clear tables in shadows** on — which is the default — any
table falling inside the shadow footprint of an inverter control room, the main
control room, a unit substation or a placed object across the shadow window is
removed. **Shadows (keep-clear)** in the **Layers** popover shows that ground as a
grey hatch, and showing it changes nothing in the layout.

This is how you exclude a tree, a mast or an existing shed: place an object, give it
a realistic height in the **Place object** window, and let its shadow clear the
modules. See
[Structures and shadow](/docs/inputs/structures-and-shadow) and
[Main control room, substations and street lights](/docs/layout/site-equipment).

Lightning arresters clear the tables touched by the mast's pile or by its shadow,
on fixed tilt and on trackers alike. See
[Lightning arresters](/docs/layout/lightning-arresters).

## Drawing an obstruction by hand

Use this when the boundary file did not carry an exclusion, when a site visit
found something the survey missed, or when you want to reserve ground for
something the application does not model — a laydown area, a construction
compound, a future extension.

The **Obstructions** card is the first card of the **Tools** tab. Its buttons
are greyed out until a layout exists — the tools act on the layout already on
screen.

<Screenshot id="obstruction-controls" />

| Button | What it does | Hint while armed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Draw Rectangle** | Arms the rectangle tool | *"Click and drag on the plot to draw a rectangular obstruction."* |
| **Draw Polygon** | Arms the polygon tool | *"Click to add vertices. Double-click or right-click to close."* |
| **Undo Last** | Removes the most recently drawn obstruction | — |
| **Clear All** | Removes every hand-drawn obstruction | — |

<Screenshot id="obstruction-controls-armed" />

The application calls all of these **Obstruction**, whatever the ground is
actually for. Tables overlapping a hand-drawn obstruction are removed the same
way as for one read from the file, and the status line reports the cost at
once: *"Obstruction added — n tables | m modules | x MWp"*. After **Clear All**
the status line reads *"All roads cleared."*

Each draw, **Undo Last** and **Clear All** places the inverters again and, with
**Calculate cables** on, routes their cables again and recomputes the DC, AC and MV
trench totals on that run. If that re-route would restore automatic trenches you
deleted, the [Re-route cables](/docs/editing/sketch-mode#the-re-route-cables-question)
question comes first. The bill of materials recounts when next opened.

One cable run happens at a
time: while it runs, the **Obstructions** and **Grid connection & Objects**
cards are disabled, and their tooltip and the status bar read *"Cables are
being routed… Obstruction, MCR and object tools and ICR dragging are back when
they finish."* A change you make meanwhile waits, and runs as soon as the
current routing finishes.

<Screenshot id="obstruction-controls-routing" maxWidth={360} caption="The card while cables are being routed" />

### The obstruction tools in Sketch Mode

Sketch Mode's **PLANT OBJECTS** group carries two corridor tools, drawn the same
way: drag a straight **centre-line** on the plot, then enter a width. They treat the
tables differently.

| Tool | Prompt | Default width | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **🚧 Road** | **Road width** | 5.0 m | Places a road and moves the rows on each side apart to make room; a road at a slant to the rows removes the tables under it. See [Roads move rows](/docs/editing/sketch-mode#roads-move-rows) |
| **⚡ T-Line** | **Transmission line corridor** | 10.0 m | Draws the red centre-line, records a corridor of that width as a keep-out and removes the tables inside it |

The **DRAW** group's **╱ Line** and **⌒ Polyline** ask for a **Line width** as
well: **0** draws a line only, and anything more than 0 makes it a corridor that
removes the tables under it — a way to clear an irregular strip.

A hand-drawn transmission line **does not use the Line corridor** on the Site
tab. The width you enter is the whole corridor: the centre-line is
buffered by half of it on each side, so the strip cleared of tables is the
width you gave, not twice it. So a line you draw and a line of the same route
read from the boundary file clear different ground unless you convert one figure
into the other. To match a 15 m setback each side, enter a corridor width of
30 m.

### What Generate does to hand-drawn obstructions

Hand-drawn obstructions, roads, corridors and drawn trenches **survive** a later
**Generate Layout**. Obstructions and corridors are handed to the placement as
ground to keep clear, so the plant is rebuilt around them. What does *not* survive is
any table you placed or deleted by hand in Sketch Mode — Generate re-places every
table from scratch. Draw the obstructions first, generate, and hand-edit
individual tables last. The full rule is on
[Sketch Mode](/docs/editing/sketch-mode#what-generate-layout-does-to-your-edits).

## What else the usable area governs

Subtracting an obstruction does more than remove tables. The same usable area is
the test every other placement decision is made against, which is why a large
exclusion changes the plant in ways that are not obvious from the table grid
alone:

- **Cable routes.** DC string runs are clipped to the usable area, so they stay
  off the ground an exclusion takes. See
  [Cable routing and trenches](/docs/layout/cables).
- **Lightning arrester positions.** A grid point is kept only if its place in the
  nearest gap falls inside the usable area, so an obstruction thins the arrester
  grid locally, and the coverage is completed afterwards with extra masts.
- **Control room siting.** A control room must be **fully contained** in the
  usable area, so an exclusion can push a room away from the centre of the group
  of tables it serves.
- **Reported area.** Excluded terrain is reported in acres, so you can see how
  much ground the slope limits took. The **Plant area (acres)** row of the Summary
  view is the gross boundary area, so it does not fall as exclusions grow. The land
  account in the [Workings view](/docs/workspace/workings) books each kind of
  exclusion.

## What reaches the deliverables

The [deliverables package](/docs/exports/deliverables) carries the exclusions from one
record of the design, so the drawings and the Google Earth file agree.

| Output | Where the exclusions are |
| --- | --- |
| The CAD twin of the [drawing set](/docs/exports/drawing-set) | Layers `C-SITE-EXCL-WATR` for water, `C-SITE-EXCL-TLCR` for line corridors, and `C-SITE-EXCL-OBST` for obstacles and hand-drawn obstructions |
| The [Google Earth file](/docs/exports/schedules-and-kmz) | The **Exclusions** folder: Water, Obstacle, Transmission-line corridor, Road, Steep ground and Drawn obstruction, those present |

## Checking your exclusions before you trust the layout

<Steps>
<Step>
### Generate and look at the gaps

Every gap in the table grid should correspond to something you can name. A gap
you cannot account for usually means a feature was classified as something you
did not intend.
</Step>

<Step>
### Switch Satellite on

**Satellite** in the Layout view's tools puts the aerial photograph behind the
layout and shows the real ground. A pond visible on the imagery with tables
sitting on it means the polygon is missing from the file or was drawn outside
the boundary.
</Step>

<Step>
### Fix the file, not the layout

A missing exclusion is best fixed by naming and drawing it correctly in the
boundary file, then generating again. Hand-drawn obstructions are for what the
file genuinely cannot carry.
</Step>
</Steps>
