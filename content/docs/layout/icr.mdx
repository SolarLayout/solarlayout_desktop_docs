---
title: Inverter control rooms
description: What an inverter control room is, how many the plant gets, where they are placed, the tables they clear, and how to move one by hand.
---

An **inverter control room (ICR)** is the building where the plant's collection
cables terminate and its medium-voltage export begins. In a string-inverter
plant the inverters stand out in the field and run their AC cables into it. In a
central-inverter plant the central inverters themselves stand inside it.

The application places the rooms for you. It works out how many the plant needs
from the capacity block you set, divides the tables into that many groups,
places a room at the centre of each and clears the tables it stands on. You can
then move any room by hand if the ground does not suit it. The tables finally
wired to each room form its **ICR block**.

## The footprint

A control room is drawn and cleared as a rectangle with a height, and all three
dimensions are yours to set on the **Structures & Shadow (keep-clear)** card of
the Site tab.

| Dimension | Default | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Length | 10.0 m | The building's east–west extent |
| Width | 4.0 m | The building's north–south extent |
| Height | 5.0 m | Drives the shadow the building casts |

Length is always the east–west side and width the north–south side, for the
control room and for every other structure on that card. Enter the building
you are actually going to build: the length and width are what the placement
has to find room for and what clears tables, and the height is what casts the
shadow. See [Structures and shadow](/docs/inputs/structures-and-shadow).

## How many the plant gets

The count comes from one field. **ICR Block** on
[Site parameters](/docs/inputs/site) is the DC capacity one control room
serves, and defaults to **18.000 MWp** with a range of 0.1 to 500.

The count is worked out **for each separate piece of land**, not for the plant
as a whole:

1. **The tables are split into separate pieces of land.** Two tables are on the
   same piece when a chain of tables links them with no gap in the chain wider
   than **300 m**. Internal roads, corridors and setbacks are far narrower than
   that, so they never split a piece; a plant split by a wide water body or an
   excluded valley can be several pieces.
2. **Each piece gets its own rooms**: the piece's capacity divided by the ICR
   Block, rounded **up**. A half table counts as half a table's capacity here.
3. **The plant's count is the sum** over its pieces.

On a site in one piece that is simply the plant's capacity divided by the block
size, rounded up. On a site in several distant pieces it can be more, because
each small far-off piece gets a room of its own rather than being cabled
hundreds of metres to a neighbour's.

Rounding up means a plant — or a piece of land — that overshoots a block
boundary by a small margin gets a whole extra room. The capacity is then shared
**equally** between the rooms of that piece, so every block becomes a little
smaller; there is no small left-over block. It is worth checking the count
against your own single-line concept before you accept it.

You cannot set the count directly. It follows the capacity that is actually
placed — so the road width, obstructions and the building-shadow keep-clear can
all change it — and how the land splits into pieces.

Set the block size to the capacity your transformer and switchgear design
actually serves. It is the single most consequential number on this page: it
sets how many buildings the plant carries, how the tables are grouped around
them, how far the AC runs are, and how many medium-voltage circuits leave the
plant.

## Where they go

The rooms are placed after the table grid, once the plant's capacity is known,
and their positions come from where the tables actually are rather than from a
geometric division of the site.

The tables of each piece of land are divided into exactly as many compact
groups as the piece has rooms, balanced so that each group carries close to the
same capacity — within about 5 % of the average. A group never reaches across
two pieces of land. Each room is then placed at the centre of its group, so the
room sits near the middle of the load it serves, which is what keeps the AC runs
from that group short. If the centre itself is not free, the nearest free
position is used, searched north and south first and then outward.

Each room must fit **entirely** inside the usable area, at the length and width
you set, the same rule the tables follow. A position that would put part of the
building in the perimeter road band, in a water body, in a transmission line
corridor or on excluded ground is not used. See
[How placement works](/docs/layout/how-placement-works).

### A room that finds no place

A room that finds no valid position anywhere near its group is **not** dropped
silently. It is still counted as needed, and the plot is reported with the
design warning **ICR not placed**, for example *"2 of the 6 ICRs this plot needs
found no room in it."* The warning appears in the Summary view, on the
**Design warnings** chip and in the PDF summary and the BOM — see
[Design warnings](/docs/reference/design-warnings). The usual causes are a
building too large for the gaps the site leaves, or a piece of land too narrow
to take one; check the length and width fields first.

## Seeing the blocks

Which tables belong to which room is a layer of its own. Open **Layers ▾** in
the Layout view's tools and switch **ICR blocks** on: each block — the tables
wired, through their inverter or string monitoring box, to one room — is filled
in its own colour and outlined. The row is off by default and becomes available
once the inverters are placed.

<Screenshot id="icr-blocks" />

With the layer on, clicking a block opens a window summarising it — its
capacity, tables, inverters, transformer, cable lengths and piles. The blocks,
that window and the ICR-block exports are covered in
[ICR blocks](/docs/layout/icr-blocks).

## The tables a room clears

**Any table overlapping a control room's footprint is removed.** The building
wins; the ground under it and the ground its footprint touches carry no
structures.

Removing tables changes the capacity the count was worked out from, so the
count is checked again:

- **After the footprints are cleared**, the count is worked out afresh from the
  tables that remain. If it differs, the rooms are placed again with the new
  count. This check can raise the count or lower it.
- **After the building-shadow keep-clear**, on a fixed-tilt plant, the count is
  worked out once more. If fewer rooms would now do, the lower count is tried,
  and kept only when the settled layout still needs no more. This check only
  ever lowers the count.

This is why the room count and the table count in the Summary view are
consistent with each other rather than with a figure you would get by dividing
the site area by the block size.

## Moving a control room by hand

The automatic position is derived from the tables. It knows nothing about where
the site road reaches, where the ground is firm enough for a building, where the
existing site access is, or where the owner will allow a structure. When one of
those decides the position, move the room. The Tools tab carries the reminder
*"Tip: Click and drag a blue ICR to reposition it."*

<Steps>
<Step>
### Leave Pan and Zoom inactive

Dragging a room only works when neither **Pan** nor **Zoom** in the Layout
view's tools is holding the mouse. If one of them is active, click it off first
— otherwise the drag moves the view instead of picking up the building.
</Step>

<Step>
### Click and hold the room

Click the dark blue rectangle and keep the button down. Fit the view first with
**Home**, then roll the mouse wheel over the room you want, so you are picking
up a building and not a table beside it.
</Step>

<Step>
### Drag it inside the perimeter road and release

Drop it anywhere the whole footprint stays inside the plant — inside the
perimeter road, clear of the exclusions. On release the layout rebuilds around
the new position.
</Step>

<Step>
### Check what came back

An invalid drop **snaps back** to where the room started, so a room that
returns to its original position was dropped somewhere it cannot stand. A valid
drop rebuilds the layout, so the tables the room used to clear come back and the
tables at the new position go away. The number of rooms does not change.
</Step>
</Steps>

A drag is ignored while cables are being routed. After an edit on the plot the
status bar reads *"Cables are being routed… Obstruction, MCR and object tools
and ICR dragging are back when they finish."*; wait for the run to end, then
drag.

<Callout type="warn">
Moving a room away from the centre of its group of tables lengthens every AC run
from that group. If cable lengths matter to your estimate, re-check the cable
rows of the Summary view after the move rather than assuming they are unchanged.
</Callout>

## In a central-inverter design

The building's role changes with the electrical topology, and so does what it
contains.

<Tabs items={["String Inverter", "Central Inverter"]}>
<Tab value="String Inverter">

String inverters stand out in the field, in the gap bands between rows. DC
string cables run from the tables to the inverters, and each inverter's AC
cable runs to a control room, where the medium-voltage circuit that leaves the
plant begins. An inverter starts on the room nearest to it **along the plant**,
up to the number of inverters that room's block is rated for; a room that is
over-full hands inverters on to a neighbouring room. A room still over its
rating is reported as the design warning **ICR over rating**.

</Tab>
<Tab value="Central Inverter">

The central inverters sit **inside** the control room. String monitoring boxes
(SMBs) stand out in the field collecting the strings, and a DC trunk runs from
each box to the room. There is no AC leg in the field at all, because the
conversion happens in the building.

One room houses at most **4** central inverters. Size the **ICR Block** so the
capacity of one block does not need more than that — the block capacity, the
number of central inverters it implies, and the building you have drawn all have
to agree.

</Tab>
</Tabs>

Either way, the medium-voltage cables leave the control rooms and run to the
main control room, sharing a trunk where their routes converge rather than
running parallel lines. See [Cable routing](/docs/layout/cables).

On a site with several plant boundaries, each plant's rooms route their
medium-voltage cables to a unit substation in their own plot instead. See
[Multi-plot sites](/docs/layout/multi-plot).

## The shadow a control room casts

A 5 m building next to a row of modules shades it. The application computes the
year-round shadow footprint of every control room, main control room and placed
object over the **Shadow Window** — the band of solar hours you care about,
9.0 to 15.0 by default — and, with **Clear tables in shadows** on, removes
the tables that fall inside it.

That option is **on** by default, so a first run already keeps tables out of
building shadows. Switching it off leaves shaded tables in place, which raises
reported capacity and makes the yield estimate optimistic for those rows. Change
the room's height and the shadow footprint changes with it.

## The fields that decide a control room

Four fields, on two cards of the Site tab, decide the rooms directly:

| Field | Card | What it decides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ICR Block** | Site Parameters | How many rooms each piece of land carries, and therefore how its tables are grouped around them |
| **ICR** length, width and height | Structures & Shadow | The footprint that has to fit and that clears tables, and the height that casts the shadow |
| **Clear tables in shadows** | Structures & Shadow | Whether the tables inside a room's year-round shadow are removed |
| **Shadow Window** | Structures & Shadow | The band of solar hours the shadow footprint is accumulated over |

Everything that changes the placed capacity changes the count as well: the
perimeter road width, the corridors, the obstructions and excluded terrain all
take ground, and so tables and capacity, away. And anything that leaves parts of
the plant more than 300 m apart — a large water body, an obstruction across a
narrow neck of land — makes separate pieces, each with rooms of its own.

## Checking the rooms after a run

Four things are worth a look before you accept a layout:

- **The count against your electrical concept.** The **ICR blocks** chip under
  the view is the number of rooms placed. If it is more than your single-line
  concept expects, either the plant has overshot a block boundary or the site
  has split into separate pieces of land — adjust the block size, or accept the
  extra building. If a design warning says a room found no place, the plant is
  a room short.
- **Where each room landed.** Zoom into each one with the mouse wheel. A room
  at the centre of its group of tables is what you want; a room pushed to one
  side of its group is usually a room that had nowhere else to fit, and it is
  the first candidate for a hand move. The **ICR blocks** layer shows the
  groups.
- **Whether the ground is buildable.** The application knows the boundary, the
  exclusions and the slope limits. It does not know where the site access road
  will run, or where the ground is firm. Those are yours to check.
- **The gap the room left.** Turn on **Wireframe** and look at the cleared
  footprint. If it is much larger than the building you intend, the length and
  width fields are set to something you did not mean.

## What the outputs report

The **ICR blocks** row of the Summary view carries the count of rooms placed in
each plant, with a Total column on a multi-plot site; the **ICR blocks** chip
under every view carries the same figure for the site. In a string-inverter
design the **AC cable to ICR (m)** row is the total length of the runs from the
inverters into the rooms; in a central-inverter design it is replaced by **DC
cable to central inverter (m)**. Every row is listed in
[Summary rows](/docs/reference/summary-columns).

For each room on its own, click its block with the **ICR blocks** layer on. The
exports under **Export ▾** in the view header go further than the Summary view
and break the plant down **per control room**:

- **Export ICR-Block PDF** — one A3 PDF with the whole plant and a table of the
  blocks on the first sheet, then one sheet per block with its key plan and
  details.
- **Export ICR-Block DXF** — one drawing set with the whole plant on the first
  sheet and then one sheet per block, each with its summary table. Both block
  exports are described in [ICR blocks](/docs/layout/icr-blocks#the-block-exports).
- **Export Cable Schedule (Excel)** — a workbook with one sheet per ICR block:
  the DC strings from each table row to its inverter or monitoring box, the AC
  or DC-trunk feeders, and the collection runs to the transformers and the main
  control room. It does not need cables calculated, but without them the
  lengths are straight-line distances × 1.15. See
  [CAD export](/docs/exports/dxf#the-cable-schedule).
- **Export Detailed Project Report** — the Word document, whose design summary
  carries the inverter and cable figures by control room. See
  [The project report](/docs/exports/project-report).
