title: How access works
description: Access belongs to your account and covers one device, checked online — what it unlocks, the access chip in the ribbon, the License window's states, and the offline grace period.
---

Access to SolarLayout Desktop belongs to **your account** — the email you sign
in with at solarlayout.app — and covers **one device**. It is not a
licence file: there is nothing to install, copy, back up or keep safe.
**Help ▸ License / Subscription…** opens the window that shows this device's
status and requests access for it. The Device ID tells the server which
computer is asking, and status is checked online.

You need not open that window just to check status. The ribbon at the top of
every window carries an **access chip**: **● Active — until &lt;date&gt;**
(with *"(offline)"* appended while a cached result is in use), **● Access
expired** or **● Access revoked** with a **Contact Us** button beside it, or
**● Access status unavailable** if the last check failed. On a device with no
access at all the chip itself is hidden and only a gold **Get Free Access**
button shows in its place. The chip is refreshed at startup, whenever the window
regains focus, after the License window closes, and every 30 minutes.

The application opens fully usable regardless of that status. Access only
matters once you ask it to generate a layout or hand you a result.

## What needs this device active

| Action | Needs active access |
| --- | --- |
| **Generate Layout** | Yes |
| The exports on the **Export ▾** menu — KMZ, DXF, ICR-block DXF and PDF, cable schedule, the report, the PDF with piles | Yes |
| The exports elsewhere — **Export TMY data CSV** on the Yield tab, the SLD and BOM exports, the pile editor's **Export to Excel…**, an ICR block window's **PDF…** | Yes |
| Filing a support ticket from the **Support** button | Yes |
| Reading and changing every input, loading a boundary file, opening a project | No |
| The **SLD** and **BOM** views | No |

Because almost everything downstream is computed *from* a generated layout, a
device without active access is in practice limited to exploring the inputs,
loading a boundary file, and opening every menu, the in-app guide and the
**License / Subscription** window itself.

Clicking **Generate Layout** or an export without active access does not show
a separate message: the **License / Subscription** window opens itself, with
the headline for this device's state, so you can act on it at once. At launch
the window also opens by itself, but only on a device that has never had
access.

## The License / Subscription window

The headline and the line under it report the state:

| Headline | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **✓ Active — until &lt;date&gt;** | *"You have access until &lt;date&gt;."* |
| **Start your free trial** | *"All features are available during your free trial. Activation takes one click in your browser."* |
| **⏳ Waiting for activation** | *"This will update automatically when you return here after activating in your browser."* — shown right after **Get Free Access** |
| **Access expired** | *"Your free trial or subscription has ended. Contact us to purchase, or to request a trial extension."* |
| **Access revoked** | *"Access on this device has been revoked. Contact us to restore it."* |
| **Access status unavailable** | *"We couldn't reach the SolarLayout server. Check your connection and press Refresh."* |

An optional line *"Access granted to &lt;email&gt;"* names the account.

The buttons depend on the state:

- While active: **Access Details**, which opens the same web page as
  activation for this device, and **Support cases**, which opens your list of
  support cases.
- While not active: **Get Free Access** and **Contact Us**.
- Always: **Refresh** and **Close**.

The **Device ID** sits behind a **Having trouble?** disclosure at the bottom:
the group **Your Device ID** with the hint *"If support asks for your Device
ID, copy it from here and include it in your message."* and a **Copy** button.
The Device ID is the Windows registry MachineGuid — the same value Windows
shows under Settings ▸ System ▸ About ▸ Device ID.

## Getting access

Click **Get Free Access** and the application opens your browser at the
activation page for this device. Complete activation there, then switch back
to the **License / Subscription** window — it re-checks whenever it regains
focus and the headline flips to **✓ Active — until &lt;date&gt;** by itself.

**Contact Us** opens the SolarLayout contact page in your browser, for
questions or to arrange paid access. [Activate this device](/docs/licence/activate)
walks through the full flow.

## Working offline

When this device cannot reach the server, a good check it already made is
cached, and the application keeps working under that cached status for up to
**3 days** before it needs to check in again; the ribbon chip reads
**● Active — until &lt;date&gt; (offline)** meanwhile. An active result is
treated as fresh for 5 minutes, so the application does not call the server on
every check. If it cannot reach the server and has no usable cached check, the
window reads **Access status unavailable**.

## A second device

Free access is for one device per account. Click **Get Free Access** on a
second computer and the activation page reads **Your access is already on
another device** — *"Your account already uses SolarLayout on another device,
so this one cannot be added."* It lists the **Registered device** and **This
device** by Device ID, and activation stops there. Contact SolarLayout to sort
it out.

Two limits, stated plainly:

- **Your device counts against your account for good.** Replacing a computer
  does not free its place: the new computer gets the same page. Contact
  SolarLayout with its Device ID.
- **A device belongs to one account.** If this computer was activated under a
  different email, the page reads **This device is already in use** — sign in
  with that account instead, or contact SolarLayout.

An expired or revoked account may still activate its first device; the device
simply shows that status.

## Updates, reinstalls, and other accounts

Access is server-side and belongs to your account, not stored in a file, so it
survives reinstalls and application updates automatically — there is nothing
to re-load, and reinstalling the application keeps this computer's place on
your account. A reinstall of Windows or a new machine can change the Device ID,
and a changed ID needs activating again. See
[Updates and reinstalling](/docs/install/updates).

A second Windows user account on the same PC has the same Device ID, so access
carries over to it too.
