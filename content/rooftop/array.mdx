The **Array** lens in SolarLayout Rooftop holds what the layout is made of:
the module, how it is mounted, how far apart the rows stand and which way
they face, and the shade-free window that keeps them clear of shade. Every
change here lays out the roof again, and the design runs again.

The lens opens at `/studio/array`. Its sections, top to bottom: **Module**,
**Mounting**, **Spacing** (not on a sloped roof), then **Shade-free window**.
On a computer the line under the lens title counts the layout, as
`… positions · … tables · … rows · … m² of modules`.

## Module

The **Module** card shows the PV module of the design, read from its PAN
file:

- the model, then its maker and rated power, as `<maker> · <Wp> Wp`;
- its size in metres, and its bifaciality for a bifacial module;
- its Voc, its Isc and its bypass diodes, and `twin half-cell` where it
  applies;
- its temperature coefficients of Voc, Vmp and Pmpp in %/°C, with where they
  come from: `PAN file`, `one-diode model` or `entered`;
- the engine's notes on the module, with an exclamation mark beside the
  coefficients;
- the file's name, and **Change**.

Before the first run the card reads `Reading the module with the first run`.

A new design starts with the INA-144MHC-TF-560: 560 Wp, 2.277 × 1.133 m,
bifaciality 0.80. It is a sample that comes with Rooftop, not one of the
library's modules.

The module's size and orientation set the footprint of every position. Its
voltages, coefficients and highest system voltage set how many modules fit in
a string (modules wired in series); its Imp and Isc how many strings an MPPT
(an inverter input with its own maximum power point tracker) can take; its
rated power the capacity of each stringing option. See
[the Strings lens](/docs/rooftop/strings).

### Choose a module from the library

**Change** opens **Choose a module**: the app's library of 119 modules, from
300 to 650 Wp, from five makers.

| Control | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Search (**Maker or model**) | Every word typed must appear in the maker, the model or the file's name |
| **Power** | A range in Wp, in steps of 5 |
| Columns | **Module**, **Wp**, **Efficiency %**, **Size m**, and **Bifacial** on a computer. Press a heading to sort; on a phone, the **Order** list sorts instead |
| Count | `… of 119 modules` shown |

The list opens sorted by power, low to high. The design's own module is
tagged `In this design`. Choose a row and press **Use this module**, or
double-click it, or press **Enter**. The module is read, the roof is laid out
again, and the design runs again.

### Use a PAN file of your own

On a tablet or a computer, drop a `.PAN` file on the strip **Or drop a PAN
file of your own here**, or press **Choose a file**. On a phone, use
**Choose a PAN file of your own**.

Rooftop reads the maker, model, rated power and size, the electrical values,
the temperature coefficients, the cells and bypass diodes, the one-diode
parameters, the highest system voltage, the bifaciality, the light-induced
degradation and the incidence-angle profile. A file must give the rated power
(PNom), Voc and Isc. A file without a size is read, but the card carries a
note and the layout cannot be made until a file with a size is chosen.

A file that cannot be read leaves the module as it was:
**This module was not loaded**, then the reason and `The module of before is
kept.`

## Mounting

**Mounting** says how the modules stand on the roof. Its fields follow the
roof type.

| Roof type | Fields |
| --- | --- |
| Flat, tilted rows | **Tilt**, **Orientation**, **Modules in tilt**, **Lower-edge clearance** |
| Flat, east-west | **East-west tilt**, **Ridge gap**, **Lower-edge clearance** |
| Sloped, flush | A **Tilt** read-out, **Orientation**, **Modules in tilt** |

| Field | Default | Range | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Tilt** | 15° | 0 to 45°, by 1° | The angle of the tables. Takes effect when the slider is let go. The hint under it suggests a tilt from the latitude, held between 5° and 15°: `Latitude … suggests …°`. |
| **Orientation** | **Portrait** | **Portrait**, **Landscape** | Portrait puts the module's length up the slope, landscape its width. |
| **Modules in tilt** | 1 | 1 to 4 | How many modules stand one above another on a table. A taller table casts a longer shadow, so a shade-free row pitch grows with it. |
| **Lower-edge clearance** | 0.5 m | 0 to 3 m | The height of the table's lower edge above the roof. The shadows of obstacles and the parapet that keep positions empty are worked out at this height. |
| **East-west tilt** | 10° | 0 to 30°, by 1° | The tilt of each side, away from the ridge. |
| **Ridge gap** | 0.3 m | 0 to 2 m | The gap between the two sides at the ridge. |

On a sloped roof the tilt is the roof's own **Pitch**, set in the
[Roof lens](/docs/rooftop/roof#edges-and-access); the read-out says `Follows
the roof pitch`, with the bearing the roof faces. East-west units always hold
one module per side with its width up the slope, so **Orientation** and
**Modules in tilt** do not apply there.

Two settings have no field. Modules stand 0.02 m apart on a table, and on a
sloped roof their lower edge sits 0.1 m above the roof. A table's depth up
the slope is the number of modules in tilt times the module's side, plus
0.02 m between each.

## Spacing

**Spacing** sets how far apart the rows stand and which way they face. On
tilted rows it holds **Row pitch** and **Azimuth**; on east-west roofs,
**Azimuth** alone. A sloped roof has no spacing: its rows follow one another
up the roof.

Each value has a switch. On, the engine decides it. Off, a field appears,
filled with the value of the last layout, and takes your own.

| Field | Switch | Default | Range when entered | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Row pitch** | **Shade-free row pitch** | On: the engine decides | 0.5 to 30 m | The distance on plan from the front edge of one row to the front edge of the next |
| **Azimuth** | **Follow the roof edge** | On: the engine decides | 0 to 359.9°, clockwise from north | The direction the rows face |

### Shade-free row pitch

With the switch on, the engine finds the lowest sun profile angle in the
shade-free window of the design day, with the sun in front of the rows,
checked every 15 minutes and never below 1°. The pitch is the one at which a
row does not shade the next at that angle:

pitch = L × (cos β + sin β ÷ tan ψ)

where L is the table's depth up the slope, β the tilt and ψ the profile
angle. The pitch is rounded up to the next 5 cm. The read-out gives it with
`Shade-free · GCR … · profile angle …°`; the GCR (ground coverage ratio) is
the table's depth up the slope divided by the pitch.

The pitch never falls below the table's depth on plan plus 0.3 m. A pitch you
enter under that is raised to it, and a note in **Notes to review** says so.

### Azimuth

With **Follow the roof edge** on, the rows face square off the roof edge
closest to the equator: the engine takes the smallest rectangle that holds
the roof, and faces the rows along the side of it that looks nearest the
equator. The read-out says `Follows the roof edge closest to the equator`; on
an east-west roof, `The ridges run square to the roof edge closest to the
equator`.

## Shade-free window

The **shade-free window** is the hours of the design day, in solar time, in
which rows and obstacles must not shade the modules. Solar time puts noon
where the sun stands highest.

| Field | Default | Range | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Shade-free window** | 09:00 to 16:00 | 05:00 to 19:00, by the quarter hour. The start is noon at the latest, the end noon at the earliest | The hours, in solar time |
| **Design day** | **Winter solstice** | **Winter solstice**, **21 Jun**, **21 Mar** | The day the window is on. The winter solstice is 21 December in the northern hemisphere, 21 June in the southern |
| **Allow shaded positions** | Off | Off, on | Off: positions shaded inside the window stay empty. On: they are used too |

<Screenshot id="rooftop-array-window" />

The engine follows the sun through the window every quarter hour. The window
sets three things:

- **Kept-clear areas.** The shadows of every obstacle and of the parapet,
  swept across the window at the height of the lower edge, are kept free of
  modules unless shaded positions are allowed. This applies on every roof
  type.
- **Row pitch** on tilted rows, when the engine decides it.
- **The gap between east-west units.** Units stand side by side with no gap
  unless the window needs one; then the engine adds the gap that keeps both
  faces shade-free, rounded up to 5 cm. A layout note gives the gap.

Widening the window adds space between rows and keeps more of the roof clear
around obstacles; narrowing it fits more modules, and accepts shade outside
the window. The window shapes the layout only. The energy run still counts
the shade on every module for every hour of the weather year, as the
[Sun lens](/docs/rooftop/sun) shows.

## How the layout is placed

The engine lays the modules out from the roof, the array settings and the
module. Weather, inverter and system voltage play no part in it.

1. **Turn the roof to the azimuth**, so that the array faces down the frame.
2. **Find where modules may stand.** Start from the roof inside the setback,
   keeping only the largest piece if the setback splits it. Take away every
   obstacle grown by **Clearance**. Unless shaded positions are allowed, take
   away the kept-clear areas too.
3. **Lay a grid** from the corner of that area. Every row shares the same
   columns. A table, all its modules up the slope, is placed only where its
   whole footprint lies inside the area. On east-west roofs each side of a
   unit is tested on its own.
4. **Space it.** On tilted rows, columns step by the module's side plus
   0.02 m, with walkway aisles, and rows by the pitch. On sloped roofs rows
   follow directly. On east-west roofs, units are two module sides deep plus
   the ridge gap, with the shade gap and the walkways between them.

What the counts mean:

| Count | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Positions | The modules placed |
| Tables | On tilted and sloped roofs, unbroken runs of side-by-side modules in a row; on east-west roofs, groups of 10 positions along a unit |
| Rows | Rows, or units, with at least one module |
| GCR | Depth up the slope ÷ pitch on tilted rows; 1.0 on sloped roofs; twice the depth ÷ (unit width + gap) on east-west roofs |

The parapet and each obstacle have their own kept-clear area on the roof.
Press one for its callout: `Kept clear · … positions` (or `Shaded in the
window · …` when shaded positions are allowed), and which obstacle shades it
between which hours.

There is no cap on the number of modules. The roof covers 30,000 m² at most.

## When something goes wrong

| Message | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| **The layout was not updated**, with the reason and **Try again** | The positions shown are those of the design before. Correct what the reason names, or try again. |
| `No module fits on this roof` | There is no room for a module inside the setback and clear of shadows. Check the outline, the setback and the obstacles in the Roof lens. |
| `… gives no size of the module (Width and Height). The layout needs it: choose a PAN file that has it.` | Choose another PAN file, or one from the library. |
| `…: not a PVsyst module file (PNom, Voc or Isc missing)` | The file is not a module file Rooftop can read. Choose another. |
| `Voc coefficient … %/°C is outside the usual −0.35 to −0.20 %/°C for crystalline silicon. Check the datasheet and enter it if it differs.` | Enter the datasheet's value under **Sizing temperatures** in the Strings lens. |
| `The equipment catalog could not be loaded (HTTP …). Try again.` | Check the connection and press **Try again**. |
| `No module in the app matches “…”.` | Clear the search, or take a PAN file of your own. |
