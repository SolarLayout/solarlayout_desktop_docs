SolarLayout Rooftop updates in the browser: the next time you open it, you
have the latest. Each update that changes what you meet has an entry here,
newest first.

## 13 October 2026

SolarLayout Rooftop is rebuilt around the order of the work: its screens,
their words and the way through them.

- **Five steps.** A design goes Roof, Array, Strings, Performance, Handover.
  The steps stand in the top bar (on a phone, the tab bar at the foot), each
  with its mark: a tick when done, a note when it needs you. One primary leads
  on from each step: **Build this roof** on Find, then **Continue to Array**,
  **Continue to Strings** and so on, and **Download all** on Handover. See
  [The five steps](/docs/rooftop/steps).
- **Find is the Roof step's first part.** The search is the first thing in
  the column, with **Use my location** and **Enter the roof by hand** under
  it. Outline with **Corners**, **Rectangle** or **Detect building**, add the
  obstacles with their **Height above the roof**, choose the roof type, then
  **Build this roof**. See [Find the roof](/docs/rooftop/find).
- **Sun is a view, on the Roof, Array and Strings steps.** **Sun** at the
  stage's top right shows the
  sun's arc with its shade-free hours, the shadows at the time on the
  timeline, the shade map and the shade over the year. See
  [The Sun view](/docs/rooftop/sun).
- **The result in one place.** The energy of year 1 stands at the top bar's
  right (on a tablet, in a row under the stage); pressed, it gives seven
  figures and **Open Performance**. Notes to review count beside it.
- **One name for each thing.** The roof types are "Flat, rows facing south",
  "Flat, rows facing east and west" and "Sloped" (**Tilted rows**,
  **East-west** and **Sloped** on the Roof step's buttons); the string length
  is **Modules per string**. Most fields say who set their value: **Default**,
  **From the weather**, **Set for** a country, or **Yours**.
- **Hide panel.** On a computer, the work column hides so the roof takes the
  whole width: the button at its top right, or ⌘ \\ (Ctrl \\).
- **Strings: the options first.** Most options carry a word on why they are
  there, such as **Recommended**; **Why**
  shows the lengths that fit and the hours that set them; **Add your own
  option** sits under the options. See [Strings](/docs/rooftop/strings).
- **Performance on one screen.** The energy of year 1 with Likely (P50) and
  Conservative (P90), the option and the weather beside it, three figures,
  and four tabs: **By month**, **Over 25 years**, **Where the energy goes**
  and **Assumptions**. See [Performance](/docs/rooftop/performance).
- **Handover by who the files are for.** The files are grouped for the
  client, the utility, the installer and you, with a preview of the one open;
  **Download all** takes them in one zip, the project file among them. See
  [Handover](/docs/rooftop/handover).
- **Home is a start screen.** The search for the roof first, the other ways
  in under it, your designs on this device, then the demo.
- **Waaree's modules in the library.** The module library holds 278 modules
  from 6 makers, 300 to 730 Wp. Waaree publishes no module files, so its 159
  are fitted to the one-diode model from its datasheets.
- **A design keeps its place's name**, from the place found on Find, and
  Present shows it.
- **Present's controls.** A move or a touch shows the scene before, pause or
  play, the scene after and the six scenes; Space, ← → and Esc work from the
  keyboard. Present starts from Performance and Handover on a tablet or a
  computer.
- **The help in the app's words.** The ? beside a section or a tool opens its
  topic, in the screen's own words, with a link to these pages.

## 10 October 2026

- **Each string's DC cable is sized.** The DC cable schedule gives each
  string the smallest of 4, 6 and 10 mm² copper that carries 1.25 times the
  module's Isc and drops no more than 1 % of its voltage, with a **Drop (%)**
  column. The bill of materials gives the metres of each size.
- **Far shading takes the sky the horizon hides.** The diffuse light the
  distant horizon hides now counts as far shading, not near shading, in the
  Sun lens and in Performance. The year's energy is the same.
- **The inverter in the CAD layout at its size.** The DXF draws each inverter
  at the width and depth its OND file gives; where the file gives none, a 2 ×
  1.2 m block named a symbol, not to scale.
- **Add the obstacle.** In the Roof lens a new obstacle joins the design by
  **Add the …** or Enter, once its height is typed; leaving the height field
  does not add it.
- **One button for a detected footprint.** On Find, the callout of a
  detected building has **Use this outline**; its corners drag as any
  outline's.

## 9 October 2026

- **Where your designs are kept, in plain words.** Home's **On this device**
  says: "Your designs are kept on this device."
- **Sloped from Find.** A roof built on Find as **Sloped, flush** gets the
  roof Sloped gives in the Roof lens: no parapet, and a pitch of 10° where it
  had none.
- **PVGIS weather without its horizon says so.** When PVGIS sends a site's
  weather without its horizon, the Sun lens reads "Far shading: not counted,
  PVGIS sent no horizon for this site", and Performance notes the same. The
  next time the weather loads, PVGIS is asked again.
- **The east-west ridge line.** On an east-west roof, the Array lens says
  "The ridges run square to the roof edge closest to the equator".
- **Your own option on a small roof.** When a roof holds too few positions
  for the run's options but one string fits, **Add your own** stands under
  the message in the Strings lens, set to that string. **Add** builds it and
  chooses it.
- **A weather CSV is a whole year.** A file of other than 8,760 hours (8,784
  in a leap year) is refused: the energy of a year needs each hour of it. A
  blank or unreadable global irradiance cell is refused with its row, and so
  is one of beam or diffuse when the file gives both. Irradiance below zero, taken as 0, and gaps in the air
  temperature, filled from the hours on either side, are noted.
- **Sloped roofs face their slope.** Modules on a sloped, flush roof face its
  **Faces**, whatever azimuth the array kept from a flat roof.
- **A row pitch under a table's depth says so.** One entered under a table's
  depth on plan plus 0.3 m is raised to it, with a note to review.
- **The Voc coefficient that sizes the strings.** Under **Sizing
  temperatures**, the field gives the one-diode model's coefficient the strings
  are sized with, unless you enter one.
- **A module file without a voltage rating.** It is taken as 1000 V, and the
  Strings lens says the limit was taken, not given by the module.
