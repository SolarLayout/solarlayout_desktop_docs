**Performance** is the workspace that shows the energy of the chosen
stringing option over a year. SolarLayout Rooftop simulates every hour of the
site's weather, from the sunlight on the roof to the energy delivered to the
grid, and shows each loss on the way, the year's figures, the months, 25
years and a summary of the design.

There is nothing to run by hand. A change to the design runs it again, and
Performance shows the figures of the last run.

<Screenshot id="rooftop-performance" />

## How a run starts

A run starts on its own half a second after a change, so that changes made
close together become one run. Opening a design runs it at once.

| You do | What follows |
| --- | --- |
| Change the roof, the array, the equipment, a loss or the weather | The design runs again |
| Choose another stringing option | Shown at once: the run already holds every option |
| Rename the design | Nothing runs |
| Change **Degradation**, **Weather variability**, **Model and data**, **Exceedance levels** or **Grid CO₂** | The design runs again, but the year is not simulated again: these change only the figures worked out from the year |

A run needs a roof outline, a module, weather for the site and a system
voltage. Without a system voltage it stops after the shading: there are no
options and no energy, and a card says what it waits for. The weather is
loaded for you when the design has none for its site.

While a run is on its way, a line under the title shows a ring that fills
as it goes, **Updating energy** (or **Opening** and the design's name, when
it opens), and the step the engine is on:

1. **Fitting modules on the roof**
2. **Loading** … **weather**
3. **Computing hourly shading for every module**
4. **Applying the losses**
5. **Sizing strings and simulating the options**

The figures from before stay on the page, dimmed, until the new ones arrive.
If a run fails, they stay, not dimmed, under **Energy was not updated** and
**Values are of the design before**, with the reason. **Try again** runs the
design again at once.

## The page, top to bottom

| Part | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| Header | The title, the run line, **Stringing option** (on a phone, the **Option** list) and **Weather** |
| The figures | **Energy to grid, year 1** with its P-values, and six tiles |
| **From sunlight to the grid** | The chain of gains and losses, with the losses you can change |
| **Energy to grid by month** | Twelve months, as a chart or a table |
| **25 years** | The energy of each year, with **Degradation** |
| **Assumptions** | The model's settings and the uncertainty |
| **Summary** | The design, summed up in one table |

On a computer the chain stands at the left and the charts and assumptions at
the right. On a tablet the two charts stand side by side, and on a phone
everything is in one column.

The run line says what was simulated: the modules in series, the strings
and the inverters of the option, then the hours of weather, its source, the
lowest and highest air temperature, and the highest angle of the horizon, or
"no horizon".

## Weather

The **Weather** list in the header offers three sources. Each is loaded for
the middle of the roof outline.

| Source | What it is | Horizon |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **PVGIS TMY** (the default) | A typical meteorological year from PVGIS, the European Commission's solar database: hourly global, beam and diffuse irradiance, air temperature and wind | The PVGIS horizon of the site, so far shading is counted |
| **NASA POWER 2023** | NASA POWER's hourly values for the year 2023 | None |
| **CSV file** | Your own hourly values, with times read as UTC | None |

Choosing PVGIS or NASA POWER asks that service for the site's weather by
its latitude and longitude. The list names the source at once with a turning
ring, and the line under the title reads **Loading PVGIS TMY weather** or
**Loading NASA POWER 2023 weather**. When the hours arrive, the design runs
on them. If they do not, **The weather was not loaded** stands under the list
with the reason, and the weather from before stays.

The weather is kept in the design. It is used again while its source is the
same and the roof stays within 0.001° of latitude and longitude of the place
it was loaded for; move the roof further and the weather of the new place is
loaded.

When PVGIS sends its year without the horizon, the run says so under the run
line and in the design's notes: "PVGIS sent no horizon for this site, so far
shading is not counted."

### Your own CSV

<Steps>
<Step>

### Open the dialog

Choose **CSV file** in the **Weather** list. **Load weather from a CSV**
opens.

</Step>
<Step>

### Give the file

Drop the file on **Drop the weather file here**, or **Choose a file**. A
`.csv` or `.txt` file of 3 MB at most is taken. The file is read and its
summary shown: the hours, the global irradiation, the beam and diffuse, and
the range of air temperature, with any notes on the file.

</Step>
<Step>

### Use it

**Use this weather** makes it the weather of the design, and the design runs
on it. Nothing changes until you press it; **Cancel** leaves the weather as
it was.

</Step>
</Steps>

<Screenshot id="rooftop-performance-csv" />

The file is a table with a header row. Rooftop finds the separator itself,
skips lines that begin with `#`, and reads the columns by their names, in
any capitals:

| Column | Names it is found by | Needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Time | `time`, `time(utc)`, `datetime`, `date`, `timestamp`, `date/time` | Yes |
| Global horizontal irradiance | `ghi`, `g(h)`, `gh`, `global_horizontal`, `allsky_sfc_sw_dwn`, `irradiance` | Yes |
| Beam (direct normal) | `dni`, `gb(n)`, `bn`, `allsky_sfc_sw_dni` | No |
| Diffuse horizontal | `dhi`, `gd(h)`, `dh`, `diffuse`, `allsky_sfc_sw_diff` | No |
| Air temperature | `temp_air`, `t2m`, `temp`, `temperature`, `tamb`, `t_amb`, `ambient_temp` | No |
| Wind speed | `wind_speed`, `ws10m`, `wind`, `ws` | No |

- Irradiance is in W/m², temperature in °C, wind in m/s.
- Times are UTC. Write them as `2023-06-01 13:00`, or as PVGIS does,
  `20230601:1300`. Each row's time is the start of its hour.
- Give every hour of a year once: 8,760 hours, or 8,784 in a leap year. A
  file of other hours is refused, since the energy of a year needs every hour.
- Give every hour its irradiance. A blank or unreadable irradiance cell is
  refused, with its column and its row.

What Rooftop does with a file that lacks something, and says in a note:

| The file | What is taken |
| --- | --- |
| Has no beam or no diffuse column | Both are worked out from the global irradiance with the Erbs model, less exact than measured values |
| Has no air temperature | 25 °C for every hour. The strings are then sized for 25 °C: enter the site's lowest and highest ambient in the [Strings lens](/docs/rooftop/strings) |
| Has no wind speed | 1 m/s for every hour |
| Gives a row every few minutes | Each hour is the average of its rows |
| Has irradiance below zero | Taken as 0 |
| Has gaps in the air temperature | Filled from the hours on either side |

## The model

The simulation runs every hour of the weather, in the order of the
PVsyst loss diagram:

1. **Sun position**, at the middle of each hour.
2. **Transposition** of the light onto the plane of the modules, with the
   Perez model and the roof's albedo.
3. **Far shading.** The direct light is lost while the sun is behind the
   horizon line, where the weather has one.
4. **Near shading.** Row-to-row shading is worked out from the sun's profile
   angle. The shadows of obstacles and the parapet are cast on each module.
   The sky each module sees is reduced by the obstacles and the row in
   front.
5. **IAM**, the light reflected off the glass at a low angle: the PAN file's
   profile when it gives one, else the ASHRAE model.
6. **Bifacial rear gain**, for a bifacial module on a flat roof.
7. **The module**, by the PVsyst one-diode model fitted to the PAN file.
8. **Cell temperature**, by the PVsyst thermal model.
9. **Electrical shading.** A shaded module costs its bypass section: in
   portrait, a whole module, or half of a half-cell module; in landscape, one
   substring. A string runs at its weakest module.
10. **Module quality, LID, mismatch and DC wiring.**
11. **The inverter**: its efficiency from the OND file's curves, its
    threshold, clipping at its AC power, and its night consumption.
12. **AC wiring and availability.**

## The losses you set

Boxed values in the chain are fields of the design. Entering one is a
change, and the design runs again.

| Field | Default | Range | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Soiling** | 2.0 % | 0 to 30 % | Dirt on the front glass |
| **Bifacial rear gain** | On | On or off | Counts the light on the back of a bifacial module. With a monofacial module: "The module is monofacial: no rear gain." |
| **Module quality** | 0.0 % | −5 to 10 % | The module's output against its rating; a negative value is a gain |
| **LID** | The PAN's value, else 1.5 % | 0 to 10 % | Light-induced degradation. **Use the PAN's** or **Use the default** sets it back |
| **Mismatch** | 1.0 % | 0 to 10 % | Differences between modules of a string |
| **DC ohmic** | 1.5 % | 0 to 10 % | DC wiring loss at STC power; it grows with the power of the hour |
| **AC ohmic** | 1.0 % | 0 to 10 % | AC wiring loss at the inverter's AC power; it grows with the load |
| **Availability** | 99.5 % | 80 to 100 % | The share of the year the system runs |

The **Assumptions** card holds the rest:

| Field | Default | Range | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Thermal Uc** | By roof type: 29 for tilted rows, 20 for east-west, 15 for sloped | 5 to 60 W/m²K | How well the modules shed heat. **Use the roof type's** sets it back |
| **Rear shading and mismatch** | 10.0 % | 0 to 50 % | Taken off the rear gain |
| **Roof albedo** | 0.20 | 0.05 to 0.9 | The share of light the roof reflects; the same value as **Albedo** in the Roof lens |
| **Weather variability** | 3.0 % | 0 to 20 % | How much one year's sunlight differs from another's |
| **Model and data** | 3.0 % | 0 to 20 % | The uncertainty of the model and its inputs |
| **Exceedance levels** | P50, P75, P90 | P50 to P99 | The three P-values shown; each level once, sorted |
| **Grid CO₂** | 0.716 t/MWh | 0 to 2 t/MWh | The grid's emissions, for **CO₂ avoided** |

The other rows of the chain follow from the design and its files: the
horizon and near shading from the roof, the layout and the weather; IAM and
the module's behaviour from the PAN file; the inverter's efficiency,
threshold, clipping and night consumption from the OND file.

## The figures

All figures are those of the chosen option.

| Figure | How it is worked out | Unit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Energy to grid, year 1** | The energy of every hour at the grid, after availability | MWh |
| P-values, under it | See [P50, P75 and P90](#p50-p75-and-p90) | MWh |
| **Performance ratio** | Energy ÷ (DC capacity × in-plane irradiation) | % |
| **Specific yield** | Energy ÷ DC capacity | kWh/kWp |
| **Capacity factor, AC** | Energy ÷ (AC capacity × 8,760 h), followed by the DC capacity factor | % |
| **In-plane irradiation** | The light reaching the plane of the modules, before shading | kWh/m² |
| **Energy over 25 years** | The sum of years 1 to 25 | MWh |
| **CO₂ avoided** | Energy × **Grid CO₂** | t/yr |

The performance ratio (PR) is the share of the energy the modules would
deliver at their rating, under the sunlight they receive, that reaches the
grid.

**From sunlight to the grid** has 21 rows, from **Global horizontal
irradiation** and **Transposition to plane** to **Energy to grid**. Each
shows its change from the step before, a gain or a loss, and the running
total in kWh/m² for light or MWh for energy. Point at a row for its figures.

**Energy to grid by month** shows twelve bars, with the highest and lowest
labelled; **Table** gives each month in MWh and kWh/kWp, and the year.
**Summary** lists the site, the roof, the structure, the module, the
inverter, the stringing, the DC and AC capacity, the weather with its
irradiation, the losses (clipping, LID and the uncertainty), the energy with
its P-values, the specific yield with PR and capacity factor, the module area
and the CO₂ avoided.

## P50, P75 and P90

A P-value is the energy of a year that is reached or exceeded with that
probability: P90 is the energy exceeded in 90 years out of 100, P50 the
middle. Rooftop works them from the year's energy and one uncertainty:

- σ is the square root of **Weather variability**² + **Model and data**².
  The defaults give 4.24 %.
- P*x* = energy × (1 − z × σ), where z is the normal distribution's value
  for *x* %. P50 equals the energy of year 1.

On the demo roof, the year's 617.8 MWh gives P75 600.2 MWh and P90
584.2 MWh. The last cell names σ beside its level. Change the levels under
**Exceedance levels**.

## 25 years

The **25 years** card draws the energy of each year, falling by
**Degradation**, 0.5 % a year by default (0 to 3 %). Year *n* is the energy
of year 1 × (1 − degradation) to the power *n* − 1. The chart is scaled to
its own range, not to zero; the table gives years 1, 5, 10, 15, 20 and 25 and
the 25-year total.

## Choosing an option

The run simulates up to five stringing options, lettered A, B, C. A string
is a chain of modules wired in series; an MPPT is an inverter input that
tracks its own best operating point; the DC/AC ratio is the array's DC
rating over the inverters' AC rating. Options within the DC/AC target come
first, then by energy. A is **Recommended** when it meets the target.

Choose an option under **Stringing option**, or in the **Option** list on a
phone. The figures change at once, with no new run, and the same choice
holds in the Strings lens and in Handover.

When the run has no options, a card gives its reason in the engine's words,
with **Open the Strings lens**: no system voltage chosen, no string length
that fits the inverter, or too few positions for a string. The
**Assumptions** card still shows.

<Callout type="info">
Moving the roof to another place or changing the weather source loads
weather again. The run waits for it, then follows.
</Callout>
