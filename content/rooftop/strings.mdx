The **Strings** lens in SolarLayout Rooftop sizes the strings and wires the
roof. A **string** is a run of modules wired in series; an **MPPT** (maximum
power point tracker) is an inverter input that holds its strings at their
best operating point; the **DC/AC ratio** is the array's DC capacity divided
by the inverters' rated AC power. The lens finds the lengths of string that
fit the system voltage, the inverter and the site's temperatures, simulates a
year of energy for each way of stringing the roof, and draws the strings on
the roof in colour.

The lens opens at `/studio/strings`. Its sections, top to bottom:
**Inverter**, **System voltage**, **Modules in series**, **Options** (with
your own), **MPPT loading**, then two rows that open: **Sizing
temperatures** and **Inverter limits and cabling**.

A result that does not fit carries an exclamation mark; one that fits, a
tick.

## System voltage

The system voltage is the highest DC voltage the system is built for. It caps
how many modules one string can hold.

| Field | Default | Choices | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **System voltage** | 600 V in the United States, its territories and Canada; 1000 V everywhere else | **600 V**, **1000 V**, **1500 V** | The limit a string's open-circuit voltage must stay under when its modules are coldest |

**Your country sets where it starts.** A design made or opened without a
voltage takes the voltage of your country, so that it is sized and run at
once. The country is the one your connection comes from, else your device's
time zone, else India. The roof's own place sets the sun, the weather and so
the sizing temperatures, but not the voltage.

The words beside the heading say who set it:

| Words | When |
| --- | --- |
| `Set for` and your country, such as `Set for India` | The app set it, at your country's voltage |
| `Set by the app` | The app set it, at a voltage other than your country's |
| `Your choice` | You chose it |

Under an app-set voltage a line says where it comes from. At your country's
voltage, for 600 V, `600 V, the limit for homes in the United States (NEC
690.7). Change it if the site allows more.` (for Canada, CE Code, Section 64);
for 1000 V, `1000 V, the usual limit for rooftops in <country>. Change it if
the site needs another.` At a voltage other than your country's, the line
speaks of the United States and Canada for 600 V, and of rooftops outside
them otherwise.

The right voltage depends on the site, its building and the code that
applies there. Press a choice to make it yours; the app never moves it again.

<Callout type="info">
The app never raises the voltage. When the inverter or the module is rated
lower, the strings are sized to that rating and the voltage stays as it is.
When both take more, the lens offers a higher one with a button. Choose it
only where the site's code allows it.
</Callout>

**Equipment rated lower.** The rating is the lower of the inverter's highest
DC voltage and the module's highest system voltage. Under an app-set voltage
the lens says `Strings sized to … V, this inverter's limit.` (or `module's`).
A module whose file gives no rating is taken as 1000 V, and the lens says
`Strings sized to 1000 V, taken for this module: its file gives no limit.`
Under your own, it says so with an exclamation mark, `Your system is set to …
V. The bill of materials follows the voltage set.`, and offers **Use … V**,
the highest of 600, 1000 and 1500 V at or under the rating, where there is
one.

**Going higher.** When the rating is above the voltage, the lens says `This
inverter takes up to … V. Raise it only if the site's code allows.` with
**Use … V**. When no string length fits and a higher voltage would fit one,
it says `No string length fits under … V; at … V one does. Raise it only if
the site's code allows.`

The DC cable in the bill of materials is named by the voltage set.

## Inverter

The **Inverter** card shows the model, `<maker> · <AC kW> · <n> MPPT`, the
current per MPPT and the number of inputs, and the OND file's name. A current
you entered reads `entered`. Where no current per MPPT is known, the card
says `Current per MPPT not in the OND` with an exclamation mark.

A design built on Find starts with the WP-330KTL-H1, the sample inverter that comes with Rooftop:
275 kW AC, MPPT window 500 to 1500 V, highest DC voltage 1500 V, nominal MPP
voltage 1080 V, 6 MPPT and 28 inputs. Its file gives no current per MPPT, so
it is worked out as its DC input current of 390 A shared over 6 MPPT: 65 A.

### Choose an inverter

**Change** opens **Choose an inverter**: the app's library of 388 inverters.

| Control | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Search | Every word must be found in the maker, the model or the file's name |
| **AC power**, **MPPT**, **Max DC** | Filters by rated AC power, by MPPT count (1 to 15), and by highest DC voltage |
| Columns | **Inverter**, **AC kW**, **MPPT**, **MPPT V** (its window), and **Max DC V** on a computer |
| Tags | `In this design`; `Preliminary` for a file its maker calls preliminary |
| Mark | `Up to … V: strings sized under the … V set`, with an exclamation mark, on an inverter rated under the system voltage |

The list opens sorted by AC power, low to high. Choose a row and **Use this
inverter**. On a tablet or a computer, an OND file of your own can be dropped
on **Or drop an OND file of your own here** or chosen with **Choose a file**;
on a phone, use **Choose an OND file of your own**. A file without the rated
power (PNomConv) or the MPPT maximum (VMPPMax) is refused. A file that cannot
be read leaves the inverter as it was: `This inverter was not loaded`, the
reason, and `The inverter of before is kept.`

Choosing another inverter clears any current per MPPT you entered, and the design runs
again.

### How many inverters

For each length of string, the run tries every number of inverters from one
up to the larger of the fewest that take every string and the most that keep
DC/AC at 1.10 or above. It keeps the number that places the most strings
within the DC/AC target.

## Modules in series

The **Modules in series** bar shows the lengths of string that fit. Its left
end is the shortest, set by the `MPPT minimum`; its right end the longest,
set by the `Voc limit` or the `MPPT maximum`, whichever is tighter. The words
beside the heading read `… to … fit`, or `None fit`. The marker is the length
of the chosen option. While the strings are being sized the bar says
`Sizing the strings for … V`.

<Screenshot id="rooftop-strings-series" />

| End | How it is worked out |
| --- | --- |
| Shortest | The inverter's MPPT minimum ÷ the module's Vmp at 1000 W/m², with the cell at the highest ambient plus the cell rise, rounded up |
| `Voc limit` | The lowest of the system voltage, the inverter's highest DC voltage and the module's highest system voltage, ÷ the module's Voc at 1000 W/m² and the lowest ambient, rounded down |
| `MPPT maximum` | The inverter's MPPT maximum ÷ the module's Vmp at the lowest ambient, rounded down |
| Nominal | The inverter's nominal MPP voltage ÷ the module's Vmp at STC, rounded, and held inside the range. Without a nominal voltage, the longest |

The module's highest system voltage comes from its file, or is taken as
1000 V when the file gives none. The inverter's highest DC voltage is its
absolute maximum, else its MPPT maximum.

Without a Voc coefficient entered, the cold Voc comes from the module's
one-diode model. With one entered, it is Voc at STC × (1 + coefficient ÷ 100
× (lowest ambient − 25 °C)), as IEC 62548-1, Annex F.

Under the bar stands the engine's verdict on the chosen option: its Vmp at
STC against the inverter's nominal voltage, its Voc at the lowest ambient
against the limit, its Vmp at the hot cell against the MPPT minimum, its
strings, inverters and DC/AC, and its currents where they matter. It carries
a tick when the option is in its DC/AC target and every MPPT is within its
limits, and an exclamation mark otherwise.

### Sizing temperatures

**Sizing temperatures** opens from its row, which shows the range, as
`… to … °C`.

| Field | Default | Range | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Lowest ambient** | The coldest hour of the weather year; 5 °C with no weather | −50 to 40 °C | Sets the cold Voc, and so the Voc limit and the MPPT maximum |
| **Highest ambient** | The hottest hour of the weather year; 40 °C with no weather | 0 to 60 °C | With the cell rise, sets the hot Vmp, and so the shortest length |
| **Cell rise** | 25 °C on flat roofs, 35 °C on a sloped roof | 0 to 60 °C | How far the cell runs above the ambient |
| **Voc coefficient** | The one-diode model's, shown as `<x> %/°C, the one-diode model's` | −0.999 to −0.001 %/°C | Enter the datasheet's Voc coefficient to work out the cold Voc from it instead |

Each field you change offers its way back: **Use the weather's … °C**, **Use
the roof type's**, **Use the module's**. When the module's coefficient lies
outside −0.35 to −0.20 %/°C, a note with an exclamation mark asks you to
check the datasheet and enter it if it differs.

## Options

**Options** are the ways to string the roof that the run simulated, each for
a full year.

The run tries up to three lengths: the nominal, the longest, and the one,
from the nominal up, that places the most modules on the roof (or the
longest but one, when that is one of the other two). For each length:

1. It forms strings along each row, out on its top line of modules and back
   on the bottom one. What the rows leave at their ends runs on into the next row of
   the same tilt and azimuth. On an east-west roof a string stays on one side
   and never mixes east and west.
2. It ranks the strings: those within a row before those across rows, each
   least shaded first.
3. It makes up to three combinations: the most strings within the DC/AC
   target, on as many inverters as that needs; the strings that fill those
   inverters; and every string the roof holds, on the fewest inverters that
   take them.

Each combination is simulated for a year. Those in target come first, then
by energy. At most five are kept, lettered from **A**; the first is
**Recommended** only when it is in target.

| Value | How it is set |
| --- | --- |
| DC/AC | Strings × modules in series × module power ÷ (inverters × inverter AC power) |
| DC/AC target | 1.10 to 1.30; above 1.40 is `Heavy clipping` |
| Strings per MPPT | As many as the current per MPPT allows for both Imp and Isc, one at the least; with no current known, the inputs shared over the MPPT |
| Strings per inverter | Strings per MPPT × MPPT count, no more than the inverter's inputs |

<Screenshot id="rooftop-strings-options" />

Each card shows its letter and a caption, `series × strings · kWp · DC/AC`,
and the energy of the first year in MWh. The caption is one of
`Recommended`, `Your own`, `Below target`, `Above target`, `Heavy clipping`
(with the number of inverters when there are more than one), `Fills the
inverter`, `Lower clipping`, or else `Option` and its letter. A tick means
`In target`. An exclamation mark means it misses the DC/AC target, or an
MPPT is over its current, or its limit is not known.

Press a card, or use the arrow keys, to choose an option. Every option is in
the run's answer already, so nothing runs again. The note under the cards
states the target and how many strings one inverter takes, and lists the
options that miss the target.

### Your own

**Add your own** opens **Series**, **Strings** and **Inverters**, each held
to what fits:

| Field | Hint |
| --- | --- |
| **Series** | `… to … fit`: a length in the range |
| **Strings** | How many strings of that length the roof holds, or `No string of … fits on the roof` |
| **Inverters** | `1 to …`: no more than the strings, 200 at most |

The fields start from your own option's numbers, else the chosen option's. **Add** saves your option,
chooses it, and the design runs again; **Change** and **Remove** edit it
later. Removing it returns to the run's first option. Your own option is not
held to the current per MPPT; the MPPT loading shows what it puts on each.

When the engine cannot build it, its card says why with an exclamation mark:
too few strings to give each inverter one, or a length whose Voc is over the
limit, whose Vmp is over the MPPT maximum, or under the MPPT minimum. Each
ends `Change your option or remove it.`

When the roof holds too few positions for the run's options, **Options** says
`Too few positions for a string` with the length that would fit; where one
does, **Add your own** stands under the words, with **Series** set to that
length. When no length fits, or no module fits on the roof, there is no
**Add your own**.

## MPPT loading

**MPPT loading** shows the current and the power on each MPPT of the chosen
option. Its strings, in layout order, are shared among its inverters, then
spread over each inverter's MPPTs as evenly as they go.

| Column | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| **MPPT** | `MPPT …`, with its inverter, `INV-…`, under it |
| **Strings** | The strings on it |
| **Current** | Imp (strings × the module's Imp) and Isc (strings × the module's Isc), each against its limit: `… of … A` |
| **kWp** | Strings × modules in series × the module's power |
| Mark | `Fits`, or `Over the limit` or `Limit not known` with an exclamation mark |

The Imp limit is the inverter's current per MPPT from its file, else its DC
input current shared over its MPPTs. The Isc limit is its short-circuit
current per MPPT, else the same as the Imp limit. A current you enter
replaces both. On a phone the rows are cards.

When a limit is not known, the note under the table says why, with **Enter
the current per MPPT**: it opens **Inverter limits and cabling** with the
field ready.

### Inverter limits and cabling

| Field | Default | Range | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Current per MPPT** | The file's current per MPPT, else its DC input current ÷ MPPT count | 1 to 2000 A | The limit for Imp and Isc on each MPPT. An entered value reads `entered`, with **Use the file's … A** to go back |
| **AC cable per inverter** | 30 m | 1 to 2000 m | The length of AC cable from each inverter |

## Strings on the roof

While the run has an option, the roof shows its strings in colour, and the
positions the option does not use in grey, **Not used**.

**Colour the strings by** chooses **String**, **MPPT** (the default) or
**Inverter**. Six colours go round in turn. The legend sits at the bottom
left of the view on a computer and at the bottom right on a tablet; on a
phone it stands on the view in short form, and the switch is the sheet's
first control.

Point at a string to trace it; press it to select it. The traced or selected
string turns white and the others dim. Its callout reads `String … · INV-…,
MPPT …`, then its modules, its cold Voc and its length of cable. Each
inverter stands on the roof as a box marked `INV-…`, on the setback line,
nearest the middle of its strings' starts.

On a computer the line under the lens title counts the strings, their
length, the inverters and MPPTs, and the positions not used.

## When something goes wrong

| Message | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| `No string length fits this inverter` | At least so many modules are needed for the MPPT minimum, but fewer fit under the limit. Change the system voltage, the inverter or the sizing temperatures. |
| `Too few positions for a string` | The roof holds too few positions for the run's options. Where a string fits, add it as your own option at the length given. |
| `No module fits on this roof` | Check the outline, the setback and the obstacles in the [Roof lens](/docs/rooftop/roof). |
| `Current per MPPT not in the OND` | Enter the datasheet value under **Inverter limits and cabling**. |
| `Over the limit` in MPPT loading | The run's options keep within the limits; your own option is not held to them. Change your option, or check the current per MPPT against the datasheet. |
| `This inverter was not loaded` | Choose another OND file, or one from the library. The inverter of before is kept. |
