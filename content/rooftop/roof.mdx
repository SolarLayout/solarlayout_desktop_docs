The **Roof** lens is the first of the Studio's four lenses in SolarLayout
Rooftop. It holds what the array is laid on: where the roof is, its type, its
edges and the access kept along them, and what stands on or beside it. Every
change here lays out the roof again and runs the design again, except
**Albedo**, which runs the design alone.

The lens opens at `/studio/roof`, and it is where Rooftop lands after **Build
this roof** on Find. Its sections, top to bottom: **Location**, **Roof type**,
**Edges and access**, then **Obstacles**. On a computer the line under the
lens title reads the roof's area, the area inside the setback and the number
of obstacles, as `… m² · … m² inside the setback · … obstacles`.

## Location

The **Location** card says where the roof is and how it came into the design.

| Line | What it says |
| --- | --- |
| Place | The latitude and longitude of the roof, to five decimals |
| How it was entered | `Outlined on satellite`, `Entered by hand` or `From a file` |
| Corners and obstacles | For example `4 corners · 3 obstacles` |
| Size and facing | A rectangle by its two sides (`a × b m`), any other outline by its area in m², then `· faces` and the bearing of the side of its longest edge that looks toward the equator |
| File | The name of the drawing, for a roof read from a KMZ or a DXF |

**Edit** opens Find with this design's outline and obstacles on the map,
where they stand, and its roof type selected. On a phone the whole row is
the link, labelled "Edit the outline". Build the roof again and the design
takes the edited outline, its place, its obstacles and its roof type; the
parapet, setback, walkways and the rest of the design stay as they were,
except as a change of roof type to **Sloped** sets them (see below).
[Find the roof](/docs/rooftop/find) covers the map and its tools.

A roof covers 30,000 m² at most.

## Roof type

**Roof type** decides how the engine lays the modules on the roof. The Roof
lens shows the three types by their short names; Find shows the same choice
by their full names. The default is **Flat, tilted rows**.

| Type (short name) | Caption | How the modules sit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Flat, tilted rows** (**Tilted rows**) | Rows facing the equator | Rows of tables at the array's **Tilt**. Row pitch is shade-free unless you set it. A walkway comes every so many modules along a row. |
| **Flat, east-west** (**East-west**) | Back-to-back, low tilt | Back-to-back units, one face turned east and the other west. Units stand together, with a gap only where the shade-free window needs one. A walkway comes every so many units. |
| **Sloped, flush** (**Sloped**) | Modules follow the roof pitch | Modules lie on the roof at its **Pitch**, facing its **Faces**, their lower edge 0.1 m above the roof. Rows follow each other with only the module gap between them. A walkway comes every so many rows. |

Choosing **Sloped** sets the parapet to 0 and, where the roof had no pitch,
gives it a pitch of 10°. Choosing either flat type changes only the type.
The same rule applies on Find, to a roof built there as **Sloped, flush** and
to one built again with its type changed to it.

The roof type also sets three things the other lenses use:

| Setting | Tilted rows | East-west | Sloped | Used in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cell rise, for sizing strings (modules wired in series) | 25 °C | 25 °C | 35 °C | **Sizing temperatures**, Strings lens |
| Thermal Uc (how fast the modules shed heat) | 29 | 20 | 15 | The energy simulation |
| Bifacial rear gain, for a bifacial module | Counted | Counted | Not counted | The energy simulation |

## Edges and access

**Edges and access** holds the parapet, the setback from the edges, the
walkways, the clearance around obstacles and the roof's albedo. A sloped roof
adds **Pitch** and **Faces**. The fields stand two to a row on a tablet or a
computer, one to a row on a phone.

| Field | Default | Range | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Parapet** | 0.9 m | 0 to 5 m | The height of a parapet wall along every edge. It is drawn as a wall 0.2 m thick inside each edge, and casts shadows as an obstacle does. 0 means no parapet. |
| **Setback** | 1.0 m | 0 to 10 m | The strip along every edge, measured inward, where no module is placed. |
| **Walkway** | 0.6 m | 0 to 3 m | The width of each walkway left between modules. |
| **Every** | 24 modules (tilted rows); 4 rows (east-west, sloped) | 0 to 500 | How often a walkway comes. On tilted rows, an aisle across the rows after every so many modules along a row; on east-west roofs, after every so many units; on sloped roofs, after every so many rows. 0 means no walkway. |
| **Clearance** | 0.5 m | 0 to 5 m | The gap kept free of modules around every obstacle. |
| **Albedo** | 0.20 | 0.05 to 0.9 | The share of light the roof reflects. The energy simulation uses it for the light reflected onto the modules; it moves no module and no shadow. |
| **Pitch** (sloped only) | 10° for a roof made sloped | 0 to 60° | The slope of the roof, and the tilt the modules take. |
| **Faces** (sloped only) | 180° | 0 to 359.9° | The direction the slope looks, clockwise from north. The modules face it: lying on the roof, they can face nowhere else. |

The **setback** is the strip inside the roof edge kept clear of modules, for
access and for the edge zone your code asks for. The engine shrinks the roof
inward by it, with sharp corners. Where that splits the roof into pieces,
only the largest piece is used.

A value takes effect when you press **Enter** or leave the field. A value
outside the range is set to the nearest end, and the field says which: the
highest or the lowest that can be set.

The parapet's shadow through the shade-free window of the design day is kept
clear of modules, as an obstacle's is, unless shaded positions are allowed in
the [Array lens](/docs/rooftop/array#shade-free-window). When the roof has a
parapet, the obstacles list ends with a row `Parapet, all edges` and its
height; the height itself is set here.

## Obstacles

An obstacle is anything on or beside the roof that takes room or casts a
shadow: a stair room, a lift room, an AC unit, a tank, a wall, a tree, a
taller building next door. Each has a height above the roof surface, and each
counts three times:

- No module is placed on its footprint or within the **Clearance** around it.
- The area its shadow sweeps through the shade-free window of the design day
  is kept clear of modules, unless shaded positions are allowed.
- Its shadow is counted, hour by hour over the weather year, in the shading
  of every module it falls on.

Boxes, tanks, walls and buildings can be drawn on Find over the satellite
image. Trees are added here, in the Roof lens.

### Add an obstacle

<Steps>
<Step>

### Choose its kind

Under **Obstacles**, press **Add** and choose a kind. Each shows the size a
fresh one starts at.

| Kind | Starts at |
| --- | --- |
| **Box** | `2.0 × 2.0 m` |
| **Tank** | `⌀ 2.0 m` |
| **Wall** | `6.0 m, points` |
| **Tree** | `crown ⌀ 4.0 m` |
| **Building** | `beside the roof, points` |

</Step>
<Step>

### Place it

The obstacle stands at the middle of the roof, its card open. Press the roof
where it belongs to move it there, or type where it stands in the card.

</Step>
<Step>

### Give its height

Type its **Height** above the roof surface. The obstacle joins the design with
that height, and the design runs again. **Remove** drops it before it is
added.

</Step>
</Steps>

An added obstacle is named after its kind and its count, such as *Box 2*. The
name heads its card: type over it to rename it.

### Edit an obstacle

Select a row in the list, or press the obstacle on the roof, to open its
card. Its fields depend on its kind.

| Kind | Fields |
| --- | --- |
| Box | **Length**, **Width**, **Height**, **Rotation** (counter-clockwise), **East**, **North** |
| Tank | **Diameter**, **Height**, **East**, **North** |
| Tree | **Crown ⌀**, **Height**, **Crown starts at**, **East**, **North** |
| Wall | **Height**, **Thickness**, and a table of its points, two at least |
| Building | **Height**, and a table of its corners, three at least |

| Field | Range |
| --- | --- |
| **Length**, **Width** | 0.1 to 500 m |
| **Height** | 0.05 to 200 m, above the roof surface |
| **Rotation** | −180 to 180° |
| **East**, **North** | −5000 to 5000 m from the middle of the roof |
| **Diameter**, **Crown ⌀** | 0.1 to 100 m |
| **Crown starts at** | 0 to 100 m |
| **Thickness** | 0.05 to 5 m |

The points of a wall or a building are metres east and north of the middle
of the roof, in order. **Add a point** adds one; the bin beside a point
removes it, down to the fewest the kind needs.

On the roof, a selected box, tank or tree moves with a drag. Its handles do
the rest: a corner of a box resizes it with the opposite corner held still,
the round handle turns it and snaps to a right angle within 3°, the handle
on the stem above it sets its height, and the handle on the edge of a tank
or a crown sets its diameter. Drags move in steps of 0.1 m. A wall and a
building are moved by their points in the card; their callout says so.

The bin in the card's head removes the obstacle.

### Trees

A tree is a crown on a thin trunk. **Crown starts at** is the height at which
the crown begins, and **Height** is the top of the crown, both above the roof
surface. Modules keep clear of the whole crown's footprint. The shadow is that
of a thin trunk up to the crown, and of the crown above it.

### Heights to review

A drawing imported without heights has them set to 1.0 m. A note under the
**Location** card says `These obstacles have no height in the file and were
set to 1.0 m.`, with how many are `to review` and a **Review** button.
**Review** opens the first of them; its row carries an exclamation mark, labelled `Height
to review`, and its card reads "Enter the real height. The file gave none; 1.0 m
stands until you do". Type the real height, and **Next** opens the next one.

## When something goes wrong

| Message | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| **The layout was not updated**, with the reason and **Try again** | The positions on the roof are those of the design before. Correct what the reason names, or try again. |
| `The roof outline encloses no area. Give at least three corners that are not on one line.` | Edit the outline on Find. |
| `The roof outline crosses itself. Give the corners in order around the roof.` | Edit the outline on Find so that no two edges cross. |
| `This outline covers … m². SolarLayout designs one roof at a time, up to 30,000 m² (about 3 MWp). …` | Outline each building as its own design. |
| `No module fits on this roof` | Inside the setback and clear of shadows there is no room for a module. Check the outline, the **Setback** and the obstacles in this lens. |
