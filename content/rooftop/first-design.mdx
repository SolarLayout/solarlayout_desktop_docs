---
title: Your first rooftop design
description: A walkthrough from a place on the map to a design report, on a real flat roof, through Find, the Studio's four lenses, Performance and Handover.
---

This walkthrough designs a flat roof in Pune from nothing: the building on the
map, its outline and a stair room on it, then the layout, the sun, the
strings, the energy of the year and the files. It takes about twenty minutes.
The screenshots are of a real run; the figures of yours will differ with the
roof you choose.

<Steps>
<Step>

### Find the building

On Home, type the building's address into the search, or paste its
coordinates. Coordinates are read as you type and shown under the field,
marked **Coordinates**. Press **Enter**, or **Find my roof**.

<Screenshot id="rooftop-walk-home" />

Find opens on the satellite map at the place, close enough to outline the
roof, at the **Outline** step.

</Step>
<Step>

### Outline the roof

With **Corners** in hand, click each corner of the roof, then the first one
again to close the outline. **Square the corners** keeps the corners at 90°.
The tiles give the roof's area and the way it faces, and the edges' lengths
can be typed if you have measured them.

<Screenshot id="rooftop-walk-find" />

</Step>
<Step>

### Add what stands on the roof

Once the roof is closed, **Box** is in hand. Drag a box across the stair room,
then give its height above the roof surface, here 2.5 m, and **Add the box**.
Tanks, walls and buildings beside the roof are drawn the same way, each with
its height. Their shadows are counted hour by hour, and no module is placed on
them.

<Screenshot id="rooftop-walk-height" />

Leave **Roof type** at **Flat, tilted rows** for a flat roof with rows facing
the equator.

<Screenshot id="rooftop-walk-outlined" />

</Step>
<Step>

### Build the roof

**Build this roof** opens the Studio at the **Roof** lens, with the roof in
3D, the modules already on it and the figures under the view. The design is
named after the place you searched for, or "Untitled roof". From here on,
every change runs the design again.

<Screenshot id="rooftop-walk-roof" />

The Roof lens holds the roof type and the edges: the parapet, the setback
from the edge, the walkways and the clearance around obstacles.

</Step>
<Step>

### Check the array

The **Array** lens holds the module, from the app's library or a PAN file of
your own, the mounting, and the spacing. **Shade-free row pitch** and **Follow
the roof edge** are on at first: the engine spaces the rows so that they do not
shade each other in the shade-free window, and faces them square off the roof
edge closest to the equator.

<Screenshot id="rooftop-walk-array" />

</Step>
<Step>

### Read the sun

The **Sun** lens plays the design day with its shadows on the roof, and its
**Shade map** tab colours each position by the shading it gets over the year.

<Screenshot id="rooftop-walk-sun" />

</Step>
<Step>

### Size the strings

The **Strings** lens holds the system voltage, set for your country to begin
with, the inverter, the range of modules in series that fits, and the
stringing options. Each option is simulated for a full year; **A** is
**Recommended** when it meets the DC/AC target. The roof shows the strings in
colour.

<Screenshot id="rooftop-walk-strings" />

On a small roof, as here, the options can fall below the DC/AC target: the
inverter is larger than the array. Choose a smaller inverter with **Change**,
under **Inverter**, and the options are worked out again.

</Step>
<Step>

### Read the energy

**Performance** shows the energy of the chosen option over a year, hour by
hour on the weather of the site: PVGIS's typical year by default. The chain
from sunlight to the grid shows each loss, and the figures give P50, P75 and
P90 and 25 years.

<Screenshot id="rooftop-walk-performance" />

</Step>
<Step>

### Hand it over

**Handover**, or **Export** in the top bar, lists what the design hands over:
the design report, the single-line diagram, the bill of materials, the DC
cable schedule, the CAD layout, the Google Earth model, the hourly data and
the project file. Each opens in a preview; the files have their own download,
and **Download all** takes them in one zip.

<Screenshot id="rooftop-walk-handover" />

</Step>
</Steps>

Your design is kept on this device from its first edit after it opens in the
Studio: change anything, and it is kept. **Save a copy** saves its project file.
