**Handover** lists what a design hands over: "Everything the installer, the
client and the utility need". Each item opens in a preview, and each file
has its own download. **Download all** takes the files in one zip.

Open it with **Handover** in the top bar, or the tab bar on a phone, or with
**Export** in the top bar. Every file is made from the design as it is at
that moment, for the stringing option the design shows: the one you chose,
or the run's own choice until you choose. A string is a chain of modules
wired in series; an MPPT is an inverter input that tracks its own best
operating point.

## The items

| Item | Format | The line under its name |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Design report** | PDF | "A4 landscape · *n* pages" |
| **Single-line diagram** | PNG | The strings and their modules, the inverters and their MPPTs |
| **Bill of materials** | On screen | The modules, the tables and the metres of DC cable |
| **DC cable schedule** | On screen | The strings, and the highest cold Voc of any string |
| **CAD layout** | DXF | "Metres · one layer per string" |
| **Google Earth model** | KMZ | "Roof, obstacles with heights, *n* modules" |
| **Hourly data** | CSV | "*n* rows · irradiance, temperature, power" |
| **Project file** | RTD | The file's name, and "opens again here" |

Six items are files. **Bill of materials** and **DC cable schedule** are
tables on screen with no file of their own; both are printed in the design
report, on page 5, and **Copy as table** copies either one for a
spreadsheet. **Project file** stands at the end of the list as a card with
its own **Save a copy**.

Press a row to open its preview. A press never downloads. The download is
in the preview's head: **Download PDF**, **Download PNG**, **Download DXF**,
**Download KMZ**, **Download CSV**, or **Save a copy** for the project file.
On a tablet or a computer a file's row ends in a download icon and a
table's row in a chevron; on a phone every row ends in a chevron. Either way
the row opens the preview.

| Size | How the list and the preview stand |
| --- | --- |
| Computer | Side by side. **Design report** is shown at first |
| Tablet | Side by side, the list narrower |
| Phone | The list fills the screen; an item opens over it, with **Handover** at the top to go back |

## Downloading

The browser asks for the file and saves it when it arrives. A ring stands in
for the download icon while it is on its way; a second press meanwhile does
nothing. A download that arrives shows no message of its own, only the
browser's download.

Files are named after the design: its name, with spaces and characters a
file name cannot hold turned into hyphens, cut to 80 characters, then the
ending. A design with no usable name gives `rooftop-design`.

| Item | Ending |
| --- | --- |
| Design report | `-report.pdf` |
| Single-line diagram | `-single-line-diagram.png` |
| CAD layout | `-layout.dxf` |
| Google Earth model | `.kmz` |
| Hourly data | `-hourly.csv` |
| Project file | `.rtd` |
| Download all | `.zip` |

The report carries your own date, the date of the device you work on.

While the design runs again after a change, the diagram, the bill of
materials and the cable schedule stay on screen, dimmed, until the new run
arrives.

## Design report

A PDF on A4 landscape, one page for each part. A design of 28 strings or
fewer has six pages.

| Page | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| 1 · Summary | The design's name, "SolarLayout Rooftop" and the date, then the site, roof, structure, module, inverter, stringing, DC and AC capacity, weather, energy of year 1 with its P-values, specific yield with PR and capacity factor, module area and CO₂ avoided |
| 2 · Layout | "Layout and strings": the plan with the strings |
| 3 · Shadows | The plan in shadow at the end of the shade-free window on the design day, with the near, electrical and far shading of the year and the area kept clear |
| 4 · Energy | The loss diagram, the energy of each month, and the energy of years 1, 5, 10, 15, 20 and 25, the 25-year total and the P-values |
| 5 · Schedule | "String schedule and bill of materials": the first 28 strings, then the bill of materials |
| Continued | "String schedule, continued": 40 strings to a page, as many pages as needed |
| Last | The single-line diagram |

P50 and P90 are the energy of a year reached or exceeded in 50 and 90 years
out of 100; PR, the performance ratio, is the share of the energy the
modules would give at their rating that reaches the grid.
[Performance](/docs/rooftop/performance) says how each is worked out.

The preview shows the pages themselves, in a paper frame, with thumbnails
to jump between them, **Previous page** and **Next page**, and the **Left**
and **Right** keys. Its head reads, for example, "Page 1 of 6 · Summary".
The pages are made once the run has an option; after a change the old pages
stay, dimmed, until the new ones arrive.

## Single-line diagram

A PNG on A4 landscape, titled with the strings and the module: "Single-line
diagram · *n* strings of *s* × *w* Wp". It runs from the strings, through
each MPPT and inverter, to the AC distribution board (ACDB), the net meter
and the grid.

- Each inverter is a box, "INV-1" and so on, with its AC power and MPPT
  count.
- Each MPPT carries a block of its strings, "*count* × *s* mod". An MPPT
  with no strings reads "not used".

The preview draws the same diagram in the page, its blocks in the colours of
the MPPTs. Point at or tap a block for its strings and its current against
the MPPT's limit.

## Bill of materials

A table of the option's lines, as the engine lists them, with **Item**,
**Specification**, **Qty** and **Unit**. On a phone each line is a card.

| Item | Specification | Quantity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PV module | Maker, model and Wp; the bifaciality for a bifacial module | Modules used |
| String inverter | Maker, model, AC kW and MPPT count | Inverters |
| Mounting structure | The structure, as the report's summary words it | Tables: a run of modules side by side in a row; on an east-west roof, a row in use |
| DC cable | One line for each cable size the strings take, smallest first: its size and the system voltage, "6 mm² Cu, 1000 V DC, red + black" | The lengths of the strings of that size, summed, in metres |
| DC connector pair | "MC4-compatible, matched to the module" | Two for each string |
| AC cable | "Inverter to ACDB", sized for its AC current and voltage | **AC cable per inverter** × inverters |
| AC distribution board | One incomer for each inverter, one outgoing | 1 |

**AC cable per inverter**, 30 m unless you change it, is set in the Strings
lens under **Inverter limits and cabling**.

## DC cable schedule

One row for each string of the option, in the engine's order:

| Column | What it gives |
| --- | --- |
| **String** | S1, S2 … |
| **Inverter** | INV-1, INV-2 … |
| **MPPT** | The MPPT of its inverter, from 1 |
| **Modules** | Modules in series |
| **Voc cold (V)** | The string's open-circuit voltage at the lowest ambient |
| **Isc (A)** | The module's short-circuit current |
| **DC cable (m)** | The string's cable, out and back |
| **Drop (%)** | The voltage the cable drops, as a share of the string's voltage at its maximum power point at STC |
| **Cable** | The cable's size and the system voltage it is rated for |

Press a heading to sort by it; the caption says how it is sorted, and the
engine's order is the default. The last row gives the number of strings and
the total length.

Each string's cable is an estimate: twice the sum of the east-west distance,
the north-south distance from the string's start to its inverter and 2 m of
slack. On an east-west roof, or where every module stands in one line, the
string's own length is added once. Each inverter stands on the setback line,
the edge of the clear margin kept inside the roof edge, at the point nearest
the mean of its strings' starts.

Each string's cable is sized for itself: the smallest of 4, 6 and 10 mm²
copper that carries 1.25 times the module's Isc and drops no more than 1 % of
the string's voltage at its maximum power point. The current is checked
against the rating of PV cable (EN 50618) laid side by side on a surface at
60 °C: 44, 57 and 79 A. The drop takes tinned copper conductor at 70 °C. A
string that drops more than 1 % even at 10 mm² takes 10 mm², and its drop
carries an exclamation mark: a shorter run, or the inverter nearer, brings it
down. The DC ohmic loss in Performance stays the percentage set there.

## CAD layout

A DXF (R2010), in metres, in the roof's own frame: x to the east, y to the
north. For a roof outlined on the map, the origin is the middle of the
outline.

| Layer | What is on it |
| --- | --- |
| ROOF | The roof outline |
| SETBACK | The line inside the setback |
| OBSTACLES | The obstacles' footprints |
| KEEPOUT | The areas kept clear |
| MODULES_UNUSED | Positions the option does not use |
| INVERTERS | Each inverter at its size on plan, width by depth, as its OND file gives it. Where the file gives none, a 2 × 1.2 m block |
| TEXT | Each obstacle's name and height, "h=2.5", and each inverter's name; "INV-1 (symbol, not to scale)" for one drawn as a block |
| STRING_S01 and on | One layer for each string, with its modules |

The preview shows the layout page of the report and the layers.

## Google Earth model

A KMZ named after the design. The folder "Roof" holds the roof and each
obstacle with its height in its name; the folder "Modules (*n*)" holds one shape
for each module used, placed on the map at the roof's latitude and
longitude. Unused positions, the inverters, the wiring and heights as shapes
are not in the file.

A KMZ saved here can be imported again with **Import a drawing**: the roof
and the obstacles' heights are read back.

## Hourly data

A CSV with one row for each hour of the design's weather, values to three
decimals.

| Column | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `time_utc_hour_start` | The start of the hour, UTC |
| `ghi_w_m2` | Global horizontal irradiance, W/m² |
| `poa_eff_w_m2` | Effective irradiance on the plane of the modules, W/m², the mean over the strings |
| `t_cell_c` | Cell temperature, °C, the mean over the strings |
| `p_dc_kw` | DC power, kW |
| `p_ac_kw` | AC power, kW; slightly below zero at night, the inverters' night consumption |

The preview shows the first 8 rows and the columns.

## Project file

The project file (`.rtd`) carries the design: its name, roof, array, losses,
the module and inverter files themselves, the weather, the system voltage and
your choices. It holds no results; they are worked out again when it opens.
It needs nothing to be saved: no option, no run.

The preview shows its name, which you can change here, the file's name and
size, and what is in it. [Your designs and the project
file](/docs/rooftop/projects) says how it opens on another device.

## Download all

**Download all**, at the head of the list, takes the six files in one zip:
the report, the CAD layout, the Google Earth model, the hourly data, the
single-line diagram and the project file, each named as its own download.
It waits until the run has an option.

<Callout type="info">
Free, no account needed. Your designs are kept on this device. A design on
one device is not on another: the project file carries it across, and the
files you download carry it to the installer and the client.
</Callout>

## When something goes wrong

| Message | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| "The files were not updated" · "They are of the design before" | The last run failed. **Try again**. |
| "The files were not made" · "There are no files yet" | The run failed before any files. **Try again**. |
| A card with the run's reason and **Open the Strings lens** | The run has no options yet. Act on the reason, then come back. |
| "The table could not be copied. Select it and copy by hand." | The browser refused the clipboard. |

[Troubleshooting](/docs/rooftop/troubleshooting#handover) lists the rest.
