---

**Find** is where a design starts: the building on the satellite map, the
outline of its roof, and what stands on it, each with its height. It works in
three steps, shown at the top: **Find**, **Outline**, **Build**. **Build this
roof** turns the outline into a design and opens the Studio.

You reach Find from Home's search and **Find my roof**, from **New design** in
the project switcher, and from **Edit** on the roof's place in the Studio's
Roof lens, which brings the open design's outline back onto the map.

## Find the building

The panel's first step, **Find the roof**, offers three ways in.

<Screenshot id="rooftop-find-start-here" />

| Way in | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Search for the building** | The search at the top takes an address, coordinates or a maps link. The map flies to the place, close enough to outline the roof. |
| **Use my location** | The browser's location, when you are at the building. |
| **Enter dimensions by hand** | The roof by its measurements, when it is measured already. See [By hand](#by-hand). |

What the search understands:

| You type | For example |
| --- | --- |
| An address | `Koregaon Park, Pune` |
| Coordinates, latitude first | `18.53621, 73.89380` |
| Degrees, minutes and seconds | `18°32′10.4″N 73°53′37.7″E` |
| A maps link | `google.com/maps/@18.53621,73.89380`, and OpenStreetMap, Apple Maps and Bing Maps links |

An address offers up to five places, the ones near the map's centre first.
Coordinates and links are read as you type: one line under the field shows
the point, marked **Coordinates**. Coordinates never go to the address
search; when they cannot be read, the field says why, and a latitude and
longitude typed the wrong way round are offered swapped.

<Screenshot id="rooftop-find-search" />

The map is satellite imagery only. On a tablet or a computer, the buttons at
the bottom right turn it north up and zoom in and out; on a phone the map is
pinched and turned by hand.

## Outline the roof

Choose a tool in the toolbar at the bottom of the map.

| Tool | How |
| --- | --- |
| **Corners** | Click each corner of the roof. Click the first corner again, or press **Enter**, to close the outline. Three corners at least. |
| **Rectangle** | Drag a box across the roof, then turn it to the building by its round handle. It snaps to north within 3°. |
| **Detect building** | Click inside the building. Where its footprint is known, it is proposed as the outline: accept it, or drag its corners to the roof edge you see. Footprints come from Overture Maps and are approximate, about 0.6 m. |

<Screenshot id="rooftop-find-corners" />

<Screenshot id="rooftop-find-detect" />

Once the outline is closed:

- **Drag a corner** to move it. It snaps to a right angle, or parallel to an
  edge, within 3°. On a touch screen a magnifier shows above your finger.
- **Add a corner** by pressing the dot in the middle of an edge.
- **Type an edge's length** under **Edges** to set it as measured. The edge
  scales about its middle; on a rectangle, with **Square the corners** on, the
  opposite edge follows.
- **Square the corners**, on at first, snaps corners to 90° when they are
  within 3°.
- The tiles give the roof's **Area**, the way it **Faces** (outward from its
  longest edge, toward the equator), and its **Corners**.

| Key | Does |
| --- | --- |
| **Enter** | Closes the outline |
| **Esc** | Takes back the last corner |
| Arrows | Nudge the selected corner 0.1 m, 1 m with **Shift** |
| **Delete** | Removes the selected corner, keeping three at least |
| **⌘ Z** or **Ctrl Z** | Undoes; with **Shift**, redoes |

**Clear outline** in the toolbar removes the corners and the obstacles at
once, and can be undone.

## Move or turn the roof

When the outline sits off the building on the imagery, move it rather than
drawing it again. With **Box** or **Tank** in hand (**Box** is the tool you
hold once the roof closes), click inside the roof to pick it up. Drag it to
move it, with its obstacles, or nudge it with the arrows. Drag the round
handle to turn it; it snaps square to north, east, south or west within 3°.
**Esc** lets it go, and each move or turn is one step to undo.

## What stands on the roof

Once the roof is closed, the toolbar offers four obstacles. Each asks its
height above the roof surface as soon as it is drawn, because the imagery
cannot give it.

| Obstacle | How to draw it |
| --- | --- |
| **Box** | Drag corner to corner across it, then turn it by its handle: a stair room, a lift room, an AC unit |
| **Tank** | Drag from its middle to its edge |
| **Wall** | Click each point; click the last again, or press **Enter**, to end it |
| **Building** | Click the corners of a building beside the roof; click the first again to close it |

<Screenshot id="rooftop-find-height" />

The height dialog suggests a name, and **Add the box** (or the tank, the wall,
the building) waits for a height. Closing it
without adding discards the shape. The obstacles are listed under
**Obstacles** with their sizes and heights; press one to select it on the map,
drag it to move it, and turn a box or a building by its handle. **Delete**, or
the row's bin, removes it.

Trees are added in the Studio's Roof lens. Add them once the outline is
final.

<Screenshot id="rooftop-find-building" />

## The roof type

Under **Roof type**, choose **Flat, tilted rows** (the default), **Flat,
east-west** or **Sloped, flush**. A roof made sloped has no parapet and a
pitch of 10°; both can be changed in the Studio.

## Build this roof

**Build this roof** is ready once the outline is closed. A new design opens in
the Studio's Roof lens, its place the middle of the outline, its name the place
you searched for, or "Untitled roof". Built again from **Edit**, the outline,
the place, the obstacles and the roof type go into the open design, and
everything else stays as it was.

A roof covers 30,000 m² at most. For a larger site, outline each building as
its own design.

## By hand

**Enter dimensions by hand instead**, under the panel, takes what you have
measured. The roof is drawn as you type.

| Field | What to enter |
| --- | --- |
| **Latitude**, **Longitude** | The place, filled by the search, or typed: five decimals, north and east positive |
| **Shape** | **Rectangle**, or **Corners** |
| **Length**, **Width** | For a rectangle: the longer of the two is the long side |
| **Long side faces** | For a rectangle: the direction its long side looks, in degrees clockwise from north |
| Corner table | For corners: each corner in metres east and north of the middle of the roof, in order around it, three at least |

**Draw on the map instead** returns to the map.

## Import a drawing

A roof drawn already comes in from Home's **Import a drawing**, or the
project switcher: a KMZ, KML or DXF with the roof and its obstacles. The
largest closed shape is the roof. A KMZ or KML says where the roof is; for a
DXF, give its latitude and longitude, and its units, metres or millimetres.
Heights written in a shape's name or description as `h=2.5` are read; the
others are set to 1.0 m and listed for you to check in the Roof lens. The
roof opens in the Studio as a new design.

## When something goes wrong

| Message | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| "The map is not available", "This browser cannot draw the map" or "The imagery does not load" | Check the connection, try another browser, or use **Enter dimensions by hand instead**. |
| "Nothing found for …" | Try a fuller address, or paste coordinates. |
| "Your location is not available." | Allow it in the browser, or search for the address. |
| "No footprint known here" | Outline the roof by its corners or as a rectangle. |
| "The outline crosses itself." | Move the corners so that no two edges cross. |
| "This outline covers … m²." | Outline each building as its own design, up to 30,000 m². |
