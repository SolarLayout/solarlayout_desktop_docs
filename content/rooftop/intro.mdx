---
title: What SolarLayout Rooftop does
description: Rooftop PV design in the browser, in five steps from the roof on the map to the strings, the energy of a year and the files for handover.
---

SolarLayout Rooftop designs a rooftop PV system in the browser, on a phone, a
tablet or a computer. You outline the roof on the map. The app lays out the
modules, works out the shade, wires the modules into strings, and computes the
energy the design makes in a year, on one model of the roof in 3D.

Free, no account needed. Your designs are kept on this device. A design on one
device is not on another: the project file carries it across. See
[Your designs and the project file](/docs/rooftop/projects).

There is no Run button. Each change you make runs the design again: the layout,
the shade, the strings and the energy follow the roof as you change it, and the
result shows the energy of year 1.

## From a roof to a design

A design goes through five steps, in this order: **Roof**, **Array**,
**Strings**, **Performance** and **Handover**. They stand in the top bar (on a
phone, in a tab bar at the foot), each with a mark that says whether it is
done, needs you, or is still to do. See
[The five steps](/docs/rooftop/steps) for the screen itself.

### Roof

The roof's outline, its edges and what stands on it.

The Roof step starts on the map. Search an address, or paste coordinates or a
maps link, and the map moves to the building. Outline the roof by its corners,
as a rectangle, or from the building's detected outline. Add the obstacles with
their heights: a box such as a stair room, a tank, a wall, a tree, or a
building beside the roof. Choose the roof type, then press **Build this roof**.
See [Find the roof](/docs/rooftop/find).

If you have measured the roof, type its length and width, or its corners, by
hand. If you have a drawing, open it as a KMZ, KML or DXF file.

The roof then opens in 3D with the array on it. Check the outline, the roof
type and the edges: the parapet height, the edge margin (setback) kept free of
modules, the walkways and the clearance around obstacles. The roof type is one
of three:

- Flat, rows facing south (north south of the equator): tilted rows, the usual
  for a flat roof.
- Flat, rows facing east and west: low rows back to back, more modules, less
  from each.
- Sloped: the modules follow the roof pitch.

See [Roof](/docs/rooftop/roof).

### Array

The modules on the roof, how they are mounted and spaced.

Choose the module from the app's library, or read your own
module file (PAN). Set the tilt, the orientation (portrait or landscape) and
how many modules stand in each row's height. The app spaces the rows so that
they do not shade each other in the shade-free hours of the design day, and
keeps modules out of the areas the parapet and the obstacles shade in those
hours. See
[Array](/docs/rooftop/array).

On Roof, Array and Strings, the **Sun** button turns on the Sun view: the shadows at any hour,
and the shade on each module over the year. See
[The Sun view](/docs/rooftop/sun).

### Strings

How the modules are wired to the inverter.

Choose the inverter from the app's library, or read your own
inverter file (OND). The system voltage, the highest DC voltage of the system,
starts from your country: 600 V in the United States, its territories and
Canada, 1000 V elsewhere. The app works out how many modules one string can hold at that
voltage, then offers up to five options of modules per string and strings,
each simulated over a year, the best first. Choose one, or add your own option.
See [Strings](/docs/rooftop/strings).

### Performance

The energy the design makes in a year, and where it is lost.

The page leads with the energy to the grid in year 1 of the chosen option,
with the Likely (P50) and Conservative (P90) figures, the system size, the
yield in kWh/kWp and the performance ratio (PR). Under them: the energy by
month, over 25 years, where the energy goes from sunlight to the grid, and the
assumptions you can change. The weather is a typical year from PVGIS, 2023 as
measured by NASA POWER, or your own CSV file. See
[Performance](/docs/rooftop/performance).

### Handover

The files for the client, the installer and the utility.

| File | Format | For |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Design report | PDF | The client |
| Single-line diagram | PNG | The utility |
| Bill of materials | CSV | The installer |
| Cable schedule | CSV | The installer |
| CAD layout | DXF | The installer |
| Project file | RTD | You |
| Hourly data | CSV | You |
| Google Earth model | KMZ | You |

Download each file on its own, or all of them in one zip with
**Download all**. See [Handover](/docs/rooftop/handover).

On a tablet or a computer, **Present** on Performance and Handover plays the design as a tour on the whole screen, for a meeting with a client.
See [Present](/docs/rooftop/present).

## What you need

**One of these, to start a design:**

| You have | Start with | See |
| --- | --- | --- |
| An address, coordinates or a maps link | The search on Home, then the outline on the map | [Find the building](/docs/rooftop/find#find-the-building) |
| The roof's measurements | **Enter the roof by hand**: its length and width, or its corners in metres | [Enter the roof by hand](/docs/rooftop/find#enter-the-roof-by-hand) |
| A drawing of the roof | **Import a drawing**: a KMZ, KML or DXF file | [Import a drawing](/docs/rooftop/find#import-a-drawing) |
| A project file from the app | **Open a project file** (`.rtd`) | [Open a project file](/docs/rooftop/projects#open-a-project-file) |

**Optional:**

- A module file (PAN) and an inverter file (OND) of your own, when the
  equipment is not in the app's libraries. A design starts with the app's
  module and inverter. See [Equipment files](/docs/rooftop/reference#equipment-files).
- A CSV of the site's weather, when you have one. A design starts with the
  typical year from PVGIS.

**The size of the roof.** The app designs one roof at a time, up to
30,000 m² (about 3 MWp). For a larger site, outline each building as its own
design.

**A browser** that can draw in 3D and can keep data on this device, and a
connection to the internet: the app's server lays out the roof and runs the
energy. See [What the browser needs](/docs/rooftop/getting-started#what-the-browser-needs).

To look around before you start your own, open the demo: a finished design on
a 90 × 40 m roof in Pune. See [Try the demo](/docs/rooftop/getting-started#try-the-demo).
